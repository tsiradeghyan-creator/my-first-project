## What is new compared with 01–05 (Cloud, 2026-11-26)

1. **REFERENCE ROLES — READ FIRST, with declared deltas.** Each reference says what it governs: identity for the three runners; place, light, colour and grade for the prairie, which is "a location reference, not a starting frame". Where the shot must differ from a reference, it says so ("THE GRASS IS MUCH TALLER THAN IN THE REFERENCE"). This is the fix for the plate conflict in 04/05: state the difference instead of claiming a match.
2. **Negative character reference.** "<<<…>>> IS NOT IN THIS SHOT AND NEVER APPEARS" keeps the series hero from leaking into a shot where he does not belong.
3. **The camera is the prey.** The operator's behaviour is written as a character: pitching, losing them, over-correcting, burying itself in grass, catching sky, one bad stumble, horizon never level. "If the shot looks operated, it is wrong."
4. **Visible proof of speed.** It uses wakes torn through the grass ("you see his wake before you see him") and foreground stems whipping past against a slow far ground (parallax), instead of the word "fast".
5. **Staged geometry over time.** Three runners at three distances, entering at different moments so "the count keeps going up", on lines that converge on the lens.
6. **Movement quality as body mechanics.** "No self-preservation" is broken into arms, torso, head and machete, with a limit on the other side: "not monsters" (no all-fours, no spasms). The menace comes from how they move, not from effects.
7. **Sound concept: a call-and-response relay** ("you can hear the shape of the net closing"). The calls are an invented system that copies no real culture (an ethical guard), raw and unprocessed, in the dead acoustic of fog with no reverb. **The audio is a whitelist** ("ONLY THE FOLLOWING SOUNDS EXIST", numbered), not only a blacklist.
8. **A frozen lighting state.** "Identical exposure and grade from the first frame to the last; nothing emits light; eyes do not glow." This closes common AI artefacts: exposure ramps and glowing eyes.
9. **A recap block at the end** ("POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS") repeats the key locks. For a long prompt this puts the rules last, where models weigh them most.

**Weak spots to watch:**
- **Timecodes are written as seconds:frames** (0:20 = 20 frames, 3:12 = 3 s 12 frames at 24 fps). A model can read "0:20 – 2:00" as twenty seconds to two minutes. Write decimal seconds (0.0–0.8 s, 0.8–2.0 s, 2.0–3.5 s…).
- **Length.** About 16,000 characters may exceed the prompt field of many engines; anything past the limit is cut silently, and here that would be the recap block. Check the target engine's limit; if needed, keep the references, grass, camera, timing and audio blocks, and fold the recap into one short paragraph.
- **"POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS" is mostly negatives.** For video models, positive phrasing ("the horizon tilts and lurches") pulls harder than "never stabilised". Keep the negatives only for known artefacts.
- **Very heavy capitals.** For video models, the effect of capitals is unproven; for Claude-side skills we have already removed shouting. Do not copy this style into our skill text, only into target prompts if tests show it helps.
- **Deep focus while the focus "wanders" is a deliberate mix.** It is fine, but the two instructions pull against each other; keep one.

**Pattern so far (01–06):**
- the emotion and the menace live in the body and in physical evidence (steps, finger, hands, wakes);
- references have explicit roles and declared deltas, and absent characters are named as absent;
- camera behaviour is written as a person with a reason (a hunter, a getaway driver, prey);
- sound is diegetic and mapped, whitelist-first;
- known artefacts are locked by name;
- geography and continuity are locked in text;
- the series template is fixed.
