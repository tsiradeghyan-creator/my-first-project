# 01: John backs away through the fog (6 s, single close-up)

Realistic film acting at close-up scale, like a live-action drama. His fear is never acted: no wide eyes, no gasp, no open mouth, no trembling, no panic, no frowning. Only the slow, careful steps backward and the work behind his eyes.
THE EVENT (read from the ending): getting away unseen. The shot ends with John still backing away slowly, his eyes still on the fog.

MOTIVE (his fuel): he has stayed alive this long by not being seen.

OBSTACLE: the open prairie: nowhere to hide, every step in the grass can be heard, and every breath shows in the cold air; turning his back on the fog means not seeing what comes out of it.

— He checks the ground behind him, fast, and is back on the fog.

## Analysis (Cloud, 2026-11-26)

**Take these techniques:**
1. **Acting through one physical channel.** The fear lives in his feet (each step tested) and eyes (searching the fog), not on the face. This is the strongest idea here: models render visible behaviour well and "acted emotion" badly.
2. **Acting task as Goal / Obstacle / Tactic plus moment-to-moment beats** (Stanislavski, Weston). The beats give the 6 seconds a structure: search, check behind, search again.
3. **Anti-mugging line.** "His fear is never acted: no wide eyes, no gasp…" stops the stock AI "scared face".
4. **Gaze safety.** The gaze is engaged in the task, never glassy, with natural blinks. This fixes the dead-eyed stare.
5. **A role for each reference.** The environment reference supplies the look only ("do not reproduce its framing"). The prop reference comes with integrity locks (barrel straight and rigid, completely dry).
6. **Physics and continuity.** Fog drifts evenly and never swirls; breath shows as vapour but there is no frost. Each line closes a known artefact.
7. **Camera with weight.** Handheld, a beat behind, the dip of each step, "never weightless, never a game-engine look".
8. **Sound policy.** Raw production sound as recorded on set, diegetic only, with the specific sounds named.

**Leave these out or tighten them:**
1. **Length (~900 words) and repetition.** The NO BGM block appears twice, and there are many negatives. A long tail of "no X" has weak pull and can prime the very thing it forbids. One NO BGM line plus positive locks does the same job.
2. **No defined last frame.** "Ends still backing away" is a state, not a frame. Give the end composition.
3. **Labels the model cannot see** ("THE EVENT (read from the ending)", "MOTIVE (his fuel)"). Motive and the event are director's notes; their effect on the model is unproven. Keep Goal / Obstacle / Tactic and the beats, which turn into visible behaviour.
4. **Stock vs light.** Tungsten-balanced stock at dawn gives a cool cast. That fits here, but say "cool" on purpose rather than leave it to chance.
5. **Character names.** Our skills drop names from the prompt body. Here "John" is fine because his reference tag anchors him.

**What our skills already do:**

- cinema-worldbuilder has Subject Lock, Last Frame and Capture Realism.

**What our skills do not have yet:**

- prop-integrity locks.
