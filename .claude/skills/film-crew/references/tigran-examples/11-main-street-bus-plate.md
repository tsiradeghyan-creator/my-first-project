# 11: Main street with the overturned school bus, environment plate (still)

Shared by Tigran on 2026-11-26. This is the third world plate, in the same template as 09–10. The prompt is verbatim below.

```text
Cinematic film still. Empty main street of an abandoned small American town, a school bus overturned at the far intersection, late autumn day, flat overcast. No people, environment plate.

COMPOSITION: 35mm, camera at chest height standing in the middle of the roadway, looking straight down the street; the two-lane road recedes in one-point perspective with parked and abandoned cars along both kerbs; low two-storey commercial buildings line both sides; well down the street, at the cross-intersection, a yellow school bus lies on its side off the roadway, pushed well over to the left-hand side and partly screened from the camera by the abandoned cars in the mid-ground and by the corner of a building, so that only a portion of it is visible — the roadway itself stays open and passable; street furniture, signage and overhead wires close the upper part of the frame; a flat featureless grey sky; brown hills just visible beyond the intersection.

THE BUS — read as an accident, not a placement: it left the road at the intersection carrying too much speed, mounted the kerb and rolled onto its right side, sliding to a stop with its roof against the face of a corner storefront; it sits well out of the traffic lanes and clearly off-axis to the street, not squared to anything; a steel lamp post is bent flat beneath the front of it and pinned under the body, its lantern head crushed into the sidewalk; the storefront behind it has had its awning torn down and its door frame shunted out of true, though the glass alongside is still intact; the bus is seen from behind and at an angle, so the camera reads mainly its underside, chassis rails, flat tyres and the rear end, with the flank and the front largely hidden behind a parked sedan and the building corner; roughly half the vehicle is occluded.

THE EVIDENCE ON THE ROAD: a long curving double gouge scored into the asphalt, running from the middle of the roadway in the mid-ground across to the kerb and up to the bus — the mark left by the body and the wheel rims as it went over and slid; the asphalt is torn and pale along the gouge, the edges weathered and now filled with grit and sprouting a thin line of weeds; a scatter of broken safety glass along the same path, long since dulled and half buried in dust; two black tyre scuffs leading into the curve; the gouge is the clearest physical statement in the frame and it explains the bus entirely.

THE BUS CONDITION: paint still unmistakably school-bus yellow but heavily oxidised, chalked flat and dulled under a year of dust, the black lettering faded and mostly illegible from this angle; the upward-facing windows grimed opaque, one starred, one missing entirely; roof hatches sprung open; tyres flat on their rims; rust blooming along every seam, the wheel arches and the exposed chassis; dead leaves and windblown grit drifted deep into the angle where the body meets the sidewalk; weeds grown up through the gap beneath it; no burn damage, no blood, no bodies, no scattered belongings.

THE BUILDINGS: a typical small-town western main street of mixed vintage — brick and painted-render two-storey blocks with flat parapets and faded ghost signs on the side walls, a few single-storey infill shops; a hardware store, a bank with a dead ATM, a pharmacy with a cross sign, a bar with a dark neon beer sign; projecting signs on brackets, several hanging crooked; awnings sagging and torn.

THE DINER: midway down the street on the right, well before the intersection, a diner with a tall pole-mounted sign — a period roadside design with a neon tube outline and a changeable letter panel, the neon dark, the plastic faces cracked and yellowed, the sign hanging slightly out of level; the diner's plate glass intact but grimed, its interior dark with chairs upside down on the tables where they were stacked at closing and never taken down.

THE ABANDONED CARS: eight or ten vehicles along the kerbs and two stopped at angles in the roadway — mid-size sedans, a pickup, a minivan, all from before the collapse; several with doors standing wide open, one with the driver's door open and the seatbelt hanging out onto the road; flat tyres on their rims, windows filmed grey, a windscreen crazed; nothing burnt, nothing stripped — left where they stopped; two of them sit between the camera and the bus and partly hide it.

THE DECAY — fourteen months, restrained: deep drifts of dead leaves piled against every shopfront, banked along the kerbs and in the car wheel arches; weeds through the sidewalk joints and along the base of every wall; windblown paper caught in doorways; a traffic signal dark on its wire at the intersection, hanging at an angle; parking meters with fogged faces; rust streaking from brackets and downpipes; the buildings entirely intact — no fire damage, no collapse, no smashed glass beyond the bus impact, no graffiti, no bodies.

THE LIGHT: flat overcast daylight, the cloud layer acting as one enormous diffuser; no sun, no visible shadows, no directional modelling, everything evenly and softly lit; the sky a featureless pale grey, slightly brighter beyond the intersection; the shop interiors and the bus underside the darkest areas; honest and unflattering.

THE AIR: a low steady wind moving the leaves along the gutters.

THE GRADE: desaturated and cool-leaning; brick drained to grey-brown, painted render to chalk, asphalt to flat mid grey, the leaf drifts to a muted ochre-brown; the school bus yellow present and unmistakable but oxidised and dust-dulled rather than vivid, and reduced further by distance and partial occlusion — a warm note glimpsed down the street rather than a feature of the composition; low overall contrast, nothing approaching black and nothing approaching white except the sky.

PALETTE, stated first: 40% drained grey-brown and chalk — the buildings, the sidewalks, the abandoned cars; 32% flat pale grey — the overcast sky and the asphalt; 20% muted ochre and rust — the leaf drifts, the weeds, the rust streaks; 8% dulled oxidised yellow — the partly hidden bus at the intersection.

LIGHTING, palette restated: flat diffuse overcast light as the only source; no sun, no shadows, no warm accent from any light; the sky the brightest value, the bus underside and the shop interiors the darkest.

Focus: deep focus, the near asphalt and the gouge in it, the abandoned cars, the shopfronts, the diner sign and the overturned bus at the intersection all sharp and readable; the view beyond the intersection softening into haze; anamorphic elongated vertical oval bokeh only on bright glints on dirty glass.

Shot on real 35mm film, daylight-balanced 250-speed colour negative film, fine grain, natural halation; anamorphic 35mm lens, rectilinear, straight verticals; wind-moved leaves; tech tail palette: drained grey-brown 40%, flat pale grey 32%, muted ochre and rust 20%, dulled yellow 8%; gritty documentary realism, a real small town photographed on a grey day about a year after everyone left, nothing arranged for the camera; absolutely no CGI, no render look, no colour grade, no dramatic lighting, no post-apocalyptic stylisation, no burnt-out vehicles, no graffiti, no blood, no bodies, no scattered personal effects, and the bus must never sit centred or squared across the roadway. Clean environment plate, no people, no moving vehicles. No text overlay, no watermark. Aspect ratio 2.39:1.
```

## What is new compared with 05, 09 and 10 (Cloud, 2026-11-26)

1. **The hero object is read as an accident, not a placement.** The bus has a full physical backstory (too much speed, over the kerb, rolled onto its right side, slid into a storefront), plus its consequences: a lamp post pinned under it, an awning torn down, a door frame shunted. AI models default to "dramatic object placed in the middle of the road"; a causal chain beats that.
2. **Evidence that explains the object.** A curving double gouge across the asphalt, tyre scuffs into the curve, and dulled safety glass along the path. "The gouge… explains the bus entirely." The weeds in the gouge also date it.
3. **Deliberate de-emphasis of the dramatic element.**
   - The bus is about half occluded, off-axis and pushed to the side;
   - it gets only 8% of the palette, "a warm note glimpsed down the street rather than a feature";
   - "the bus must never sit centred or squared across the roadway".
   
   The prompt holds back the spectacle so the plate reads as real, not staged.
4. **A restrained decay with an elapsed time.** It is "fourteen months, restrained": leaves, weeds and rust, but no fire, no collapse, no graffiti, no burnt cars, and "no post-apocalyptic stylisation". The default AI apocalypse is banned item by item.
5. **Humane details, one per zone.** Chairs upside down on the diner tables "stacked at closing and never taken down"; a seatbelt hanging out of an open car door.
6. **World continuity.** A pharmacy with a cross sign sits on the street (the interior is plates 09–10), and the diner is set up as a possible later location. The plates add up to one town.
7. **The lettering problem handled.** The bus lettering is "faded and mostly illegible from this angle", which avoids AI gibberish by reason, not by ban.

**Weak spots to watch:**
- **"No visible shadows" under overcast light.** Real overcast still has soft contact shadows under cars, in doorways and under awnings. Removing them all is what makes images look CGI. Write "only soft contact shadows under the cars and in doorways".
- **Still-open contradictions.** "THE GRADE" against "no colour grade"; deep focus against bokeh; "tech tail palette"; the aspect ratio in the text. "PALETTE, stated first" is not actually first.
- **Signage.** Hardware store, bank, ghost signs and a changeable letter panel will produce gibberish text. Use the bus trick: "signs faded, weathered and unreadable at this distance".
- **"Fourteen months" and "about a year"** both appear. Keep one figure for the series bible.

**Pattern so far (01–11):** everything from 01–10. Plates now also use:
- causal backstory for any dramatic object, plus physical evidence that dates it;
- de-emphasis of the spectacle (occlusion, off-axis, a small share of the palette);
- restrained decay with a stated elapsed time, with clichés banned item by item;
- one humane detail per zone;
- plates that connect into one world.
