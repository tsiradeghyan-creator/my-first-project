## What is new (Cloud, 2026-11-26)

1. **One character, two worlds, two references.** Western Mira is reference 1 and cyberpunk Mira is reference 2, plus a CONTINUITY line mapping each to its segment. The identity and costume per world are kept apart.
2. **Violence suggested, not shown.** One gunshot heard, the firing and the impact "outside the camera's clear view", her weapon partly hidden by her own shoulder, the fall half hidden by dust, "no visible wound or blood". The outcome is told by editing, sound, silhouettes and reactions. It is good craft, and it stays inside moderation.
3. **Accelerating editing with sub-second inserts.** Boots for 0.7 s, fingers for 0.7 s, eyes for 0.6 s, then the draw. "Editing accelerates during the duel" is written as actual timecodes, like a Leone cutting pattern.
4. **Comedy from absolute seriousness.** "The humor comes from how seriously she performs every western cliché." Delivery is specified to the word ("heavy emphasis on ONE SHERIFF, lower on *this town*"), with an accent ("natural American female accent") and locked eye contact.
5. **A practical foreground wipe as the genre transition.** A wagon crosses the lens left to right to cover a world change, then "establish the environment for TWO SECONDS before Mira enters".
6. **A visual rhyme between the worlds.** Shot 1 and Shot 7 both open on 28 mm, 15 cm above the ground, tracking forward, first dust, then wet asphalt. The bookends make the switch feel designed.
7. **A rear three-quarter motorcycle tracking shot.** "Keep Mira primarily BACK-FACING, motorcycle silhouette clearly readable": it protects identity by not demanding a face at speed.

**Weak spots to watch:**
- **27 s, about 10 cuts (shot 5 alone has 4) and two worlds in one generation** is beyond what one clip holds reliably. Sub-second inserts tend to merge or get skipped. Split at the wipe (Western 0–20 s, cyberpunk 20–27 s) and match the wipe in the edit.
- **"MATCH CUT" after a wipe is a label clash.** A wipe and a match cut are different transitions. If both are generated in one clip, the model may blend the two worlds (a morph) instead of wiping. Generate the wipe's end frame and the city's first frame separately.
- **Two Mira face references at once** (western and cyberpunk) can mix outfits across segments. That is another reason to split at the wipe.
- **A person shot dead**, even off-screen, may still trigger moderation. The hidden-impact staging is the best mitigation; keep "no blood".
- **"FORMAT: 7 shots"** doesn't match the real cut count. State the real cuts.

**Pattern for the Mira school (19–23):**
- comedy from incongruity (scale, seriousness, genre);
- dialogue timing written out (finishes, silences, mid-line interruptions);
- costume overrides on identity references;
- image-level effects kept apart from body morphing;
- violence suggested off-screen;
- named camera packages and mm:ss timing.

It is more ambitious per generation than the John school and less physical in detail.
