## What is new (Cloud, 2026-11-26)

1. **Behaviour transfer as the gag.** A monster performs a puppy's behaviour list:
   - the head perks up;
   - a play bow (shoulders down, hindquarters up);
   - the tail wags;
   - a snap at the tail that misses;
   - one full circle;
   - a dizzy stop.
   
   Each item is a known, readable animal behaviour, so the model has concrete targets, and the comedy is the mismatch of scale and behaviour.
2. **Scale shown through consequences.** Footfalls shake the courtyard, dust and pebbles fly outward, the tail just misses the stone walls, and the folded wings bounce with the movement.
3. **Anatomy guards for a complex body in motion.** "Maintain consistent anatomy, tail attachment and body proportions"; "no transformation, no cartoon anatomy". A spinning creature is where AI loses limbs and tails, and the lock names the exact risk (the tail's attachment).
4. **A reaction shot with a placed eyeline.** Mira watches "slightly below and to one side of the camera". She is filmed from slightly above, so her look down toward the dragon reads as a real eyeline. The reaction moves from surprise to affectionate laughter ("no exaggerated theatrical laughter").
5. **A COMEDIC RHYTHM block** with the beats per shot and their seconds. The timing of the joke is written down.

**Weak spots to watch:**
- **One full rotation of a colossal dragon in under 6 s, kept in frame on a 28 mm lens.** It is very hard to keep the anatomy stable. Half a turn, or a single snap and a stumble, is far more likely to survive.
- **Mira has a different identity reference here** (`3259a8c6…`, the medieval costume), against `ca8ce164…` in 19–20 and 22 and two more in 23. Several face references for one character invite identity drift. Keep one face reference and use the others for costume only.
