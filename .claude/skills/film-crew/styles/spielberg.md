# Steven Spielberg — style card

**Signature in one line:** fluid long takes that block characters in depth so cuts aren't needed, and slow
push-ins on faces looking up at something wonderful or terrible.
**ASL:** medium, with many hidden "oners" · **Collaborators:** DP Janusz Kamiński; editor Michael Kahn; composer
John Williams (context only).

## Composition & framing
- Deep staging: foreground, middle and background actors all doing something; reflections and silhouettes;
  characters looking off-screen before the reveal.

## Lenses & format
- Wide-to-normal lenses for staging in depth; backlit atmosphere.

## Camera movement
- The "oner": dolly and pan re-frame from wide to two-shot to close-up as actors move, replacing coverage;
  push-in on a face for awe; dolly zoom for shock (Jaws).

## Light & colour
- Strong backlight, shafts of light through smoke/windows, lens flares; warm family interiors; high contrast
  in danger.

## Production design & costume
- Lived-in everyday spaces (suburban kitchens, cars) against extraordinary events.

## Editing
- Classical, invisible; withholds the monster/the wonder; reaction before reveal.

## Sound & music
- Big thematic score, but also long scored silences before payoffs.

## Performance
- Natural, overlapping family talk; children's points of view.

## Use sparingly / avoid
- Too much flare and push-in = parody. Earn the reveal with withholding.

## AI translation
- "slow push-in on his face as he looks up off-screen in awe, backlit by shafts of light through haze"
- "one continuous shot: the camera dollies and pans as the family moves through the kitchen, re-framing from wide
  to a close two-shot without a cut"
- "dolly zoom: camera dollies back while zooming in; his face stays the same size as the background stretches"

## Example shot
MCU, low angle, slow push-in: a boy looks up past the camera; warm light grows on his face and his mouth opens;
we do not see what he sees; cut later to the reveal; 5 s.
