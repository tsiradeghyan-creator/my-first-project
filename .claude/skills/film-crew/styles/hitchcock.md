# Alfred Hitchcock — style card

**Signature in one line:** suspense engineered by showing the audience more than the character knows, through
point-of-view cutting and precisely storyboarded shots.
**ASL:** medium; montage bursts for shock · **Collaborators:** composer Bernard Herrmann; designer Saul Bass for
titles (context only).

## Composition & framing
- The POV triad: character looks → what they see (their POV) → their reaction (Kuleshov effect); high angles at
  moments of fate; objects of suspense placed clearly in frame (the key, the glass, the bomb).

## Lenses & format
- Classical studio framing; deliberate use of tight close-ups on objects.

## Camera movement
- Long crane move from a wide establishing view down to a tiny detail (a key in a hand); dolly zoom for vertigo;
  slow approach to a door that should not be opened.

## Light & colour
- Classical studio lighting; strong shadows in suspense; colour as code in his colour films (green, red) (verify).

## Production design & costume
- Ordinary places turned threatening (motels, staircases, windows into neighbours' flats); cool, elegant costume.

## Editing
- Suspense over surprise: show the danger, then make the audience wait; rapid montage of many short shots for
  shock; cross-cutting.

## Sound & music
- Strings stabs and ostinatos for dread; silence before the shock.

## Performance
- Composed surfaces with anxiety under them; the "wrong man".

## Use sparingly / avoid
- The dolly zoom is now a cliché; use the POV/suspense logic more than the gimmicks.

## AI translation
- "close-up: she looks off-screen right; cut to her point of view — the door handle slowly turning; cut back to
  her face as her breathing stops"
- "slow crane down from a high wide view of the crowded room to an extreme close-up of the key in her hand"
- "high angle looking straight down the staircase; the figure is small at the bottom"

smiling; nobody in the room knows; 3 shots, 8 s total.
