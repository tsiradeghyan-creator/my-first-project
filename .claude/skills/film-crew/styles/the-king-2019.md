# The King (2019, David Michôd) — style card

**Status:** researched-unreviewed · **Built:** 2026-11-03 · **Covers:** film (director David Michôd, DP Adam Arkapaw, editor Peter Sciberras, composer Nicholas Britell)
**Based on:** The King (2019) only, with emphasis on the Agincourt sequence, the marches and camps, and the
candle/firelit council interiors.
**Signature in one line:** a still, silent young king held in wide, sombre, large-format frames of grey sky and
wet earth, until the battle drops the camera to eye level into a tight, claustrophobic crush of mud and armour.
**Average shot length (ASL):** not measured; deliberate and slow in the court/camp scenes, faster and fragmented in
the melee (verify) · **Key collaborators:** DP Adam Arkapaw [1][7], editor Peter Sciberras (his third Michôd
feature) [9], composer Nicholas Britell [3], production designer Fiona Crombie [5], costume designer Jane Petrie [6]
(context only).

## Composition & framing
- Battle: camera kept "for the most part" at human eye level, tight and claustrophobic, so the fight feels real
  rather than choreographed spectacle [2]. Bodies and shields fill the frame edge to edge; horizon often hidden.
- Battle geography is deliberately sacrificed for closeness; critics complained you cannot always tell what is
  happening [8]. Use that as a choice: disorientation in the melee, clarity only in the wides before and after.
- Outside the melee: wide, painterly compositions; reviewers compare the frames to epic oil paintings [8]. Small
  figures in large wet landscapes, armies as dark masses against pale sky (verify).
- The king is framed still and centred or near-centred in medium close-ups, often silent while others talk
  around him (verify).
- One high, top-down angle on the piled, crushed bodies in the mud is part of the battle's vocabulary [10]; it is
  also the film's most-cited "borrowed" image, see Avoid.

## Lenses & format
- Shot on ARRI Alexa 65, ArriRAW Open Gate [1]: large format, shallow depth at normal distances, very smooth
  tonal roll-off in grey skies and skin.
- Lens set not documented in any source found; Prime DNA or other 65mm-coverage glass is plausible but
  unconfirmed (verify). Do not prompt a specific lens brand; prompt the look instead.
- Practical working range for the AI crew (craft suggestion, not a film fact): wide 28-35mm (65mm-format
  equivalent wide) for landscapes and marches; 50-80mm for the king's close-ups; wide-ish lens close to bodies in
  the crush (verify).

## Camera movement (signature moves → camera-movement-lexicon.md)
- Natural-light tracking shot following the prince through his followers in a forest is singled out in the DP's
  profile [7]: slow, steady, travelling with the character, not ahead of him.
- Battle: eye-level, inside the press of bodies [2]; operated (handheld or shoulder) jostling with the crowd,
  stumbling with the mud (verify for exact rig).
- Court, camp and council: locked-off or very slow pushes; the camera waits (verify).
- Rule of contrast: stillness everywhere except where violence is happening.

## Light & colour
- Exteriors: overcast, flat, soft daylight; low-contrast grey-green-brown; no hard sun (verify, consistent with
  the "natural light" description [7]).
- Interiors: natural window light by day and candlelit night interiors in deep chiaroscuro, compared by one
  reviewer to Caravaggio [11]. Faces lit from one warm low source, falling to black.
- Grade: desaturated, sombre; mud browns, steel greys, skin pale; warmth only from flame (verify).
- Interiors shot in real historic houses (Haddon Hall, Berkeley Castle/Hall) [5], so windows and stone motivate
  the light.

## Production design & costume
- Brief: a world "raw, dirty" and historically authentic but slightly otherworldly [5].
- Recurring diamond and graphic patterning across design and costume [5]; criss-cross motifs [6].
- Battle camps of painted linen tents [5]: muted, weathered textiles, not bright heraldry.
- Armour is real-looking plate with restrained decoration (patterns etched and filled with gold paint) [6];
  the king's costume evolves in three phases as he changes [6].
- The mud was made on set by hosing the ground and running horses over it [12]; the French die crushed and
  drowned in it [2]. Mud is a character, not a texture.

## Editing (rhythm, transitions, tricks, structure)
- Long, quiet dialogue scenes, slow overall pacing; critics found the first two hours morose and slow [8], so
  the battle lands as a release.
- Battle cut from long, quiet anticipation (lines facing each other, waiting) into chaotic fragments (verify).
- Straight cuts; no flashy transitions or speed ramps (verify).
- Sciberras's AACTA nomination for editing [9] — rhythm is restrained, not montage-driven.

## Sound & music
- Score: "most sombre" of Britell's career; low-register strings, brass, an old analogue synth, prepared piano,
  digitally manipulated percussion, and the Trinity Boys Choir [3].
- Choir is used wordless [4]; the brief was rich and classic but "unusual, otherworldly" [3].
- A dedicated march cue scores the advance to Agincourt ("Marche - Agincourt") [4]: the music builds before
  the battle more than during it (verify).
- In the melee: breath, clanking metal, sucking mud, screams; music recedes (verify).

## Performance
- The king is still, quiet, watchful; reviewers note one held expression for most of the film [8]. Power is
  shown by not reacting.
- Eruptions are rare and short; speeches are low and plain rather than rousing (verify).
- Extras in the battle wear helmets they can barely see through, on slippery mud [2]: real struggle, not
  stage-fighting.

## Use sparingly / avoid (clichés that turn homage into parody)
- The top-down shot of a hero buried in a pile of bodies is already a recognised quotation of a TV battle [10];
  do not make it the centrepiece.
- No slow-motion hero charges, no epic choir swell under every shot, no orange-teal grade, no lens flares.
- Do not over-saturate fire; flame is the only warmth, keep it small.
- Do not stage a big rousing speech; the stillness is the point.

## Example shot in this lens
MCU, ~75mm (large-format), locked-off: the young king on horseback in flat grey light, still and silent, wet hair,
dull mail; behind him out of focus, a column retreating through smoke; only his eyes move once; sound is wind,
hooves in mud and a single low string note; 6 s.
