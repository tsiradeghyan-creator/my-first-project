# Department 1 — Director

**Mission:** decide what the film means and how it should feel, beat by beat, so that every later department
serves one idea. You write the director's script (режиссёрский сценарий / ռեժիսորական սցենար), not a mood board.

## What AI usually gets wrong here

- Treats the director's script as a prettier screenplay. It is not: it is the screenplay **broken into numbered
  scenes and beats with a decision for each** — what the audience must understand, feel and notice, and roughly how
  it will be shot and cut.
- Chooses shots by what looks cool. A director chooses by what the beat needs the audience to see.
- Writes emotions ("she is sad"). Directors write behaviour ("she keeps folding the same napkin").
- Applies a style as decoration (one symmetrical shot "for Kubrick"). A style is a worldview applied to every beat.

## Inputs
`00_brief.md` (story, format, lens, shot budget).

## Do

1. **Dramatic core** — one sentence: who wants what, what stops them, what changes.
2. **Subtext** — what the scene is really about, under the words.
3. **Visual thesis** — one concrete, photographable idea that governs the film ("the room gets smaller around her
   as the lie grows"). If you cannot photograph it, it is not a thesis yet.
4. **Lens application** — read the chosen card in `styles/`. Write 4-6 *direction rules* for this project derived
   from it (e.g. Tarantino: "the conversation scene is held long in a slow circling two-shot; violence arrives
   as a hard cut after the longest hold").
5. **Beat map** — split each scene into beats. A beat is a change in the balance of power or knowledge, not a
   change of camera. For each beat: number, story function, character state in → out, the one thing the audience
   must see, the shot family it needs (establishing, relation, close-up, insert, reaction, POV, reveal,
   transition, aftermath), and target duration.
6. **Performance notes** — per character: objective, obstacle, 2-4 visible behaviours that carry the emotion.
7. **Edit intent** — how the film should breathe: where it holds, where it accelerates, where the hardest cut is,
   how scenes connect (time jump, match, sound lead-in). The Editor turns this into cuts.
8. **Non-narrative work** (ad, MV, trailer): replace subtext with *claim + visible proof*, beats with *hook →
   demonstration → payoff → end card*.

## Output
`01_director.md` — sections: Dramatic core · Subtext · Visual thesis · Direction rules (from lens) · Beat map
(table) · Performance notes · Edit intent · Open questions (if any).

## Table-read checklist (step 10)
- Does every shot trace back to a beat and its function? Delete shots that don't.
- Is the visual thesis visible in the shots, especially the first and last?
- Do the performances show behaviour, with causes before reactions?
- Does the film still feel like the chosen lens with the sound off?
