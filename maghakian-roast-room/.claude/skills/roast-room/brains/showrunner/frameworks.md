# Frameworks: Showrunner

Composite archetype built from writers' room and showrunning practice. Scoring lines are guidance.

## SHO-F1 Engine test
What: list 20 episode ideas in an hour, name 3 recurring conflicts and 1 central question. Fewer than 12 strong ideas means a weak engine.
Score: 20 ideas and clear conflicts = 8+. Under 12 = 4 or lower.

## SHO-F2 Room capacity math
What: writers times draft pages per writer per week, minus rewrites and notes, against episodes per week required. Add a 20% buffer.
Use: any volume commitment (dailies, long seasons) or taking a second project in parallel.
Score: capacity below demand plus buffer = ruin risk to the deadline and the reputation.

## SHO-F3 Format grammar
What: each format has rules. Daily drama: short scenes, many storylines, a hook at every break. Weekly sitcom: A and B story, reset at the end. Streaming: serial arcs, cliffhanger endings. Film: three acts, one transformation. Kids: short, visual, one problem the child solves.

## SHO-F4 Bible minimum
What: before a buyer meeting: logline, one-page synopsis, main characters with wants and flaws, the engine, season one arc, 5 to 10 episode loglines, the pilot script, tone references.

## SHO-F5 Season arc and story of the week
What: decide the mix of serial arcs and stand-alone episodes. More episodic = easier to sell in volume and rerun; more serial = stronger streaming hook.

## SHO-F6 Note triage
What: sort notes into story problems, clarity problems and taste. Fix the first two at root. Discuss the third.

## SHO-F7 Writer ladder
What: grow writers from scene writers to episode writers to story editors so the showrunner is not the bottleneck.

## SHO-F8 Pilot as proof
What: one pilot script (or animatic for kids) proves tone and engine better than any deck.
