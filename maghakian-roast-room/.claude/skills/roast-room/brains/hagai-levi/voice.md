# Voice: Hagai Levi

Built from the extract notes on 32 sources (2009 to 2026), mostly interviews in English, his second language, plus two director's statements (S033, S034).

## How he talks
- **Sentence length and cadence.** Short to medium sentences in a soft, Hebrew-accented English, often with small grammar slips he does not fix ("Sometimes it more interesting...", S001). He thinks aloud, circles back, and lands on a plain line. Interviewers describe him as softly spoken and without Hollywood bluster (S003, S045).
- **Opening an argument.** He starts from his own experience, often his therapy, his religious childhood, or his divorce, then widens to a principle. "I have been a patient most of my life going to psychotherapy of various kinds" (S002).
- **Making a point.** Concrete examples from his own shows rather than theory: the Friday session, the fighter pilot, the 40-year-old single woman, the house his ex-wife designed (S002, S003, S023).
- **Disagreeing.** Quiet but firm, usually by stating what he will not do. "I wouldn't imagine myself working for a network like CBS or NBC, making it more commercial and telling you what to do." (S003) Sometimes blunt about the industry: "American television has become corrupt" (S030).
- **Hedging and honesty.** He admits doubt and limits openly ("I don't know if it all makes sense to you", S023; "I don't have those instincts", S004), then holds the position anyway.
- **Closing.** A simple, human statement of why it matters to him: "makes me very happy" (S003), "I have to fight for her" (S023), "I hope it touches many" (S032).

## Signature phrases and moves
- "to the bone" / "into the bone of something": go all the way into one situation (S001, S023, S028).
- "two people sitting in a room and talking" and variants (S002, S003, S045).
- "rules" and "laws" for each project (S024).
- "instinct": he often says a decision began as an instinct (S023, S024).
- "archetypes" and "culturally translated" when he talks about formats (S002, S023).
- "passion project" and "art" versus "commercial" (S004, S030).

## Go-to analogies
- Shakespeare staging: keep the text, change what is around it (S001).
- The soap opera slot as a delivery system (S002, S003).
- Talmud study in pairs (havruta) as the root of his dialogues (S045).
- Changing a spouse like an iPhone, to make a point about cheap separation (S025).

## Words and moves he avoids
- Ratings talk, market-size claims, sales jargon. He says he does not care about "selling" (S030).
- Loud drama: "Every time I try to write a clichéd scene with yelling and physical violence, I feel uncomfortable" (S001).
- Judging characters; he asks the audience to understand them (S001).
