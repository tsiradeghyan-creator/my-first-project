# Stories: Hagai Levi

Built from the extract notes on 32 sources (2009 to 2026). 14 stories he reuses, or that the record tells about his work, each with the lesson for a small studio selling abroad.

1. **Two years at home, days abroad.** BeTipul took two pilots and about two years to get commissioned by HOT; days after it aired, calls came from all over the world and three US networks fought for it. Lesson: the home market's doubt says little about foreign demand. (S002, S003)

2. **A disc in Los Angeles.** An Israeli actress trying her luck in Hollywood asked Levi for a subtitled disc and passed it to her manager, then to Ari Emanuel, then to HBO's head of entertainment. Lesson: informal networks open the first door; the product must still be unique. (S040)

3. **The soap that taught the format.** He made 270 episodes of the daily drama Love Around the Corner, hated the genre, and kept its one strength: a daily slot that lives in people's homes. Lesson: steal the distribution power of a popular form. (S002, S003, S005)

4. **The Friday session.** While planning BeTipul he realized the therapist would see his own therapist on Friday, and knew he had something. Lesson: one structural device makes a format. (S003)

5. **The 40-year-old single woman in New York.** In Israel her pain was no children; in New York writers said most women there are unmarried at that age, so the problem became a lack of love. Lesson: translate the problem, not the plot. (S002)

6. **The fighter pilot.** The Israeli air force pilot was "second only to God" at home and read wrong in the US; later versions made him an elite policeman (Argentina, Brazil) or an undercover cop in the Mafia (Italy), on his advice. Lesson: national institutions must be swapped. (S003, S008, S040)

7. **No helicopters.** Everyone told him HBO would open the show up and fly helicopters in; HBO left it in the room, partly because it needed it on air fast. Lesson: the right buyer protects the form; speed can be leverage. (S003, S004)

8. **The Dutch version by the Big Brother company.** He disliked a version made by a reality producer and still felt responsible for it. Lesson: choose the local producer, not only the price. (S040)

9. **Leaving The Affair.** It started as art; at a moment he saw it moving away and handed it to his partner Sarah Treem, then went home to make The Accursed. Lesson: leave when you cannot answer why you are doing it. (S004, S023)

10. **Daniel Bergman's email.** Bergman's son wrote to him because BeTipul and In Treatment owed so much to Scenes from a Marriage. The project sat seven years until the gender swap gave him the reason. Lesson: an influence made visible becomes an asset; a remake needs its why. (S023, S024, S027, S028)

11. **Not American.** Before shooting in the US he felt "this is not my language" and showed the set and the masked crew on screen to turn the couple into a fable. Lesson: a foreign creator can turn the gap into form. (S023, S025)

12. **Our Boys on HBO in Hebrew and Arabic.** HBO asked him in 2015 to turn a news story into a series; with Keshet and two co-creators he made the first HBO original in Hebrew and Arabic, and was attacked at home by the prime minister. Lesson: a local trauma can travel on a premium platform when it asks a universal question. (S014, S019, S020)

13. **The kibbutz projectionist.** As a teenager he censored films for a religious kibbutz, blocking scenes with paper and showing mostly Westerns, which taught him how films are built. Lesson (his reading): rules first, then freedom inside them. (S004, S024, S027, S045)

14. **Etty, ten years in the making.** A therapist gave him Hillesum's diaries after In Treatment; he pitched every time by saying it is not a Holocaust series, and it was financed by European money, with ARTE, and premiered in Venice. Lesson: an old subject sells again only in a new language. (S030, S033, S034)
