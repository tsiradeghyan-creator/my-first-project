# Positions: Hagai Levi

Built from the extract notes on 32 sources (2009 to 2026). 26 topics. Topics that decide whether a story travels from a small market come first, then craft and business topics a studio brings to this seat, then common founder topics. Each stance carries a verbatim quote, source id and date. Where his view moved, both versions are shown and the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say "no recorded position".

## Format sale versus finished show versus remake (6 sources)
- **Stance:** For BeTipul the format sale was the business: buyers wanted to remake it in their own language with their own stars, and the money came from abroad. He was an executive producer or consultant on most versions. He does not describe a rule for choosing between selling a format and selling finished episodes; the evidence is his one big case.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Days after the show went on air in Israel I got a lot of calls from all over the world, saying 'We would like to remake it here.'" (S002, 2009-10)
  - "It took me about two years to sell it in Israel, though HBO bought the American remake rights immediately." (S003, 2009-10-10)
  - "If it wasn't for the overseas business, I would have lost money." (S008, 2009-01)
  - The HBO deal "turned it into a coveted global commodity" (study, S040, 2016); Levi executive producer and consultant on most adaptations (S027, 2021-09-09; S038).
- **Note:** For Our Boys and Etty, the route was a co-production with a buyer attached from the start (HBO with Keshet; ARTE with European producers), not a format (S014, S034).

## What makes a story travel (6 sources)
- **Stance:** Archetypes, a bare concept and one strong device travel; local institutions and details do not. Yet the power of TV is local specificity, so the local layer must be rebuilt in each country.
- **Evidence:**
  - "it was more like archetypes of characters that I wanted to discuss. The couple, a girl coming of age, these very, very basic archetypes." (S023, 2021-10-03)
  - "It's a very conceptual show. It's not very specific." (S023, 2021-10-03)
  - "I think this is the power of television, that it says a very particular, specific story which is totally rooted in a place and in a culture." (S023, 2021-10-03)
  - "We tried to understand hate crime. That's relevant to America, to Europe, to everywhere." (S019, 2019-08-07)

## Local details that do not travel (4 sources)
- **Stance:** Plotlines tied to a national institution (the Israeli fighter pilot) or a national norm (a single woman at 40) must be rewritten for each market.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Some of the plotlines, such as the fighter pilot, are too Israeli." (S008, 2009-01)
  - "In Israel being a pilot is second only to God" (S003, 2009-10-10)
  - "Now in Israel if you are single at the age of 40 you might as well be dead... having kids is so important." (S002, 2009-10)
  - Later versions in Argentina, Brazil and Italy changed the pilot "on the advice of the format creator" (S040, 2016).

## How close a remake should stay (5 sources)
- **Stance:** Changed over time. In 2008 the US season one stayed almost word for word; by 2009 he thought that was too close. For his own Bergman remake (2021), keep structure and episode spine, change the world and the meaning.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2009 (older): "The first season is very close to the original, almost word for word in the first few weeks." (S003, 2009-10-10)
  - 2009: "After the first season we thought it was too close, and that the adaptations should be culturally translated." (S002, 2009-10)
  - 2021 (newer): "In every episode, the story always followed the original, changing everything, but keeping the structure and the story of every episode." (S024, 2021-09-26)
  - 2022: "It's like Shakespearean theater. You can keep the text, but can change things around the text." (S001, 2022-01)

## Working with the foreign buyer's notes (4 sources)
- **Stance:** Expect "open it up" pressure and resist it. HBO left the format intact; a commercial network would not have.
- **Evidence:**
  - "There was no pressure at all from HBO, perhaps helped by the fact they needed it on the air very quickly." (S003, 2009-10-10)
  - "That they would cut it, bring helicopters into the sky, and no, they left it the way it is." (S004, 2014-12-26)
  - "I wouldn't imagine myself working for a network like CBS or NBC, making it more commercial and telling you what to do." (S003, 2009-10-10)

## Choosing the platform (4 sources)
- **Stance:** Premium cable and public European broadcasters (HBO, ARTE) suit him; broad commercial US networks do not. The US is so big that a small loyal audience can sustain a show.
- **Evidence:**
  - "I did this one with HBO, which I feel so at home with and [with whom] I have the most creative freedom." (S025, 2021-09-06)
  - "that's what's fun about the US, that it's so big that even a small amount of viewers is enough for three seasons" (S004, 2014-12-26)
  - Etty: European co-producers and ARTE; "it's only European money" (S030, 2026-03-23).

## Budget (4 sources)
- **Stance:** Design cheap; it eases the first sale. Writing time is the real cost.
- **Evidence:**
  - "My intention was always to make it on the cheap...two actors, one location." (S008, 2009-01)
  - "In fact, I needed an entire month to write each episode." (S008, 2009-01)
  - "Except for Serbia, those countries have much higher budgets than Israel" (S008, 2009-01)

## Pilots and proof of concept (2 sources)
- **Stance:** When buyers think a concept is too highbrow for a slot, shoot pilots.
- **Evidence:** "I produced two pilots and I think when people watched it they were convinced that it could work" (S002, 2009-10)

## The creator's role on foreign versions (5 sources)
- **Stance:** Stay involved: consultant, executive producer, sometimes living abroad to help direct. Care about quality even after the sale.
- **Evidence:**
  - "I am executive producer, which was more like a consultant in the first series; I was more involved in the second series." (S002, 2009-10)
  - "Actually I was living in New York for six months... helping direct." (S002, 2009-10)
  - "While it's true that we were the ones who sold them the format, I do still care." (S040, 2016)

## Local writer partners (4 sources)
- **Stance:** Always pair with a local writer abroad.
- **Evidence:** "I knew from the beginning that if I'm doing a show in America or in any other culture I need to collaborate with a local writer" (S027, 2021-09-09); "That was for me, like, a safety net." (S023, 2021-10-03)

## Casting for export (3 sources)
- **Stance:** A two-hander needs stars who can hold a close-up; each country casts its own prestige actors.
- **Evidence:** "To hold the viewers' attention for half an hour you need to have that star quality, be able to look at a close-up and be fascinated all the time." (S003, 2009-10-10). The study calls the format an "actor's paradise" that drew top local actors (S040, 2016).

## Remakes of classics (3 sources)
- **Stance:** Only with a reason that makes the remake definitive for today.
- **Evidence:** "So I felt that if you do it today, you have to make it definitive again." (S028, 2021-09); "I had to have an answer for why I should do it and why I was the one to do it." (S024, 2021-09-26)

## Number of seasons and episode count (3 sources)
- **Stance:** Shorter is better; seasons are commercial.
- **Evidence:** 2009: tired of repeating, he did not make a third Israeli season (S002). 2021 (newer): "I'm starting to feel that five season is never justified." (S024, 2021-09-26)

## Commercial versus art (4 sources)
- **Stance:** He does not do commercial TV and admits he lacks commercial instinct; every project is a passion project.
- **Evidence:**
  - "I know what is good and what is not good, not what will bring (in viewers) and what won't." (S004, 2014-12-26)
  - "I don't do commercial television. Every project I work on is a passion project." (S030, 2026-03-23)
  - "I don't really care about 'selling'. That's not my thing." (S030, 2026-03-23)

## Audience size (2 sources)
- **Stance:** A small engaged audience is enough.
- **Evidence:** "I don't have the ambition to reach a lot of people." (S004, 2014-12-26)

## The state of American television (2 sources)
- **Stance:** 2009: admired HBO for taking television to its limits (S002). 2026 (newer): the golden age is over and US TV has become corrupt.
- **Evidence:** "HBO make amazing shows and they took television to its limits." (S002, 2009-10); "The Golden Age of Television is over." (S030, 2026-03-23)

## Television versus film (4 sources)
- **Stance:** TV is a writers' medium for exploring a concept; film tells a story. He began in TV as practice for film and stayed.
- **Evidence:** "For me, television is more like exploring a world or a concept. Cinema is more about telling a story." (S028, 2021-09); "I feel very at home in television." (S024, 2021-09-26); Etty "is a film split into six episodes" (S030, 2026-03-23).

## True stories and national conflict (5 sources)
- **Stance:** Stay close to the facts, do not chase balance, do not entertain with pain, expect attacks.
- **Evidence:** "It wasn't about being balanced, it was about being true to what happened" (S019, 2019-08-07); "We embraced the criticism" (S014, 2019-11-23)

## Period and historical stories (4 sources)
- **Stance:** Do not repeat the old way; make history speak to now.
- **Evidence:** "If you keep telling WWII and Holocaust stories in the old-fashioned way, they become anecdotes from the past." (S030, 2026-03-23); "I wanted it to be modern, but not contemporary" (S041, 2025-09-17)

## Timing and luck in a sale (2 sources)
- **Stance:** BeTipul arrived when US networks were looking abroad; he does not assume it repeats.
- **Evidence:** "I showed up at a unique moment when the American networks were looking for ideas from abroad." (S008, 2009-01)

## Cultural boycott and financing from Israel (2 sources)
- **Stance:** Boycotts can be just, but a blanket cultural boycott punishes artists who oppose the government; buyers should check who and what money they work with.
- **Evidence:** "But the problem with a cultural boycott is that you punish the wrong people." (S030, 2026-03-23)

## Pitching (2 sources)
- **Stance:** State plainly what the project is not, so the buyer does not file it in the wrong box.
- **Evidence:** "In every pitch, I started by saying: 'This is not a Holocaust movie. This is not a Holocaust series'." (S030, 2026-03-23)

## Co-creation and partners (3 sources)
- **Stance:** Collaborate with strong partners, including from the other side of a conflict; hand over to a partner rather than run a show you no longer believe in.
- **Evidence:** "with "The Affair" I had an American partner, Sarah Treem. At a certain point, she took over." (S023, 2021-10-03)

## Leaving a successful show (2 sources)
- **Stance:** Leave when the answer to "why am I doing this?" is foggy.
- **Evidence:** "(The Affair) started out as art, and there was a specific moment when I started to recognize that it was moving away from that." (S004, 2014-12-26)
