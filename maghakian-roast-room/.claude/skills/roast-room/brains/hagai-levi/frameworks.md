# Frameworks: Hagai Levi

Built from the extract notes on 32 sources (2009 to 2026): long interviews on BeTipul and In Treatment (2009), The Affair (2014), Our Boys (2019), Scenes from a Marriage (2021) and Etty (2025 to 2026), plus format and export context (a 2016 academic study of the BeTipul format, trade pieces, Wikipedia). 15 frameworks. Most-used first, with the two that answer the seat's question (what travels, and how it is translated) at the top. Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. "How to score" lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his own statements, not quotes. "Sources" counts distinct sources.

---

## 1. Archetypes travel, specifics get translated *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A format crosses borders when its characters are basic human archetypes (a couple, a girl coming of age, a woman who cannot marry) and its concept is bare: little local religion, symbol or politics. The local texture is then rebuilt in each country.
- **Parts:** (1) Build the cast from archetypes "everybody can identify with" (S002). (2) Keep the concept abstract, "not very specific" (S023). (3) Accept that some plotlines are too local and must be rewritten, like the Israeli fighter pilot (S003, S008). (4) Expect each country to rebuild the local layer, as Argentina and Brazil turned the pilot into an elite policeman and Italy into an undercover cop (S040).
- **When to use:** Deciding whether a story should be sold as a format, as finished scripts, or as a finished show; reading which parts of a script will survive translation.
- **How to score:** High if the engine works with the names, religion and politics removed. Medium if one or two story lines depend on a national institution (army, church, a historic event) that can be swapped. Low if the premise itself is the local institution. (guidance, inferred from S002, S008, S023, S040)
- **Quotes:** "It was very important for me to show archetypical stories that represent something that everybody can identify with, like the woman who can't get married or the girl who is afraid to grow up." (S002) "it was more like archetypes of characters that I wanted to discuss. The couple, a girl coming of age, these very, very basic archetypes." (S023) "It's a very conceptual show. It's not very specific." (S023) "Some of the plotlines, such as the fighter pilot, are too Israeli." (S008)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S003, S008, S010, S023, S040)

## 2. Cultural translation of a format *(his phrase: "culturally translated")*
- **What it is:** A remake that copies the text line by line feels wrong in the new country. Translate the problem under the scene, not the words. In New York a 40-year-old single woman's problem was not missing children but missing love.
- **Steps:** (1) First season close to the original, often near word for word (S003). (2) Watch what does not land. (3) Rewrite the underlying problem in local terms, with a local writing team (S002, S008). (4) Keep the structure, the sessions, the real time.
- **When to use:** Planning a remake, briefing a foreign co-writer, writing a format bible.
- **How to score:** High if the pitch names, per storyline, the local equivalent of the problem. Low if the plan is "translate and recast". (guidance, inferred from S002, S003, S008)
- **View over time:** 2009: the first US season was too close; the second was "much deeper and more adapted" (S003). By 2021 he had helped 19 versions and called it something to "crack" each time (S023).
- **Quotes:** "After the first season we thought it was too close, and that the adaptations should be culturally translated." (S002) "So then we transformed it so the problem was no longer a lack of children but a lack of love." (S002) "The first season is very close to the original, almost word for word in the first few weeks." (S003) "So again and again, I came to a specific culture to help them adapt it. And we had to crack how can we do the adaptation, culturally, adapting it to a different place." (S023)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S003, S008, S023, S040)

## 3. Two people, one room, real time *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** His core form. Long continuous dialogue between two people in one place, with no cuts inside the scene, so the drama has to go to the bone. It is cheap, actor-led, and portable to any market with good actors.
- **Parts:** One location; two actors per episode; half an hour of continuous real-time dialogue (S010, S023); strong actors who can hold a close-up (S003); weeks of rehearsal when possible (S024).
- **When to use:** Any low-budget drama meant to travel, any project where dialogue is the asset.
- **How to score:** High if each episode can be shot in one room with two actors and still escalates. Low if the episode needs action or many locations to work. (guidance, inferred from S008, S010, S023)
- **Quotes:** "My first rule in "In Treatment" was that every episode should be non-stop, like continuous, real-time dialogue for half an hour. There shouldn't be any cut in it." (S023) "My intention was always to make it on the cheap...two actors, one location." (S008) "Each episode is a continuous 30 minutes dialogue between the therapist and the patient." (S010) "When two people have a conversation, a third entity is created between them through dialogue." (S001)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S002, S003, S008, S010, S023, S024, S045)

## 4. The good version of a popular bad form *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Take a form the market already buys (the daily soap, the telenovela strip) and keep its distribution power while replacing its content with quality. BeTipul was a "daily series for intellectuals".
- **Parts:** (1) Name the form's real asset: the strip enters the viewer's weekly schedule (S002). (2) Keep that asset. (3) Drop the shallowness. (4) Sell it as cheap and as prestige at once (S002, S040).
- **When to use:** Designing a format for a small broadcaster or a buyer abroad; finding a hook beyond "good writing".
- **How to score:** High if the project borrows a proven slot or rhythm a buyer already schedules. Low if it asks the buyer to invent a new slot. (guidance, inferred from S002, S003, S040)
- **Quotes:** "I realised this daily thing has power because it's getting to people's schedules in their home... and I thought it was a really good format." (S002) "The problem is that it's shallow and stupid most of the time, but why can't we use this form and create something that's better?" (S002) "I wondered why there couldn't be a good daily drama rather than a bad one" (S003)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S003, S008, S040)

## 5. Cheap by design *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Build the low cost into the concept, not into the budget line. The format's price is part of why HBO bought it: about five times cheaper per episode than a US series (S040).
- **Parts:** Two actors, one location (S008); a show that "won't cost you anything" (S004); but budget the hidden cost, writing time: a month per episode and a year and a half of development (S008).
- **When to use:** Any first sale from a small market; any pitch to a buyer who is risking a slot.
- **How to score:** High if cost per episode is a selling point the buyer can repeat. Medium if cheap only because of local wages. Low if the concept needs scale. (guidance, inferred from S004, S008, S040)
- **Quotes:** "It's also cheap, which helps." (S003) "they did like the idea of having a daily series for intellectuals and of course it's cheap so it's always good for broadcasters." (S002) "In fact, I needed an entire month to write each episode. So development took me one and a half years before shooting." (S008) "each episode of BeTipul/In Treatment cost 5 times less than the average television series produced in the U.S. industry" (S040)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S003, S004, S008, S040)

## 6. The structural device that turns the format *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** One rule or twist that makes the format unlike anything else. In BeTipul, the therapist sees his own therapist on Friday, so the hero is the therapist. In Scenes from a Marriage, the gender swap.
- **Steps:** Look for the one device that changes who the hero is or how time works; test it; build the schedule around it.
- **When to use:** When a pitch sounds like "a good drama about X" and needs a format hook a buyer can repeat in one line.
- **How to score:** High if the device can be stated in one sentence and is copyable in another country. Low if the format is only its subject. (guidance, inferred from S003, S023, S025)
- **Quotes:** "When I realised during planning for the original series that he would go to his therapist on a Friday I knew I had something." (S003) "This whole thing is kind of a gender experiment for me. That was actually the decision that helped me to start working on it." (S023) "keep[s] the structure, the story, but it creates suddenly a totally different reality" (S025)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S003, S023, S025)

## 7. A new form for every story *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Each new show starts by asking what form only this story could have.
- **When to use:** Development, before writing the pilot.
- **How to score:** High if the form could not be moved to another story. Low if it is a genre template. (guidance, inferred from S001, S030)
- **Quotes:** "When I create a new show I look for a new form. I believe there is a new way to tell a certain story that isn't conventional." (S001) "It's the most interesting inquiry. How can I tell the story in a way that only fits this story?" (S001)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S024, S030)

## 8. Local writer partner abroad *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** When you write for another culture, pair with a writer from it. He knows a foreign culture "95%, but not 100%" (S023). Sarah Treem on The Affair, Amy Herzog on Scenes from a Marriage.
- **When to use:** Any remake or foreign-language version; any Armenian writer pitching a US or European remake of their own show.
- **How to score:** High if a named local writer is attached. Low if the plan is to translate the scripts and hand them over. (guidance, inferred from S023, S027)
- **Quotes:** "I knew from the beginning that if I'm doing a show in America or in any other culture I need to collaborate with a local writer" (S027) "When people don't do that, the output is very generic because you're not rooted in that culture." (S027) "when I direct in Israel, I know in America, like 95%, but not 100%." (S023)
- **Sources:** 4 (S008, S023, S025, S027)

## 9. Find the why before you adapt *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Do not remake until you know why it must exist now and why you are the one. Scenes from a Marriage sat seven years until the gender swap gave him the reason. A remake must be "definitive again".
- **When to use:** Buying or selling remake rights; pitching a remake to a platform.
- **How to score:** High if the pitch states what has changed in the world since the original. Low if the reason is only that the title is famous. (guidance, inferred from S024, S028)
- **Quotes:** "I had to have an answer for why I should do it and why I was the one to do it." (S024) "So I felt that if you do it today, you have to make it definitive again. And reality has changed so much. That was a reason to remake it." (S028)
- **Sources:** 3 (S024, S025, S028)

## 10. Remake means remake: keep the text, change around it *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A true remake keeps the structure and episode spine of the original and changes the world around it, like staging Shakespeare. Not "inspired by".
- **When to use:** Deciding between a format remake and a loose adaptation.
- **Quotes:** "It's like Shakespearean theater. You can keep the text, but can change things around the text." (S001) "The idea was totally to make a remake, and that was, for me, the essence of it." (S023) "In every episode, the story always followed the original, changing everything, but keeping the structure and the story of every episode." (S024)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S023, S024, S028)

## 11. Rules and laws per project *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Set strict formal rules at the start (one room, no cut, real time) and work freely inside them. He links it to his religious upbringing.
- **Quotes:** "I feel good when I have rules. Probably because I grew up as a religious person, I feel that every time I have a project, I set up some rules and laws, and then I feel free to work within these rules." (S024)
- **Sources:** 2 (S023, S024)

## 12. Tell, don't show *(his inversion)*
- **What it is:** Let characters tell what happened instead of showing it. It saves money and puts the drama in how people narrate.
- **Quotes:** "It's against everything that they teach you in school, which is, show don't tell. And I tell and don't show, which is totally interesting for me." (S024) "I feel that the way people tell something is often more important than what really happened." (S024) "Sometimes it more interesting to hear characters describe what happened than actually seeing it happen on screen." (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S024)

## 13. Short, closed series *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Fewer hours; British-style runs of six; a new season only when the character is not finished. Seasons are a commercial device.
- **When to use:** Deciding episode count and whether to promise season two to a buyer.
- **How to score:** High if the story is complete in one run. Low if more seasons are planned only to fill a slot. (guidance, inferred from S001, S024)
- **Quotes:** "I like more the British way of six hours, and then another six hours, and that's it." (S024) "The season thing is a very commercial decision, more than everything else." (S024) "I want to feel that I'm not done with a character at the end of a season, so I have to write a new one." (S001)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S002, S024)

## 14. Fable, not case study *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Make the story abstract enough to be about everyone. In Scenes from a Marriage he showed the set and crew to say "this is not a couple from Boston". In Etty he set 1940s Amsterdam in an unplaced present.
- **When to use:** When the story is foreign to its buyer, or historical.
- **Quotes:** "Its' a fable, in a way." (S024) "I felt it's much more abstract in that way. I wanted to say something about a couple from no matter where or when" (S025) "I wanted it to be modern, but not contemporary" (S041) "It should speak about every person or every population in distress or in danger" (S041)
- **Sources:** 5 (S024, S025, S030, S034, S041)

## 15. Truth over balance *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** For stories drawn from a real conflict, do not try to please both sides; stay close to what happened, examine your own side, and never turn the pain into entertainment. Expect attacks from all sides.
- **When to use:** True stories, national traumas, politically loaded projects that a foreign platform may fear.
- **Quotes:** "It wasn't about being balanced, it was about being true to what happened" (S019) "We have to be very close to the truth, while the personal stories we felt we could take some artistic liberty" (S014) "It's much more interesting to deal with my own soul searching than why 'the other' would do something to me" (S035) "Let's be honest, we're going to be attacked from right, from left, from Arabs, from Jews" (S020)
- **Sources:** 6 (S014, S016, S019, S020, S022, S035)
