# Frameworks: Cristian Mungiu

Built from the extract notes on 101 sources (interviews, press conferences, masterclass write-ups, radio transcripts and trade reports in English, Romanian, French and Spanish, 2007 to 2026). 16 frameworks, most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources that use the idea. Almost none are named by him; short descriptive names are marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated rules and numbers, not quotes. Romanian and French sources are quoted in the original language with an English gloss outside the quotation marks.

Fastest route to the seat's question (can we make this small, true and festival-strong, and who co-finances it): 1 (one shot per scene), 4 (real event, then fiction), 7 (stack the co-production), 8 (festival launch and sales), 9 (release it yourself at home).

---

## 1. One shot per scene, no score *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** His founding rule since 4 Months (2007): every scene is one continuous take, no coverage, no music. The camera moves only when an action triggers it. The aim is to respect "the integrity of time" and keep the author invisible so the viewer decides what matters.
- **Parts:** (1) Decide it while writing, and write the script so it is possible. (2) For each scene, find the one position that favours who delivers the key line or the key reaction. (3) Give characters actions while they talk ("splits the mind into two"). (4) Use depth of field, off-camera space and off-screen sound instead of cuts. (5) Replace the score with a dense real soundscape. (6) Make the style invisible: if viewers notice the long takes, it failed.
- **When to use:** Any decision on style, schedule or crew size for a small drama. It is also a budget rule: no coverage means fewer setups, but more rehearsal and more takes.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the key scenes are designed as single takes with motivated movement and the schedule allows about one important dialogue scene a day; 3 if long takes are used for effect but the film still relies on coverage and score to create feeling; 1 if the emotion depends on music cues and close-ups. His own ceiling: "We normally can do one important dialogue scene per day and no more." (S017)
- **Quote:** "I believe that it's best for the film to have everything shot in just one shot per scene. Once you decide this, the rest falls into place." (S003) Also: "My camera won't move unless there's a movement triggering the camera to move." (S003)
- **More evidence:** "there's this integrity of time that cinema can preserve, on the condition that you don't use editing" (S010, 2023). "There is no editing in life. You can't cut out the things that you don't like." (S002, 2017). "I used them because I want to make myself as invisible as possible, because whenever you cut a scene you make your presence felt." (S012, 2013). On the cost: "you become like the prisoner of your own decision, and everything is shaped in connection with this" (S032, 2008).
- **Sources:** 22 (S001, S002, S003, S005, S007, S009, S010, S011, S012, S015, S017, S022, S032, S034, S037, S044, S063, S094, S096, S098, S101, S105)

## 2. Edit the sound, not the picture; the editor sits on set *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Because the picture cannot be cut, he protects rhythm in two ways: the editor works on location every night, and dialogue is edited across takes, down to single consonants.
- **Parts:** (1) Actors say exactly the same words in every take. (2) The editor sets up in a hotel room, picks first, and starts the sound cut as soon as two scenes connect. (3) Choose the take for acting and rhythm, then patch lines from other takes; "90% is okay". (4) If no usable take exists, reshoot while the unit is still there. (5) Record wild takes without camera and every sound of the place.
- **When to use:** Any long-take shoot, any short schedule where a reshoot later is impossible, any production asking whether it can afford an on-set editor.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the plan has an editor cutting during the shoot and a sound plan that records the location; 3 if editing starts after wrap but takes are protected; 1 if rhythm problems will only be found in post when the unit is gone.
- **Quote:** "I don't have the freedom of editing the picture, but I still have the freedom of editing the sound... on the condition that the actors say the same thing every time." (S001)
- **More evidence:** "I start editing during the shooting. My editor would come on the set." (S003, 2017). "For us, it's not possible to alter the rhythm later on. If it's too slow, you're just dying there; there's nothing much you can do." (S005, 2017). "It needs to have the right rhythm and be well-played, but not 100%. 90% is okay. The rest I can correct." (S003). On Fjord in four languages: "We have 10-30 takes with tiny differences in rhythm and believability of the performances." (S050, 2026)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S003, S005, S011, S050, S097, S105, S033)

## 3. Rehearse until the embarrassment is gone *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** His actor method. Cast people whose own personality is close to the role, test whether they read the subtext, rehearse three or four weeks, then shoot 10 to 40 takes until the scene stops feeling like actors saying lines.
- **Parts:** (1) Cast wide: small theatres, students, non-professionals, Facebook photos rather than headshots; narrow about 50 to 10. (2) Read the line yourself in casting; "70% of it is in place" if they read its logic. (3) Act the scene for them; give practical notes on rhythm and intensity, not concepts. (4) Nothing improvised; actors know the text by heart. (5) Shoot until it feels free; talk to everyone between takes. (6) Actors play the character's need, never the film's meaning.
- **When to use:** Casting decisions, rehearsal budgets, using non-actors or foreign-language actors, deciding how many takes a schedule can carry.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the budget holds real rehearsal time and casting tests for subtext; 3 if stars are cast first and rehearsal is cut to a table read; 1 if the plan relies on improvisation or on actors unfamiliar with long takes with no time to train them.
- **Quote:** "If I detect that they can read this logic at the beginning of the process, then 70% of it is in place already." (S017)
- **More evidence:** "I always start from practical things: I show them what to do, how to do it, and I speak about the rhythm and intensity of the scene." (S013, 2013). "It's not for everybody, so the casting is very important. We have to check beforehand if they understand what the style is going to be." (S007, 2025). "Don't interpret the sense and the meaning of the film. You are a father. You lost your children." (S008, 2026). Limit: on 4 Months, "knowing that I had to shoot these very long takes, I couldn't take the risk of using non-professionals" (S037, 2008).
- **Sources:** 14 (S001, S003, S005, S007, S008, S013, S014, S017, S036, S037, S045, S049, S050, S097)

## 4. Real event, then fiction *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Every film starts from a real case: a newspaper story, a friend's confession, a viral video. He keeps press clippings in folders for years, researches until he sees a pattern, talks to everyone involved, then fictionalizes "in spirit" and invents what the story needs.
- **Parts:** (1) Keep folders of press stories by theme; wait until one keeps polarizing people. (2) Check that the case is typical, not exceptional. (3) Meet the real people, read the files, visit the place. (4) Fictionalize: invent relationships and characters, keep logistic detail. (5) Tell it from one character who "understands what happens".
- **When to use:** Development decisions, "is this story strong enough", true-story rights questions, whether a film needs a famous IP to exist.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the story comes from a documented real situation the team knows from inside and has researched with the people involved; 3 if real but secondhand; 1 if invented "starting from ideas" or set in a world the writer does not know in detail.
- **Quote:** "I don't like writing screenplays in which I try to invent a story starting from ideas" (S051)
- **More evidence:** "Life is always the primary source of inspiration for my stories. I start with real incidents and try to understand what that story says about us as people and about the state of the world today." (S091, 2022). "Finally, I decided to fictionalize the story, not a reenactment." (S047, 2026). "for a long while, I was just watching it as I do with a lot of cases like this, just putting material aside in a folder on the computer" (S048, 2026). "dacă nu te pricepi să vorbești despre esența naturii umane, degeaba faci filme inspirate de realitatea imediată" (S080, 2025) [true events are worthless if you cannot speak about human nature].
- **Sources:** 17 (S001, S011, S012, S013, S026, S034, S037, S047, S048, S049, S051, S080, S091, S094, S096, S105, S110)

## 5. Story first, one point of view, a short dramatic window *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** He starts from the story, not the character, and picks a short, dramatic moment of decision seen through one person. The audience knows only what that person knows.
- **Parts:** (1) A short time frame (4 Months is one day; later films days or weeks). (2) The main character is the one who understands, not the one it happens to. (3) Drop real events the POV character cannot witness. (4) Layer side stories that reveal society. (5) Start and end mid-scene: a slice of a life.
- **When to use:** Script structure decisions, whose story it is, whether a script is too sprawling for a small budget.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the script has one POV, a short window and one central decision; 3 if it has several POVs with a reason; 1 if it is an episodic chronicle with no decision at its center.
- **Quote:** "For me the main character is "he who understands what happens" and not to whom it happens." (S015)
- **More evidence:** "I don't start from the characters. I start from the story." (S002, 2017). "describiendo un momento corto, pero bastante dramático de toma de decisiones, que dura un período de tiempo relativamente corto" (S099, 2023) [a short but dramatic moment of decision]. "This is why we started the film in the middle of a very important scene, and ended the film in the middle of a scene." (S037, 2008)
- **Sources:** 11 (S002, S003, S013, S015, S017, S021, S037, S071, S098, S099, S110)

## 6. Ambiguity, no verdict, characters who are not coherent *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** His ethical rule for the script: present every side's best argument, give no solution, and reread the script so no character is fully coherent. If the meaning can be put in one sentence, it is not good enough.
- **Parts:** (1) Keep your citizen opinion out. (2) Defend each character's view in dialogue and in direction. (3) Leave one key fact unknown (who threw the stone, how the bruise came). (4) Never translate a metaphor into words; give it a rational explanation in the story.
- **When to use:** Political or social subjects, notes from financiers or distributors asking for clarity, marketing that wants a message.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if both sides of the film's conflict would recognize their own best argument; 3 if one side is a straw man; 1 if the film tells the audience what to believe.
- **Quote:** "If you make a film in which you take sides, that's just going to be propaganda for people who are already convinced" (S008)
- **More evidence:** "If a film is verbally interpretable only in a unidirectional way, it's no good." (S045, 2022). "if those decisions are coherent, they belong to you as a filmmaker, and not to them as characters" (S009, 2023). "For me, that is a very dishonest and manipulative way of story-telling." (S013, 2013). Story: his French distributor said it was unclear who threw the stone, Frémaux said it was too clear; he kept it: "Because it means that it's quite balanced. It means that it's fine." (S019, 2017)
- **Sources:** 15 (S005, S008, S009, S010, S013, S019, S020, S024, S027, S045, S049, S050, S063, S102, S105)

## 7. Stack the co-production around a national core *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** How his films are actually paid for. The national fund (CNC Romania) is the anchor; outside money comes in as co-production once a film is too expensive for Romania alone. His first three films had no French co-producer; from Beyond the Hills (2012) he works with French and Belgian partners (Why Not, the Dardennes' Les Films du Fleuve); Fjord (2026) stacked six countries.
- **Parts:** (1) Win the national competition (in 2008 about half the budget, capped near 400 to 600 thousand dollars; S105). (2) Start without the full budget if the season demands it (Occident started short and ran out mid-shoot, S022, S044; Beyond the Hills was shot fast in winter right after its CNC award to make Cannes, S072). (3) For a bigger film, bring a strong French co-producer early; they read the script and must believe in hard scenes. (4) Add each co-producer country's soft money: film institutes, regional funds, Nordic Film and TV Fund, Eurimages, Creative Europe MEDIA, broadcasters. (5) Spread post-production across partner countries to trigger their spend. (6) Keep the film's nationality: it follows the writer-director, not where most money came from. (7) Keep final cut.
- **When to use:** Any "shoot now small or wait for a partner" question, any budget above what one country can fund.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the national fund is secured, one strong co-producer is attached and each extra country brings soft money and a real creative role; 3 if co-production adds money but forces spend you would not choose; 1 if the plan waits on a partner with no anchor money and no festival track record.
- **Quote:** "J'ai fait mes trois premiers films sans aucun coproducteur français. Cela ne m'a pas empêché de remporter la Palme d'Or" (S066) [My first three films had no French co-producer. That did not stop me winning the Palme d'Or.]
- **More evidence:** "we managed to finance 4 Months with a very little budget, but to finance it entirely in Romania" (S022, 2016). The French aid "oblige les cinéastes à travailler avec des budgets relativement restreints" (S066, 2018). "We shot in Norway, edited in Romania, sound design in Finland, and mixing in Denmark" (S050, 2026). "I received the maximum legal support that can be granted to cinema, but it is entirely insufficient if you want to make a film like the one I made. The Romanian state was only capable of investing one-sixth of the film's budget." (S056, 2026). "naţionalitatea filmului este legată nu de locul din care au venit cei mai mulţi bani, ci de naţionalitatea scenaristului şi regizorului" (S087, 2026) [nationality follows the writer-director, not the money].
- **Numbers from the corpus (facts, not quotes):** Beyond the Hills: CNC 2,819,000 lei (633,500 euro), 36.1 percent of budget (S084). R.M.N.: the largest CNC grant of its 2014 session, 4,455,000 lei (S073). Fjord: largest CNC grant of its session, 703,538 euro (S089); about 40 Romanian crew brought partly because Romanian rates are lower than Nordic ones (producer Tudor Reu, S053).
- **Sources:** 16 (S001, S020, S022, S038, S046, S050, S053, S056, S059, S066, S072, S084, S087, S089, S090, S105)

## 8. Festival launch, sales agent, pre-sales *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** For a small-country film, the festival is the release. He uses rough-cut screenings to get a strong sales agent, aims every film at Cannes, and sells territories early, ideally on the script.
- **Parts:** (1) Show a rough cut to friendly festivals and sales agents (Rotterdam put money into 4 Months' post). (2) Secure a strong sales agent before the premiere. (3) Aim for Cannes, but never assume selection; refresh each time. (4) Choose the festival slot with the agent. (5) Pre-sell: Graduation was "bought as a screenplay"; Fjord sold 50-plus territories before shooting. (6) Then a US distributor that will fund an awards campaign.
- **When to use:** Release strategy, timing of the shoot against festival deadlines, whether to show a rough cut, Oscar submission.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if a sales agent is attached, the film can be ready for a top-three festival deadline, and some territories are pre-sold; 3 if the festival is the plan but no agent; 1 if the film depends on local box office alone.
- **Quote:** "I knew that I needed a strong sales agent for the film to have a good fate later on" (S015)
- **More evidence:** "I made a screening in Rotterdam with a rough cut of this film two days after I finished principal photography." (S015, 2008). "The film was bought as a screenplay by many countries and was later sold during the Cannes Film Festival to some other territories." (S022, 2016). "Many people thought that after I won the Palme D'Or my next film would automatically be accepted in Cannes. But it's not like that." (S109, 2012). "c'est pour cette raison que la réception du film en festivals est si importante pour nous. C'est la seule chose qui puisse permettre au film d'exister réellement." (S097, 2012) [festival reception is the only thing that lets the film exist].
- **Sources:** 14 (S006, S015, S022, S036, S039, S049, S061, S063, S087, S090, S097, S109, S077, S057)

## 9. Release it yourself at home *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Romania lost most of its cinemas, so he self-distributes (Voodoo Films), tours films by caravan to towns with no cinema, holds Q&As, and runs a festival to build an audience.
- **Parts:** (1) Fundraise and rent venues; bring projection equipment. (2) Tour 15 to 30 towns, including the place where the story happened. (3) Hold two debates: the official Q&A and the honest one outside. (4) Film the tour and use it as an argument with government. (5) Build taste: Les Films de Cannes à Bucarest, education programmes.
- **When to use:** Home-market release for an art film from a country with few screens; audience-building plans.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the plan has a home release beyond multiplexes and a festival or touring layer; 3 if a token release; 1 if the film only exists abroad.
- **Quote:** "I'm releasing my own films back home, because we have very few theaters back at home, we have to plan with all the equipment and plan screenings and stuff." (S005)
- **More evidence:** "I decided after Cannes to release this film myself, although this is not what I do normally, just as a way of fighting against not having cinemas over here." (S105, 2008). "After Cannes, I screened this film in 30 different villages in the region" (S010, 2023). "My extreme example became the norm." (S044, 2025).
- **Sources:** 13 (S005, S010, S022, S026, S028, S044, S055, S060, S067, S083, S094, S105, S024)

## 10. Real location, built main set *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Shoot real places for flavour and for the actors, but build the main location on site when reality cannot hold the camera choreography.
- **Parts:** Scout the real site of the event first; find a place with life visible outside; build the key house or monastery on location; dress empty real rooms from memory for period films.
- **When to use:** Studio vs location, period films on a small budget.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the key set is built or chosen for the long-take choreography in a real place; 1 if the film is studio-bound with no outside world.
- **Quote:** "For every other of my last three or four films, I built directly on the main location." (S043)
- **More evidence:** "you can never get the same flavor from a set than you can have from a real space" (S039, 2007). "I absolutely want to see from this room that there is life outside going on." (S039)
- **Sources:** 6 (S039, S043, S091, S105, S110, S047)

## 11. Plan B everything, then wait for the weather *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Shoot in the season the story needs, chronologically when possible, and reschedule daily for weather if the image depends on it.
- **Quote:** "Trebuie să-ți calibrezi așteptările în funcție de ceea ce vei găsi acolo." (S059) [Calibrate your expectations to what you will find there.]
- **Evidence:** 4 Months was planned for winter from the start (S105); Beyond the Hills shot fast after its CNC award to catch heavy snow (S072, journalist); on Fjord he changed the schedule every day waiting for snow (S063, paraphrase); Beyond the Hills shot chronologically (S011, S097).
- **Sources:** 5 (S011, S059, S063, S097, S105)

## 12. Thriller form, everyday material *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Art films must not be boring. He builds tension like a genre film from ordinary life.
- **Quote:** "I make the effort of having the form of the film as enjoyable as possible. It looks like a thriller." (S008)
- **More evidence:** "Nu cred deloc că filmul de artă trebuie să fie ceva care să fie dificil de consumat sau plicticos, doar că trebuie să aibă ceva de spus" (S061, 2026) [art film need not be hard or boring, it must have something to say]. "4 Months was designed like a thriller while Beyond the Hills is more like a novel" (S098, 2012).
- **Sources:** 7 (S008, S021, S059, S061, S063, S098, S023)

## 13. Stars on indie terms *(unnamed; Fjord 2026)*
- **What it is:** Use international stars only when the project fits both sides, on the indie set's terms: no entourage, physical change to break their image, the same method.
- **Quote:** "We worked with two Hollywood stars on budgets that we could afford. Without their generosity, we would not have been able to make the film." (S056)
- **More evidence:** "I was happy to work with these people without having to change my way of making cinema" (S007, 2025). "I waited for years to work with Sebastian until I found the right project." (S056). Stars moved the company to another level: "Odată ce angajezi staruri internaționale, de la buget la logistică, lucrurile se mută într-o altă zonă" (S059, 2025).
- **Sources:** 8 (S007, S047, S056, S059, S061, S063, S075, S076)

## 14. Directing in a language you do not speak *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Fjord's method: phonetic script, a native-speaker director as script supervisor through editing, first takes in English to find rhythm, then the target language.
- **Quote:** "They were very generous and understood my need to have the first few takes done in English. Once we got the right rhythm, we went back to Norwegian." (S050)
- **Limit:** Until 2022 he refused English "just because I think I'll have a wider audience" (S045); in 2026 he made the step "precaut" because Romania-anchored films struggle for visibility (S063).
- **Sources:** 4 (S045, S050, S059, S063)

## 15. Awards campaign as marketing budget *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** An Oscar nomination is bought with distributor muscle and campaign money, not won by quality alone.
- **Quote:** "Filmul trebuie să aibă cel mai grozav start, cel mai tare distribuitor şi un buget minimal, undeva între 5 şi 10 milioane de euro." (S087) [best start, strongest distributor, minimum 5 to 10 million euro].
- **More evidence:** "Iar câtă vreme bugetul va fi zero, șansele vor fi la fel și nu vom ajunge pe nicio lista scurtă." (S080, 2025) [with a zero budget, zero chance].
- **Sources:** 5 (S063, S077, S080, S086, S087)

## 16. Shorts as the school *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Make the mistakes in short films; the first feature is a business card.
- **Quote:** "Above all, they allow a young director to make all the mistakes that he mustn't make later on." (S042)
- **More evidence:** "Your first feature film is your business card, and so much depends on it." (S042, 2017)
- **Sources:** 2 (S042, S044)
