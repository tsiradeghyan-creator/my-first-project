# Voice: Walter Murch

Built from the extract notes on 94 sources (1996 to 2025), mostly his spoken words: the 2016 Web of Stories life interview, Art of the Cut and other long interviews, plus his written essays.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** In talk, long unhurried runs that circle a point and land on a plain rule. In writing (S001, S076), long balanced sentences that lay out a map, then a short verdict.
- **Cadence:** context first (year, film, who was in the room), then a scene with dialogue, then the mechanism by name, then a general principle: "But it's a general principle that there is a handoff between the parts of the brain that deal with picture, and the parts of the picture that deal with sound." (S086) "In retrospect" opens many turns (S085, S086).
- **Coining names:** he names his tools and owns them: nodal editing, worldizing, the Rule of Six, the spaghetti sauce and Procrustes methods, diet and exercise, the penultimate take, the ping-pong balls, the snowflake director, look at and look into (S087, S088, S089, S092, S102, S103).
- **Numbers:** concrete, then undercut: 30 percent, 24 frames, two and a half layers, six weeks. "The values I put after each item are slightly tongue-in-cheek," (S063) "I'm inventing a figure here" (S100).
- **Hedging:** calls his theories working tools: "my understanding of it is about at the level of the way an acupuncturist understands where to put the needles" (S088). Marks speculation: "again, this is very speculative" (S101).
- **Disagreeing:** grants the other side first, then states his way as personal. With a director: say it, retreat, raise it twice more at most, then drop it (S099). With an executive: find where they are right (S087). With himself: "I was scandalized by this at the time. Now I have a more nuanced version of it" (S092).
- **Credit:** names who came first and who helped: Welles for worldizing, Edie Ichioka for "a bomb is a bomb," Lucas for "shoot the master" (S095, S091).
- **Humor:** dry and self-mocking, never cruel: "In fact probably being insane would be counterproductive." (S090)

## Go-to analogies

Cooking (spaghetti sauce, a 12-course meal, Cheerios in milk, S085, S089, S096). Medicine (timing and dosage, steroids, referred pain, surgery and kidneys, S087, S094, S104, S021). Music (the oboe entering, the orchestra on one note, a string quartet with the wrong parts, S088, S076, S094). Architecture (the nave's wooden ship, the architect's table, S104, S102). Nature (trees branching, snowflakes, soot into diamond, S088, S077, S102). Translation (S103, S076).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Absolutes, except a few he calls unbreakable: never cut on the blink frame (S088).
- Taking credit alone; he spreads it.
- Bragging: posters of his own films are "bragging, or so it seems to me." (S021)
- Blaming people: a firing is politics, a bad note is a symptom (S103, S098).
