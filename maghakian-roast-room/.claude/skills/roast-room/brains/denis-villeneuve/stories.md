# Stories: Denis Villeneuve

Built from the extract notes on 126 sources (2011 to 2026). 18 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Quotes are verbatim with source ids; the rest is paraphrase.

---

1. **The pause after two features.** After August 32nd on Earth and Maelström he felt empty and saw the problem was him: "those movies were driven by ego" (S019). He stopped for years, watched theatre directors work with actors, and came back with Polytechnique and Incendies. Lesson: stop when the work is hollow; "So that pause was probably the best and most important artistic decision I've ever made." (S019) Also S061, S026, S120.

2. **Enemy as the anchor beside Prisoners.** Afraid Hollywood would crush him, he got the Prisoners producers to let him shoot the small, strange Enemy first, as an acting laboratory with Jake Gyllenhaal. "Like someone who's rock climbing, I had something solid that will help me not to fall down the cliff. 'Enemy' was that anchor." (S026) Lesson: keep a personal anchor when you enter a bigger system; the improvisation method he learned there carried into Prisoners and Sicario (S068). Also S019, S061.

3. **Pitching Prisoners with nothing to lose.** He went to Los Angeles treating the meeting as "a cultural experience", told the truth about what the film needed, and got a 50 million dollar film he would have made for 7 or 8 million in Quebec (S123). "I just told the truth, and being straightforward might be why I got the project. I wasn't trying to please them." (S019) Lesson: pitch the film you would make, not what you think they want. Also S120.

4. **The Métal Hurlant boxes.** His aunt brought three boxes of European sci-fi comics when he was a child. "I'm still haunted by those boxes." (S105) "It changed my life. Those boxes are the reason I'm here." (S123) Lesson he draws: the images that matter come from childhood, before other influences. Also S040.

5. **The village between a church and a nuclear plant.** He grew up in Gentilly, Quebec, between the church and the Gentilly power plant; his father told him to take the pills if anything happened (S123). He links that fear to his giant ships and to Dune's mix of religion and power (S040, S105). Lesson: "When you do something as an artist, you're always talking about your hometown" (S105).

6. **The TV program around the world at 22.** A Radio-Canada competition sent him alone with a camera to make a short film every week. "it was by far the best and most important film school." (S019) "You might land in Japan, not know the language, not even know how to ask for a fork" (S123). Lesson: learn by doing, alone with a camera; he still gives beginners the same advice (S061).

7. **Making soup for Pierre Perrault on Ellesmere Island.** As a young assistant on a documentary near the North Pole he learned to listen to landscape. "There's something about landscape. It's like human faces. It's like - according to the light." (S105) Lesson: landscape carries the character's emotion. Also S040, S125.

8. **Sicario's storm clouds.** Storms filled the New Mexico sky at the end of shooting days. "instead of avoiding them, we started to chase them" (S026). Lesson from Deakins: take the gifts of the gods of cinema instead of fighting them. Also S111 (a real eclipse on the day of the eclipse scene in Dune Part Two).

9. **Weeks in a Montreal hotel room drawing 2049.** He brought Roger Deakins in at the very start and, with a storyboard artist, drew the whole film. "And that was where the movie was born, I'd say. With Roger." (S048) Lesson: bring your key partner in before the script is locked; the boards are where the film is rewritten. Also S005.

10. **Deakins says no to "fix it later".** On 2049 he planned to correct unclear artwork later; Deakins told him to settle it at the start. "I've learned the hard way that you need to be as precise as you can, otherwise it goes in a lot of directions." (S025) Lesson: precision early saves the film in post.

11. **Ridley Scott's blessing and "carte blanche".** His condition for 2049 was to meet Scott and hear yes in person (S045, S019). Scott gave him full freedom and stayed away: "I would not have been able to work with Ridley on my back, it's not possible." (S048) Lesson: enter someone else's world only with the creator's blessing and real freedom. Still: "I don't think I will ever approach someone else's universe again." (S019, 2024), a vow he later broke for Bond (S126).

12. **Blade Runner 2049 as a dance with disaster.** "I knew that when I did this movie, I flirted with disaster. I put myself into massive artistic danger." (S108) The film underperformed, and he had not rewatched it years later (S108). Lesson: worth the risk, but he knows the studio had reason to doubt him after it (S123). Also S019, S107.

13. **"Dune" as the blank-check answer.** At Venice with Arrival he was asked what he would make with a blank check and said Dune; Legendary's Mary Parent called, and it was the fastest deal he ever made (S123, S108). Lesson: say your real dream out loud; and he set a condition, two films, because "it's just too dense to do in a single film" (S123).

14. **HBO Max learned from the news.** In 2021 Warner put Dune on streaming the same day as theatres. "My problem with what happened was the way they went about doing it. I learned about it in the news, the same way everybody else did." (S123) "I was so angry, bitter and wounded," (S040) Lesson: he can accept a hard business call if told the truth; he cannot accept being blindsided.

15. **The worm unit.** To shoot the sandworm ride in natural light over many weeks, he designed how a human could ride a worm, taught the crew in a seminar, and ran a second unit led by Tanya Lapointe while he shot the main unit. It was "by far the most challenging but rewarding creative experience of my life." (S105) Lesson: design the believable behaviour first, then refuse aesthetic compromise. Also S001.

16. **One hundred people grooming the dunes.** On Dune Part Two the whole crew walked one path in the sand, and every night groomers erased the footsteps so the wind could finish the job (S106). Lesson: real locations demand crew discipline, and the discipline is worth it.

17. **The 3 a.m. call to Josh Brolin.** He dreamed a new idea for Gurney and insisted Brolin come over in the middle of the night to talk it through in person (S040). Lesson: ideas are worked out face to face; with his editor too, "There are so many ideas that Joe and I have, I don't know if it's his idea or my idea" (S040).

18. **Coming back to Arrakis.** After Part Two he told his crew he was taking a break, then "I kept waking in the middle of the night with those images" (S116). He went back "to not go back to Arrakis by nostalgia or because I was missing it, but more because I had to by necessity" (S129), as a thriller with a new DP and film stock, and lined up Bond as the exit (S129). Lesson: return only by urgency, and make it a different film (S115, S117).
