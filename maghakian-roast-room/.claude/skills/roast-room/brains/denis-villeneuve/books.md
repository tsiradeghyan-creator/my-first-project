# Books: Denis Villeneuve

Built from the extract notes on 126 sources. He has written no books. His body of work is his films, and several are adaptations of books and plays he talks about often. Below: the works he teaches from in interviews, with what each taught him in his own account. No book text is reproduced.

## Films as his "body of work"

- **August 32nd on Earth (1998) and Maelström (2000).** Early features he calls driven by ego; the reason for his long pause (S019, S061).
- **Polytechnique (2009).** The 1989 Montreal massacre. A year of research meeting everyone involved; "the most painful movie" he has made (S120). The one film he made "to heal a wound at home" (S068).
- **Incendies (2010).** From Wajdi Mouawad's play. Taught him to adapt by cutting theatrical images and entering a foreign culture through family and intimacy; the first film where he felt "sovereign" (S026, S104).
- **Enemy (2013).** From José Saramago's novel The Double. His "acting laboratory" with Jake Gyllenhaal and his anchor before Hollywood (S026, S068).
- **Prisoners (2013).** First studio film; he kept his director's cut and learned he could keep control (S019, S068).
- **Sicario (2015).** Taylor Sheridan's script; tension by stretching time, violence by suggestion (S061, S106).
- **Arrival (2016).** From Ted Chiang's short story "Story of Your Life". The longest and hardest edit of his career; intimacy kept in front of the spectacle (S005, S026).
- **Blade Runner 2049 (2017).** Sequel to Ridley Scott's film, built on Philip K. Dick's doubt about identity. A crash course in world building and VFX realism with Deakins (S048, S108).
- **Dune: Part One (2021) and Part Two (2024).** From Frank Herbert's Dune. His teenage dream, split in two; Part One "an introduction to a world", Part Two the war film and the warning about the messiah (S105, S108, S111).
- **Dune: Part Three (2026).** Inspired by Herbert's Dune Messiah. A thriller after a time jump, shot mostly on film; his last Dune (S115, S116, S117).
- **Bond 26 (announced 2025).** The next James Bond film, which he calls "sacred territory" (S126).

## Books that shaped how he decides

- **Frank Herbert, Dune (1965) and Dune Messiah (1969).** He reads Dune as ecology, religion and politics, and a warning against charismatic saviours. His rule "when in doubt, go back to the book" comes from it (S040, S105, S110, S123).
- **Ted Chiang, "Story of Your Life".** Language and time; he calls it "a little masterpiece" (S106).
- **Philip K. Dick's novel behind Blade Runner.** Source of his choice to keep Deckard's identity in doubt (S005, S045).
- **Arthur C. Clarke, Rendezvous with Rama.** A book from his youth he was developing as an adaptation (S106).
- **European comics (Métal Hurlant, Moebius, Druillet, Bilal).** His first visual education (S105, S123).
