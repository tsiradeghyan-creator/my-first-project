# Frameworks: Denis Villeneuve

Built from the extract notes on 126 sources (interviews, podcast transcripts, festival talks and profiles in English and French, 2011 to 2026; refreshed 2026-11-26 with podcast and Q&A transcripts). 25 frameworks, most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources where the idea is used (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short descriptive name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated rules, not quotes. French sources are quoted in French.

Fastest route to the seat's question (what is this film really about, and how should it feel): 12 (one bold choice of focus), 7 (an intimate way in), 13 (pure image and sound), then 1 for any adaptation.

---

## 1. The book is the bible; adapting is killing (his words)
- **What it is:** His adaptation method. The source is the only reference for the whole team, and the film stays faithful to the author's idea while it destroys and rebuilds the form. The result is openly his version, and he takes the blame for it.
- **Parts:** (1) Put the team on a source-only diet: no earlier screen versions, no genre neighbours (Star Wars was the elephant in the room on Dune). (2) When in doubt, reread the source and find the answer there. (3) Be faithful to the author's intent, even against the page when the page loses the point (he gave Chani and Jessica more weight because Herbert drops them). (4) Destroy the form: cut big chunks, rewrite scenes, start from scratch where needed, but keep the dramatic structure, key ideas and main characters. (5) Accept that the film shows the adapter more than the author.
- **When to use:** Any book, play, IP, true story, remake or sequel decision. Also when a team is copying an earlier adaptation or the genre leader's look.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the team can state the author's core idea in one sentence, works from the source only, and can list what it cut and why; 3 if it is faithful page by page with no point of view; 1 if it is built from earlier versions or a competitor's look, or if it changes the author's core idea. Inferred from S015, S039, S070, S075, S089.
- **Tension he holds:** In 2024 he says he tries to stay "as invisible as possible" (S012) as the adapter, and in the same season "I think the movie says more about me than it says about Frank Herbert." (S022). Reading: invisible in atmosphere and spirit, personal in the choices (inferred from S012, S022).
- **Quote:** "the best way to respect an author is to be very honest about the way you adapt his work: to totally destroy the original and make it your own." (S070) Also: "when I was in doubt, I was just going back to the Bible, reading Dune again and finding my answers." (S039)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "I don't believe in direct adaptation. I think that it's a violent process where you transform, it's like alchemy." (S048, 2017) "My roots were deep into the book, so I didn't mind about the wind." (S085, 2021) He tells the whole team to "always go back to the book" (S110, 2024).
- **Sources:** 23 (S005, S006, S011, S012, S015, S018, S020, S021, S022, S030, S039, S040, S048, S069, S070, S071, S073, S075, S076, S089, S085, S110, S119)

## 2. Diptych and promise (series architecture; "diptych" and "promise" are his words)
- **What it is:** How he builds a story across several films. Each part keeps the same DNA but gets its own genre and tempo. The first part is a promise that opens the door on a world; later parts pay it off. Each part must stand on its own, and he labels honestly what is unfinished.
- **Parts:** (1) Split a source too dense for one film instead of condensing it. (2) Give each part its own genre and pace: Dune Part One a contemplation, Part Two a war and action film, Part Three a thriller. (3) Plant bridges in part one that pay off later (Chani, Irulan, Alia). (4) Give the next part only a minimal recap so newcomers can follow. (5) Call part one Part One; pretending it is the whole story is dishonest. (6) Complete every character's arc and owe the audience a finished story. (7) Leave seeds so someone else can continue.
- **When to use:** Series and seasons, sequels, splitting a book, a trilogy pitch, a platform asking for "more episodes".
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if each part has its own genre, pace and completed arcs and part one makes a clear promise; 3 if parts differ only in plot; 1 if part one is set-up that cannot stand alone, or a long story is squeezed into one film. Inferred from S013, S030, S096.
- **View change:** 2021: he saw room for a trilogy and would decide later (S085, S099). 2024: the first two films were "really a diptych" and a third would be separate (S029, S031). 2026 (newer): he made Part Three as a thriller after a time jump, with the same characters in a changed world (S034).
- **Quote:** "For Zendaya, I will say Part One was a promise." (S009)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** Part One opens the door and Part Two "bringing the meal" (S108, 2021). Part Two had to work alone: "to make sure that if someone will not have seen Part 1, we'll still be able to enjoy Part 2." (S110, 2024) 2026: "If the first movie was more of a contemplation, like a movie of a boy exploring a new world, and the second one being a war movie, this one is a thriller," (S115)
- **Sources:** 20 (S009, S013, S014, S017, S029, S030, S031, S032, S034, S039, S084, S085, S096, S099, S108, S110, S111, S115, S116, S123)

## 3. Nature precedes the storyboards (his words)
- **What it is:** Reality first. Real locations, natural light, built sets and in-camera work, with visual effects buried inside photographed reality. Reality feeds the actors, the DP and him; a blue room gives him nothing.
- **Parts:** (1) Find the real place that looks closest to the story's place. (2) Plan each shot by the sun (on Dune Part Two each location was recorded in 3D to plan sunlight). (3) Build sets and props so actors react to real rain, sand and objects, never a tennis ball. (4) For each VFX shot, decide first how it will be photographed, in natural light. (5) Hold every effect to one bar: as real as the actor's face.
- **When to use:** Location versus stage or virtual production, VFX budget, any plan that says "we fix it in post".
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the key scenes are photographed in real places or built sets and VFX only extend them; 3 if mixed with a clear reason; 1 if the main world lives on green screen or a virtual set. His bar is that an effect fails if it does not look as real as the actor's face (S025).
- **View change:** His older rule (date not recorded) put storyboards before the screenplay. In 2024 (newer) he put nature above the storyboards (S013).
- **Quote:** "I used to say that storyboards precede the screenplay. But nature precedes the storyboards." (S013)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "We built the whole schedule like a puzzle to maximize the power of natural light" (S025, 2025). In the desert he chose "according to sun position, to deconstruct the whole shooting schedule according to the sunlight" (S105, 2024). On green screen: "Maybe some people can, but not me." (S040, 2021)
- **Sources:** 18 (S005, S012, S013, S014, S017, S021, S025, S029, S037, S040, S045, S048, S070, S091, S098, S105, S106, S111)

## 4. Casting is the most important part *(unnamed as a method; the phrase is his in S045)*
- **What it is:** Casting is the decision he will not compromise on. He casts the central actor first on what the role needs, builds the cast around that person, and uses casting to add what the page lacks.
- **Parts:** (1) Dream of the performer while reading the script. (2) Cast the lead first on the qualities the role needs (Chalamet: youth, candour, vulnerability, intelligence in the eyes), then cast around the lead. (3) Cast for what the script lacks (Gyllenhaal to invade a thin detective role). (4) Decide on the decisive scene (the Gom Jabbar test told him). (5) Prefer actors who carry a scene with their eyes, so scenes can be silent, and collaborators with no ego. (6) Choose the strongest lead who fits, not the biggest name; fight for the right one.
- **When to use:** Any lead casting, choosing the face of a project, hiring a key creative.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the lead is chosen for the role's specific needs and tested on its hardest scene; 3 if strong but generic; 1 if chosen for availability, fame or price alone. Inferred from S012, S021, S044, S045, S065.
- **Quote:** "The most important part of the film process is casting. You need strong actors." (S045)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "I love actors who are good listeners." (S111, 2025) Casting is a bet: "You bring someone on its intuitions about the proximity of an actor and a role, and you take gamble in some ways" (S106, 2024). He casts only after the script holds: "I will allow myself to do casting when I would feel that I have something solid under my feet." (S118, 2021)
- **Sources:** 17 (S012, S018, S021, S044, S045, S047, S049, S054, S065, S070, S071, S075, S077, S084, S106, S111, S118)

## 5. Never walk in your own footsteps (his words in S091)
- **What it is:** The next project is chosen as a reaction to the last one (dark to light, contemplation to action, big to small), and he goes back to a world he already made only for a different film with its own identity, made from urgency, not nostalgia.
- **Parts:** (1) Name what the last film was. (2) Choose a next one that moves away in tone, genre or scale (after five dark films he needed optimism). (3) For a return: same DNA, different genre and pacing. (4) Go back only if the screenplay is ready and the film will beat the last one. (5) Be severe with the material. (6) Line up a very different project as the exit (he called the next Bond film a powerful rocket away from Arrakis).
- **When to use:** Choosing the next project, a sequel or season two, a spin-off, a buyer asking to repeat a hit.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the new project is a different film in genre or pace and there is a ready script that beats the last; 3 if different in plot only; 1 if it repeats the last formula because it worked, or rests on nostalgia or market appetite alone. Inferred from S016, S031, S091.
- **View change:** 2024: Messiah should be his last Dune, made only if better than Part Two (S012, S016, S031). 2026 (newer): he made it, saying he came back by urgency and because he is tired of the material, so he would not be complacent (S033).
- **Quote:** "if I go back there, it's to do something that feels different and has its own identity." (S024) Also, 2026: "To go there with a critical eye and not to be self-indulgent." (S116)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** 2026: "I don't want us to walk into our own footsteps." (S117) Same DNA, new pace: "The idea was to try to make a movie that will have the same DNA, it's a Dune movie, but it's a very different movie," (S128)
- **Sources:** 16 (S014, S016, S021, S024, S031, S033, S034, S055, S066, S085, S091, S115, S116, S117, S128, S129)

## 6. Sub-dictatorship (his word, S067)
- **What it is:** The director holds a precise vision and the final say, but listens hard, because some people on the crew are stronger than him. He says he is better as a channeler than as a dictator.
- **Parts:** (1) Be precise about the vision: colour, ambience, intent. (2) Communicate it to every department so it survives a crew of thousands. (3) State what you need and let the expert bring the answer (Deakins). (4) Pick lieutenants who share your sensibility so little needs explaining. (5) Be firm, never angry. (6) Take the better idea even when it hurts your ego.
- **When to use:** Leading a team, choosing a director or showrunner, a creative dispute, moving from small to large crews.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the leader can state the vision in one line and can name ideas taken from others; 3 if the vision is clear but nobody else shapes it; 1 if there is no clear vision, or a dictator who cannot name one borrowed idea. Inferred from S067, S076, S086.
- **View change:** 2014: "In my previous movies, I was more like a dictator." (S070). From then on (newer) he describes working more and more with others (S067, S086).
- **Quote:** "The film is better when you listen to others. You make the right decisions as a director. Very often directors are dictators, but I think it has to be a dictator who listens a lot." (S076)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "I express what I need, the way I see it, and Roger brings the answer" (S061, 2015). On a boarded action sequence he is openly firm: "I'm more of a dictator, I impose a rhythm, a precise choreography" (S106, 2024).
- **Sources:** 13 (S001, S040, S046, S047, S060, S061, S067, S070, S076, S086, S099, S100, S106)

## 7. An intimate way in (his phrase, S005)
- **What it is:** He only makes a story he has fallen in love with and connects to from a personal point of view. Then he finds a personal door into it, and that door decides what to keep and what to change.
- **Parts:** (1) Fall in love with a story that says something meaningful about the world. (2) Find the personal door: a climate and its light (Canadian winter in Blade Runner 2049), a memory, characters who are outsiders like him (the twins in Incendies). (3) Use the door to choose what to keep and what to change. (4) Dig into your own intimacy for images of fear. (5) Check whether the images keep coming back at night; if they do, the story chose you.
- **When to use:** Choosing whether to take a project, a commission or an adaptation; a pitch with no personal stake.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the maker can name the personal door and the images keep returning; 3 if interested but it is a job; 1 if the only reason is money, a slot or a trend. Inferred from S069, S077, S086.
- **Quote:** "In order to tell a story, I need to have a deep connection with it from a very intimate point of view" (S069)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "I always try to find an intimate way to get into the story to I have to create an intimate link" (S005, 2017). On Incendies the door was family: "To shoot something that you don't know is a very bad idea for a film director." (S104, 2011)
- **Sources:** 14 (S005, S039, S045, S060, S064, S065, S069, S075, S077, S079, S086, S087, S104, S107)

## 8. Anchor film and laboratory (anchor: his word in S026; laboratory: S064, S068)
- **What it is:** Before a big step into a system that could crush him, he made a small personal film that was entirely his (Enemy before Prisoners). It was insurance for his identity and a lab to learn a method he then carried into the big film.
- **Parts:** (1) Before a studio or high-stakes job, make one pure, small project you fully own. (2) Use it as a lab for one new skill (one actor, improvisation, working in English). (3) Build trust with the key collaborator there. (4) Carry the method into the big project. (5) If the big one goes wrong, the anchor still shows who you are.
- **When to use:** Before a first big commission, a platform deal, a first studio film, a new language or market.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if a small owned project tests the one new skill before the big step; 1 if the first test of the new skill is the big project itself. Inferred from S061, S068, S072.
- **Quote:** "So I did Enemy and I felt secure doing Prisoners because I knew that Enemy was there." (S068)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "the process of Enemy was a school, it was like a laboratory." (S068, 2015) "Like someone who's rock climbing, I had something solid that will help me not to fall down the cliff. 'Enemy' was that anchor." (S026, 2024)
- **Sources:** 12 (S019, S026, S027, S061, S064, S068, S071, S072, S074, S080, S086, S101)

## 9. The legacy test *(unnamed)*: entering someone else's universe
- **What it is:** His conditions for taking over someone else's world: the creator's blessing, a strong screenplay with existential depth, a project worthy of the pressure, and the freedom to make it his own. Then he accepts the comparison and works free.
- **Parts:** (1) Get the original creator's blessing face to face. (2) Demand a strong script with ideas; no film unless it is existential. (3) Check it is worthy and meaningful for you. (4) Accept the narrow odds and the certain comparison; that acceptance frees you. (5) Find your own visual identity inside the world (climate, light) while keeping what is singular (the original's sound). (6) Take control only if the owner steps back.
- **When to use:** Remakes, reboots, sequels to someone else's work, licensed IP, taking over a show.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if blessing, strong script, personal meaning and creative control are all in place; any one missing scores 2 or lower. Guidance from S045, S052, S069.
- **View change:** 2017: he took Blade Runner 2049 on these terms (S045, S051). 2024 (newer): he says he will never do it again (S019, S027).
- **Quote:** "I said if I do it one day, it will be for something that is really worthy and really meaningful artistically for me." (S045) Newer: "I don't think I will ever approach someone else's universe again." (S027)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** His conditions on 2049: the creator's blessing in person ("I wanted to be in front of him and looking into his eyes" S045), a strong script, and an artistically worthy project. In the work itself: "It doesn't mean that I was trying to copy, what it means is, I was trying to be in a relationship with the first one, the spirit of the first one." (S048, 2017)
- **Sources:** 14 (S005, S019, S027, S045, S048, S051, S052, S054, S055, S056, S069, S101, S107, S108)

## 10. The power of suggestion (his phrase, S046)
- **What it is:** Show less. What you choose not to show creates fear, tension and meaning. Violence and spectacle are suggested, not displayed.
- **Parts:** (1) Ask what you will not show (Ridley Scott's advice to him). (2) Keep the threat off screen as long as possible (the beast in Jaws). (3) Show violence from the victim's point of view, without glamour. (4) Reveal a creature or a secret layer by layer. (5) Do not explain your images afterwards.
- **When to use:** Violent, frightening, sexual or spectacular material; a trailer or campaign that wants to show everything.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the strongest moments work through what is withheld; 1 if the plan depends on showing the big moment early or explaining the meaning. Inferred from S017, S046, S059.
- **Quote:** "I wanted the audience to feel the impact, the threat and the ugliness of violence, but I don't want to make a show out of it. I prefer to go in the poetic suggestion." (S059)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "I absolutely love how it's more frightening to not seeing the beast than actually seeing it." (S105, 2024) Violence by evocation: "It's ugly poetry, yeah." (S061, 2015)
- **Sources:** 13 (S017, S045, S046, S048, S056, S058, S059, S060, S061, S070, S073, S075, S105)

## 11. Precise structure, free performance *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The story and the shots are planned in detail, but there is always a gap on set for actors and chance; he calls what enters there the chaos of life. The story is not improvised; the acting is.
- **Parts:** (1) Board and plan the whole film with the DP. (2) Keep the script precise. (3) On set: no marks on the floor, a camera that follows the actors, many takes (25 to 40) when needed. (4) Tear up the boards when a better idea arrives on the day. (5) Avoid previs so programmed that it kills life.
- **When to use:** Shooting plans, VFX-heavy work, how much to rehearse, a team that wants either total control or total improvisation.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the plan is complete and still names where freedom is allowed; 3 if fully planned with no room; 1 if the story itself is improvised. Inferred from S068, S072, S098.
- **View change:** 2017: he hated programmed previs and wanted to be free on set (S055). 2025 (newer): his boards become previs for the VFX team, and he wants precision up front, a lesson Deakins taught him on Blade Runner 2049 (S025).
- **Quote:** "The control work was about the storytelling; with the acting, I was trying to push the boundaries and explore new ways of directing." (S072)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "we love to prep, to draw everything, and then we're on set, we love to tear everything apart the storyboards and start from scratch, because we have a better idea on the day." (S048, 2017) His ideal since 2011: "I would rather work with a precise script and have time to improvise around it." (S104)
- **Sources:** 14 (S005, S018, S048, S049, S055, S068, S070, S072, S074, S096, S098, S104, S106, S107)

## 12. One bold choice of focus *(unnamed)*: what the film is really about
- **What it is:** To shape a big story, choose one spine (a point of view, one relationship, one group) and cut whatever does not serve it, including characters he loves.
- **Parts:** (1) Name the spine: Paul and Jessica, the Bene Gesserit, Paul and Chani; for Incendies, family, not war. (2) Build the structure on that relationship, in big blocks rather than scattered moments. (3) Pick a secret-weapon character whose view changes how the audience reads the lead (Chani shows Paul is not a hero). (4) Cut subplots and darlings that do not serve it (Thufir Hawat, the banquet scene). (5) Keep the story simple even when the telling is complex.
- **When to use:** The seat's core question. Any pitch, adaptation, rewrite or edit where the story sprawls.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the team can say in one sentence what the film is about and which relationship carries it, and can list the cuts; 3 if the theme is clear but the structure is not built on it; 1 if the answer is a plot summary. Inferred from S013, S030, S037, S075.
- **Quote:** "I had to make the bold choice to make a Bene Gesserit adaptation, and to focus the movie on that sisterhood." (S013)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** On Dune: "The first decision was to focus this adaptation on the Bene Gesserit sister power" (S105, 2024). In Part Two he moved the last act to Chani: "I wanted her to become a moral compass at the end, so the movie would be perceived as a cautionary tale" (S111, 2025).
- **Sources:** 13 (S001, S013, S015, S016, S020, S030, S037, S073, S075, S089, S105, S111, S118)

## 13. Pure image and sound *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Cinema is image and sound; dialogue belongs to theatre and television. Inner life is shown through faces, framing, music and sound, not voice-over.
- **Parts:** (1) Cut dialogue that is not needed; protect the writer's ideas but turn words into images. (2) Show thoughts without voice-over. (3) Cast actors who carry a scene with their eyes. (4) Edit without music first, so each scene holds its own tension; then add music the audience feels before they hear it. (5) Use sound to change how an image is read.
- **When to use:** Script reviews, dialogue-heavy drafts, narration proposals, deciding what a scene is for.
- **How to score (guidance):** His test is that a great film should work with the sound off (S017). 5 if the key scenes read with no lines; 3 if dialogue explains what the images already show; 1 if the story needs dialogue or narration to be understood.
- **Limit:** The film without dialogue is a stated dream (S001, S024, S026); he framed "I hate dialogue" as a perfect-world ideal while still co-writing his scripts (S023).
- **Quote:** "I remember movies because of their images, because of the ideas that unfold through images. That's the power of cinema." (S017)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "a great movie, you should be able to watch it without sound." (S105, 2024) Sound is planned from the script and fed to the edit early, because "for him, sound is as important as the image." (S106, 2024, on editor Joe Walker)
- **Sources:** 14 (S001, S017, S020, S023, S024, S026, S047, S059, S064, S090, S096, S105, S106, S120)

## 14. Design for the screen it plays on *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Format and release are creative decisions made at the start. He designs for the biggest, most immersive screen and wants every film's life to begin in theatres. The best defence of theatres is to make films that only reach their full potential there.
- **Parts:** (1) Choose the most immersive format for this film (IMAX for the vertigo of landscapes and the intimacy of faces). (2) Push further when you were too shy last time. (3) Write theatrical-first into contracts. (4) Let press and partners see it only on a big screen, with no viewing links. (5) Make the film that needs a theatre.
- **When to use:** Theatrical versus platform, format and budget, release plans, a day-and-date streaming offer.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if format and release are designed with the story from the start; 3 if the format is chosen late; 1 if a big-screen film is sold to a small screen at the end. Guidance from S042, S095, S098.
- **Method change:** 2021: he shot IMAX digital because film looked nostalgic (S095). 2026 (newer): he shot Part Three on 65mm and IMAX film, keeping digital IMAX for the brutality of the desert (S116). He is not against platforms: "Je n’ai rien contre les plateformes, bien au contraire." (S095)
- **Quote:** "we have to design movies that are immersive and powerful, that provide an experience you can’t have on an iPhone." (S047)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "So we designed the movie for IMAX right away" (S085, 2021). 2026: "I kept the desert in digital because I love the brutality of the digital IMAX." (S116)
- **Sources:** 12 (S011, S013, S020, S042, S047, S053, S085, S093, S095, S098, S107, S116)

## 15. Never a step too high (his words)
- **What it is:** He built his career like bricks or a staircase: each film adds one harder skill (writing, actors, control, VFX, world-building), and he refuses the big project until he has the technical means.
- **Parts:** (1) Name the new skill this project demands. (2) Check that you already have the rest. (3) If the step is too big, do something in between. (4) Turn down offers you are not ready for (he said no to big sci-fi sequels). (5) Take the dream project only when you can hold the wheel (Dune waited for Arrival and Blade Runner 2049).
- **When to use:** Scaling budget, a first big series, a first VFX film, a dream project offered too early.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the project needs one new skill on top of proven ones; 3 if it needs two; 1 if it needs several at once (budget, crew size, VFX, language). Inferred from S026, S061.
- **Inconsistency:** In 2021 he also says he has a pull toward projects out of reach: "I have some kind of a self-destructive device inside me to take projects that are out of my reach," (S038)
- **Quote:** "I knew people who had skipped steps and who found themselves in a position where they lost control over what they were doing" (S026)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "I believe in steps." (S061, 2015) "I needed to slowly build stairs, something solid under my feet." (S105, 2024) "All my filmography has been built like bricks." (S085, 2021)
- **Sources:** 11 (S021, S026, S038, S045, S061, S085, S088, S090, S099, S105, S108)

## 16. Key heads on day one *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The DP, designer, composer, editor and sound team join at the start and design the film with him, so no department applies its craft at the end.
- **Parts:** (1) Bring the DP in on day one and storyboard the whole film together (weeks in a Montreal hotel room with Deakins). (2) Set the world's rules with the DP and designer. (3) Have the composer read the script and send tracks before shooting. (4) Start sound design on day one of editing. (5) Keep trusted heads across films (editor Joe Walker, designer Patrice Vermette).
- **When to use:** Hiring order, prep budget, a production that plans to bring music or design in late.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the DP and designer shape the boards and the composer starts before the shoot; 1 if key heads arrive after the script is locked. Inferred from S046, S048, S055.
- **Method change:** 2015 (Sicario): he edited with no music and brought the composer in afterwards (S059). 2017 (newer): he brought the composer in before shooting, so music is not laid on top at the end (S055).
- **Quote:** "I brought Roger on board day one." (S046) Also: "I brought him very early on the project, even before I started to shoot." (S055)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** On 2049 Deakins came at the very start: "I needed a close partner from the start" (S005). On Dune the composer came first: "the score in Dune will be essential, will be absolutely crucial to the success of the movie to bring that kind of sacred quality that I wanted." (S106)
- **Sources:** 12 (S005, S040, S043, S046, S048, S050, S055, S059, S070, S100, S106, S110)

## 17. Flashlight in a dark room (his image, S059)
- **What it is:** Control what the audience knows. Tell the story through one character's limited view, so the audience finds the truth with them, and make the geography so clear that they are never lost and can feel only the tension.
- **Parts:** (1) Pick the point-of-view character, often an outsider like the audience. (2) Establish the geography once, wide, then stay inside the character's view. (3) Write action geography into the screenplay and fix it in the boards. (4) Stretch time; use stillness and silence for tension (Kurosawa). (5) Solve comprehension in the script, not in the edit.
- **When to use:** Thrillers, action, mysteries, any script where readers get lost or bored.
- **How to score (guidance):** His test is that his mother must know who is who and where (S014). 5 if a first-time viewer always knows where they are and whose eyes they share; 1 if tension depends on confusion.
- **Quote:** "The movie is really like you're holding a flashlight in a dark room. It's like you are discovering slowly the truth with the main character." (S059) Also: "If they are not lost, then they can only focus on the tension." (S014)
- **Sources:** 10 (S014, S026, S056, S058, S059, S061, S075, S079, S087, S091)

## 18. The pause *(unnamed)*: stop when the work is hollow
- **What it is:** After two early features he judged ego-driven, he stopped directing features for years, read, watched directors work with actors, lived, and came back only for something meaningful. He calls it the best artistic decision he ever made.
- **Parts:** (1) Notice when the work is driven by ego or by measuring yourself against others. (2) Admit that the core of the problem is you. (3) Stop and learn the missing tools (screenwriting, actors). (4) Come back only for something meaningful.
- **When to use:** A maker repeating hollow work, a studio drifting into formula, burnout.
- **How to score (guidance):** A stop rule, not a scale. Trigger it when the maker's own last two projects felt hollow to them. Inferred from S027, S092.
- **Quote:** "So that pause was probably the best and most important artistic decision I've ever made." (S027)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "So that pause was probably the best and most important artistic decision I've ever made." (S019, 2024) "I came back with more humility, and the will to be at the service of great stories." (S061, 2015)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S019, S026, S027, S061, S069, S077, S092)

## 19. Compromise on everything except the one thing *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Cinema at scale is an art of compromise, and constraint creates the ideas. But he names one non-negotiable and holds it whatever it costs (the real desert; no shortcuts on the sandworm ride).
- **Parts:** (1) Work with the forces that oppose you: a refused permit on the bridge became the helicopter shot. (2) Chase the weather instead of avoiding it. (3) Treat a small budget as a creative tool. (4) Name the one non-negotiable early. (5) Pay for it in time and pain; refuse the backlot.
- **When to use:** Budget cuts, schedule fights, a financier asking for savings.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if there is one named non-negotiable tied to the film's meaning and flexibility everywhere else; 1 if everything is non-negotiable, or nothing is. Inferred from S058, S091.
- **Quote:** "For me, cinema is about creating fiction in reality. And there are always forces that come to oppose you, to create pressure, and you have to work with those forces." (S058) And the limit: "Toutefois, il y a des choses sur lesquelles je ne fais aucune concession. J’ai imposé qu’on tourne dans le vrai désert." (S091)
- **Sources:** 9 (S013, S017, S026, S029, S030, S040, S058, S059, S091)

## 20. One project at a time, priced in years *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** He calls himself monomaniac: one project at a time, each priced in years of his life, with rest between giants.
- **Parts:** (1) Count the years before saying yes. (2) Do not scatter. (3) Recover physically after a giant shoot. (4) After a long run in one world, do something else to refresh the desire. (5) Do not shoot two giants back to back if it will leave you too tired.
- **When to use:** Taking on a second project, a slate plan, a team stretched across shows.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the lead creative has one main project and a rest plan; 1 if the key person is split across several big projects. Inferred from S027, S089, S099.
- **Exception:** In 2013 he alternated Enemy and Prisoners and found the switching gave him freshness and distance (S072); in 2024 he had four projects on the table (S019).
- **Quote:** "I'm a bit monomaniac. I can just work on one project at a time." (S089)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "I put all my eggs in the same basket all the time." (S107, 2017)
- **Sources:** 10 (S013, S014, S016, S020, S021, S027, S043, S089, S099, S107)

## 21. Faces and landscapes (his words, S012)
- **What it is:** His grammar of intimacy against scale: extreme close-ups of faces and vast landscapes, with every location chosen to express the character's inner state.
- **Parts:** (1) Shoot in extremes: faces close, bodies like ants in the distance. (2) Choose each location for the character's psychology. (3) Put characters in emptiness (the desert) to expose them. (4) Frame as if the camera cannot grab the whole world. (5) Link landscape to inner feeling and avoid postcard exoticism.
- **When to use:** Visual identity, location scouting, choosing a format.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if every location can be justified by a character's inner state; 1 if it was picked for beauty or convenience alone. Inferred from S026, S075.
- **Quote:** "Landscapes and human faces. The human face is a landscape itself." (S012) Also: "Each location has to be chosen in order to describe the inner psychology of the character, what the character is going through." (S026)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "Each location has to be chosen in order to describe the inner psychology of the character, what the character is going through." (S026, 2024) Learned from Pierre Perrault: "There's something about landscape. It's like human faces. It's like - according to the light." (S105, 2024)
- **Sources:** 11 (S011, S012, S020, S026, S065, S075, S085, S087, S096, S105, S122)

## 22. Use their machine without being swallowed (his words, S091)
- **What it is:** How he deals with studios and money: use the big system's resources to make his own films, state his terms at the start, and refuse offers that would make him a director for hire.
- **Parts:** (1) Put your terms on the table at the start. (2) Pitch the truth, not what pleases. (3) Refuse big offers that would cost your identity (Terminator, Star Trek). (4) Keep an anchor film (see 8). (5) Walk in with nothing to lose.
- **When to use:** Platform deals, financing, commissions, work-for-hire offers.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the deal states the creative terms up front and the maker would walk if refused; 1 if the maker hopes to win control later. Inferred from S019, S068.
- **View change:** 2014: he refused franchises to keep his identity (S091). 2024 (newer): the machine is still big, but he no longer has to protect his identity, because studios hire him to get a film of his: "Ça reste une grosse machine, mais je n’ai plus à protéger mon identité." (S091)
- **Quote:** "I put everything on the table, saying I’m ready to do this film if I can do it with my own sensibility." (S068)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** 2015: "I put everything on the table, saying I'm ready to do this film if I can do it with my own sensibility." (S068) And: "I'm not doing movies in Hollywood as a director for hire." (S068)
- **Sources:** 8 (S019, S026, S027, S061, S068, S069, S091, S120)

## 23. I love to commit (his words, S021)
- **What it is:** Make the hard visual and story choices before shooting and lock them in camera, so nothing waits for the grade, reshoots or a director's cut. Tell the financiers now.
- **Parts:** (1) Decide the palette in the screenplay. (2) Warn the studio early. (3) Shoot choices that cannot be undone (Giedi Prime in infrared, looks done in camera). (4) Be precise about VFX from the start. (5) Get all the ideas on paper, with a script that solves comprehension. (6) No reshoot culture, no director's cut.
- **When to use:** Prep sign-offs, a team that keeps options open, a financier who will want changes later.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the key look and story choices are locked and signed off before the money is spent; 1 if the plan relies on reshoots or post to find the film. Inferred from S021, S025, S091.
- **Quote:** "If you don't like it, tell me now, because after that, it's too late." (S021)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "I love to work with cinematographers that aren't afraid to burn the boat right at the start." (S111, 2025) On VFX: "It's not like you work with a keyboard and can change your mind all the time." (S025, 2025)
- **Sources:** 9 (S016, S021, S025, S029, S063, S072, S091, S095, S111)

## 24. World laws (his phrase, S046)
- **What it is:** For an invented world, write the rules first (society, geopolitics, climate, technology, physics) with the DP and designer, so everything on screen is believable and comments on today.
- **Parts:** (1) Write the laws. (2) Machines come from the environment and obey gravity. (3) Powers a scientist could almost explain; no magic. (4) Make the world an alternate version of today. (5) Design new things away from shapes already seen, from dreams, not internet browsing.
- **When to use:** Sci-fi, fantasy, period or any stylised world; a design team working from mood boards.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if a designer can answer "would this exist here?" from the written laws; 1 if the look is assembled from references. Inferred from S046, S053, S087.
- **Quote:** "We figure out what would be the laws, the sociological laws, the geopolitics, the climate laws, everything." (S046)
- **More evidence (2026 refresh):** "I wanted to make sure there would be an inner logic to all those languages." (S118, 2021) Each world designed from its ecology: "you're the product of your environment." (S108, 2021)
- **Sources:** 10 (S030, S040, S045, S046, S053, S056, S087, S108, S111, S118)

## 25. Key passages (his words, S092)
- **What it is:** A film turns on a few key scenes. He names them early, treats them as the film's test, and knows he has a film once he has captured them. Quiet, fragile scenes with actors scare him more than spectacle.
- **Parts:** (1) Name the scene that will decide the film (the sandworm ride). (2) Shoot the hardest intimate scene late, when trust with the actors is strongest. (3) Use an early scene that works as the measuring stick for the rest. (4) In the edit, look for the moment when the intended emotion appears.
- **When to use:** Script reviews, schedules, deciding where to spend money and rehearsal time.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the team can name the two or three scenes the film lives or dies on and has a plan for each; 1 if every scene is treated the same. Inferred from S009, S049, S092.
- **Quote:** "Si j’arrive à passer à travers ces scènes-là, si, en rentrant le soir, je sais que j’ai réussi à capturer certaines émotions, certaines idées, avec la caméra, alors j’ai un film." (S092) Also: "It involves sandworms that’s going to be one of the beautiful challenges of my life. And I know if I do it right, that will be the scene." (S009)
- **Sources:** 6 (S009, S044, S047, S049, S064, S092)
