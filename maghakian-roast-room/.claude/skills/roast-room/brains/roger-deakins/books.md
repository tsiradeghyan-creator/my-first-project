# Books: Roger Deakins

Built from what the interviews, forum replies and podcast say about his two books. Book text was not fetched and is not reproduced. Summaries are in my own words. He wrote no craft manual; his forum (2005 on, rebuilt after a hack lost the old posts, S074) and the Team Deakins podcast (2020 on, S010, S083) are his ongoing teaching.

## Byways: Photographs by Roger A. Deakins (2021, Damiani)

- **What it is:** Black and white still photographs taken from the late 1960s to the present, mostly the English seaside, rural North Devon, and places he passed while shooting films. He calls them sketches. "I think of them as just sketches that I’ve done over the years." (S040)
- **Core idea:** Simplicity, isolation and the oddness of ordinary life, found rather than staged. "It’s usually the little things that attract me. I’m much more interested in simplicity, isolation, [and] in sparse and lonely places." (S040) He shoots stills in black and white because "Black-and-white images evoke mood best." (S040)
- **Why it matters for this seat:** It shows the eye behind the films: few elements, strong composition, a dry sense of irony, and suspicion of color as decoration. He ties it to film directly: "I love it because it’s about the composition, and it’s about the juxtaposition of images within that frame. That does relate to the way I see photography in movies." (S063)
- **How it came about:** James and their agent found a publisher during the pandemic; most publishers wanted a cinematography book, Damiani took the photos as they were (S040, S024). Exhibitions and signings followed, including Still Light in Tulsa in 2026 (S083).
- **Taught in sources:** S040 (titles, the jumping dog, the bus stop, Homesick Ghosts), S063 (layout, the lightning photo, black and white as simplicity), S036 (first photo, 1969), S080 (irony and the absurd, his heroes Roger Mayne and Don McCullin), S083 (newer work, more satisfaction from stills), S070 (layout gutter, next book in larger format).

## Reflections: On Cinematography (US November 11, 2025, Grand Central; UK February 12, 2026)

- **What it is:** A memoir and a practical account of how he worked, written with James Ellis Deakins: Torquay childhood, art college, the National Film School, documentaries in Africa and a round the world yacht race, music videos, then the features, with his own lighting diagrams, sketches and frame grabs (S008, S023, S024, S081, S082).
- **Core thesis, in my words:** There is no single path into the craft and no formula inside it. Story, collaboration and lived experience decide the images; technique is only the means. Every film is a run of compromises with time, money, weather and locations, and the job is to choose which ones to accept. Inferred from S023, S081, S082, S083.
- **Key ideas it carries:**
  - Minimal lighting, motivated by real sources and designed with the art department; "Less is more." (S024)
  - Simple and cheap kit where it does the job: hardware store bulbs, gag lights, polystyrene and muslin bounces (S023, S091).
  - Plan thoroughly, then react on the day to actors and location (S081).
  - The honest record of failures and bad choices (S023).
  - An epilogue on film versus digital, AI, streaming and the narrowing of cinema (S081, S024).
- **What he fought for with the publisher:** The publisher wanted about five or seven famous films and set stories; he kept the documentaries and the early years. "The point was that your life is made up of a lot of different elements, and that’s what I wanted to sort of embrace, really." (S081) He wrote it himself after working with writer Keith Phipps: "The voice didn’t sound right unless I did it myself, you know?" (S023) He kept the price low so students could buy it (S023, S082).
- **Who it is for:** "In a way, I wrote it for my teenage self." (S082) It is not a technical manual (S070, S083) and not a tell-all (S081).
- **Taught in sources:** S008 (podcast episode on it), S023 and S024 (book tour), S070 (forum: what it contains), S081 (Far Out, structure and epilogue), S082 (Film Review Daily), S083 (Luxiere), S088 (Guardian quotes via No Film School).

## Unmade and unwritten

- He wrote scripts, including a dark First World War story set in Africa around 1990 that he still wants made (S031), and a science fiction script he thinks will never be financed (S080). He chose not to direct: "I think I'm better at doing what I do than I would be at directing really." (S031)
