# Positions: Roger Deakins

Built from the extract notes on his forum replies as Keymaster (2022 to 2026), interviews and American Cinematographer pieces (2009 to 2026) and Team Deakins podcast transcripts. 34 topics, business decisions in camera and image first. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id and date. Where his view moved, both versions sit side by side and the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Budget and where the money goes
- **Stance:** Work inside the budget, spend over plan only where the scene earns it, save elsewhere, and prefer the cheap tool that works. Big budgets do not change how he shoots; low budgets are no reason to refuse a good script.
- **Evidence:**
  - "You have to choose your battles." (S041, 2015-10) on overspending at the Sicario holding-area location and underspending on the raid set.
  - "using simple VFX work can allow money to go where it is really needed" (S071, 2024-07)
  - "You try and get the maximum out of the money you have." (S042, 2011-02)
  - "it was not a high budget and so we really were we didn't have any extra money at all so we had to do it one way" (S097, 2021-08) on Sicario.
  - On Fargo: "But in a way, that often helps you find your way of doing it, you know, less or less more approach, really." (S093, 2021-02)
  - On 1917, production wanted a cheaper backlot build in place of Glasgow; CG backgrounds made it dearer, so they went to Glasgow for a few days after rehearsing a full mock-up (S096, 2021-04).
  - "I said, 'Well, you must be mad. Why wouldn't I want to do it?'" (S029, 2023-03) on Fargo's low budget.
  - "I think sometimes it feels a bit obscene, how much money is spent on a film." (S023, 2026-03)
  - Concrete savings: factory plastic for diffusion (S091, 2020-10); a custom filter instead of gelling hundreds of lamps on Blade Runner 2049: "To gel thos lamps would have been expensive" (S073, 2023-02); a small LED floor package on Empire of Light "for cost reasons" (S073, 2023-02).
- **Read:** he never prices a film himself, but he always knows the cheaper version of his ask. Inferred from S041, S073, S091.

## Multiple cameras and coverage
- **Stance:** One camera by default; extra cameras only for unscripted moments, intense performances or costly one-off action. He refuses fixed multi-unit setups.
- **Evidence:**
  - "I prefer shooting with a single camera simply because I don’t like shooting generic ‘coverage’." (S052, 2015-01)
  - "For that reason I don’t shoot projects where I know there will be a regular use of multiple cameras." (S052, 2015-01)
  - "I said, what four or five? No!" (S032, 2020-04) on line producers asking for multiple camera crews on Blade Runner 2049.
  - "So, I am not against multi camera just don’t think it helps for every scene." (S072, 2023-12)
  - "I don’t like using multiple cameras other than for specific circumstances, so that is always a topic for discussion." (S070, 2025-10)

## Television pace and coverage
- **Stance:** He has shot TV and enjoyed the pressure, but sees TV coverage habits (endless singles, no wides) as edit convenience, not need.
- **Evidence:**
  - "I did a TV series in the late 1980s and we shot 6 pages a day. I felt rushed but I enjoyed the challenge" (S002, 2026-11)
  - "I think the close up and singles are used because it is easier to make changes in the edit and continuity is less of a problem." (S070, 2026-06)
  - TV series can go deeper into character: "Some of the TV series have been brilliant. They can delve into the characters a bit more than you can in two hours." (S066, 2020-02)
  - Wide shots rare on TV, perhaps a "go to" setting (S072, 2024-03).
- **Read:** on a fast series he would protect a few constructed scenes and accept simpler coverage elsewhere, but he has not said this directly. Inferred from S002, S052, S070.

## Crew size and hiring
- **Stance:** Small crew, the same trusted people for decades, hire on the spot when someone is good.
- **Evidence:**
  - "I like to operate myself and I also like to work with as small a crew as possible." (S052, 2015-01)
  - "when you find somebody you get on with very well, and [you] appreciate each other, really just try and stick with them" (S023, 2026-03)
  - "But when you’re not working so much, it’s hard to keep a crew together." (S023, 2026-03)
  - Hired the Trinity operator after a demo at ARRI London (S077, 2020-01).

## Second units
- **Stance:** Avoid them; allow only with precise boards (Skyfall Turkey, Skyfall opening previs). An early film with a rogue second unit (Air America) shaped which productions he accepts (S098, 2026-10, host's account of his book).
- **Also:** "They give you everything else, but not the one you really need or want." (S098, 2026-10)
- **Evidence:** "We did do a pre-vis for the opening of ‘Skyfall’ as we were working with a second unit and wanted to be very specific as to what we wanted." (S073, 2023-02); writer's summary in S032 (2020-04).

## Prep time
- **Stance:** More prep is time well spent; it saves shoot time. Depth depends on the film.
- **Evidence:** "All that time you spend in prep saves time when you’re shooting." (S057, 2015-10); "Usually I like a lot of prep." (S079, 2011-02); "It varies from film to film." (S073, 2023-02)

## Storyboards and previs
- **Stance:** Boards yes, as a template and to know the kit; extensive previs rarely; never a "go-to shot list".
- **Evidence:** "I have worked with storyboards but never with any extensive pre-vis" (S073, 2023-02); "A storyboard should only be the suggestion of an idea and that extra effort should go to shooting." (S073, 2023-04); "I don’t like that style of stiff, methodical planning." (S043, 2014-11)

## Schedule, pages and speed
- **Stance:** Compromise between time and ambition is part of the job; work speed comes from prep; he fights to keep scenes production wants to drop by showing how to shoot them faster.
- **Evidence:** "you do have to compromise between the time you are allowed and the ambition of a shot, or shots." (S002, 2026-11); "Well, I think it's about having a lot of prep and knowing what you're doing." (S091, 2020-10); one Hudsucker setup took four hours (S042, 2011-02).

## Weather and waiting for light
- **Also:** "I think that was actually the most stressful aspect to the whole shoot" (S096, 2021-04) on 1917's cloud, and "You think you'd have flexibility, but we did not know." (S098, 2026-10) on big budgets.
- **Stance:** Waiting for the right light is worth it when the scene depends on it, but the pressure lands on him; compromise only at the last moment.
- **Evidence:** "But, yeah, everyone’s looking at me." (S036, 2022-12); "The hardest part of the shoot was the waiting for cloudy conditions." (S073, 2023-03); "but you don't want to do it until the last possible moment." (S091, 2020-10)

## VFX, CGI and greenscreen
- **Stance:** In camera first; VFX used sparingly can expand a story and save money; overuse drives films to their detriment and makes audiences doubt the real.
- **Evidence:** "where it is used sparingly VFX effects can expand a story." (S071, 2024-07); "Though I feel it often drives the visuals of a film, to a film’s detriment" (S071, 2024-07); "Fix things in post. It's one of the worst expressions to come into the industry." (S054, 2009-10); "But if it's used correctly, it's a fantastic technique." (S054, 2009-10); "There was talk of making this backing greenscreen, but I thought that was kind of ridiculous." (S075, 2018-03)

## Virtual production and LED volumes
- **Stance:** No interest; it forces shallow focus and fake backgrounds. Consistent from 2021 to 2026.
- **Evidence:** "The whole effects world, the use of fake backgrounds or LED screens as backgrounds ... great, but I’ve got no interest in that whatsoever. Absolutely none." (S010, 2021-04); "It may be that lighting becomes entirely ‘controlled’ through an LED wall or some other ‘environmental’ source but that is not something that I see myself involved with." (S073, 2023-05); "to me, that is a valid reason to not shoot in a volume." (S002, 2026-11, newer); refused a months-long greenscreen-stage film (S081, 2026-02).

## AI
- **Stance:** Unsure and wary; open to it making filmmaking more accessible; insists you still need a story.
- **Evidence:** 2024-11: "I really don’t know what to think of the use of AI in filmmaking but change is a given." (S071) and "And you still need something to say" (S071). 2025-05: "my still photos will always be me." (S070). Newer, 2026-02: "Everybody’s kind of paranoid about AI, but like a lot of things, maybe it’s going to open up other avenues for opportunities for people to make films." (S024) and "I think the question’s still moot" (S081).

## Film versus digital
- **Stance over time:** 2011, first digital film: "it's the first camera I've worked with that I've felt gives me something I can't get on film." (S042). Late 2011: "with the Alexa I believe digital has finally surpassed film in terms of quality." (S055). 2015: "I don’t have any real affiliation either way." (S045). 2016, after Hail, Caesar!: "As I say, just the technical problems with film, I’m sorry, it’s over." (S047). Newer, 2023: "I do, but I don't," (S037) on missing film, "But I do miss the simplicity of film" (S037). 2026: film will survive as a niche, "It’s going to survive, but in that sort of sense of it being, yeah, a niche market really." (S081). Constant: "Whether film or digital the resulting look depends on the eye behind the work." (S074, 2023-01)

## Grading, LUTs and the DI
- **Stance:** One LUT for years; the look made on set; the DI finesses. O Brother was the exception.
- **Evidence:** "Personally, I would not use more than one LUT on a film." (S070, 2026-06); "‘O Brother’ was the first time I used the DI. At the time I vowed it would be my last but …. That didn’t last long." (S071, 2024-11); "I like to get as close as I can to the look on set" (S041, 2015-10)

## Monitors and video village
- **Stance:** A calibrated monitor helps talk with the director, but trust the eye; he misses the director beside the camera.
- **Evidence:** "I think that's a real advantage to the communication and the collaboration between me and a director." (S031, 2019-09); "A monitor is just a guide, an aid to the discussion between a cinematographer and director." (S071, 2024-11); "Now, the directors are hardly ever by the camera. They’re usually in a tent somewhere." (S034, 2023-01)

## Lighting kit and LEDs
- **Stance:** LEDs fine and useful (no gels, dimming without color shift), not a new default; big HMIs still for moonlight; old lamps have a richer quality.
- **Evidence:** "To dispense with gels is a great advantage!" (S072, 2023-10); "I wouldn’t say this use of LEDs is now my preferred way to light but it certainly suited the conditions on this particular project." (S074, 2023-01); "there’s something kind of wonderful about old fashioned lights, the quality of light they give is different to an LED." (S080, 2021-12)

## Formats, lenses, aspect ratio
- **Stance:** Primes, spherical, clean modern glass; large format since 1917; not a fan of anamorphic, zooms or vintage lenses; aspect ratio decided early with the director, by instinct.
- **Evidence:** "I am not a fan of anamorphic." (S074, 2023-01); "I have always used primes," (S017, c.2019); "I’m not mad about zoom." (S057, 2015-10); "I always though aspect ratio was a creative tool and not a marketing tool. How naive I was." (S070, 2026-06)

## Shallow focus and depth
- **Stance:** Dislikes shallow focus; wants to feel the space around a character.
- **Evidence:** "Personally, I dislike shallow focus and not only in my still photography." (S002, 2026-11); "I am trying for a deeper stop and more depth of field." (S071, 2024-12)

## Color
- **Stance:** Color must be justified by story or world; often it is eye candy.
- **Evidence:** "The color is always justified in some way. I certainly don’t use color to ‘look good’." (S072, 2024-01); "I think color is often just ‘eye candy’ and both a distraction and an excuse for a lack of something else." (S073, 2023-04)

## Drones, 3D, VR
- **Stance:** Drones have a place but are overused; refuses to shoot 3D; VR not for him.
- **Evidence:** "And you go: ‘Oh no, another one.’" (S025, 2026-02) on drone wides in TV; "No, I won’t. I’ve been offered it. I just don’t want to." (S057, 2015-10) on 3D; "It's just horses for courses, you know?" (S042, 2011-02)

## Restoration, HDR and remasters
- **Stance:** Supports remasters that restore original intent; objects to regrades that "improve" a film.
- **Evidence:** "I try to get as close to the original intent of a film as possible." (S070, 2026-01); "does anyone think they can regrade the film and make it look ‘better’?" (S072, 2024-02)

## Working with directors
- **Stance:** Serve the director's film; speak up with alternatives; pick directors whose way of working fits; part ways early if you see different films.
- **Evidence:** "My attitude is that we are here for the Director." (S089, 2020-05); "Never, and I mean never, be critical of a director’s choice without offering an alternative you might prefer." (S070, 2025-10); "There’s a certain way I like to work, and so, there are certain directors I would never get on with." (S081, 2026-02); "The most annoying thing I find with a director is they sort of pontificate, they’re just making it up," (S062, 2026-02)

## Studios and studio notes
- **Stance:** Credits studios that hold their nerve; regrets self-censoring after studio notes.
- **Evidence:** "So a lot of credit to the studio for not freaking out because it was a risky" (S096, 2021-04); after the Jesse James studio asked them to stop the lens effect, "we sort of censored ourselves a bit and we both regret it at the end" (S095, 2024-10).

## Working with production and producers
- **Stance:** Production defaults to habits from the last big show; explain needs early through a trusted go-between; producers who offer "anything you want" stifle you.
- **Evidence:** "It’s always like that with production." (S032, 2020-04); "Producers and all that nonsense." (S024, 2026-02); Rafelson's warning (S081, 2026-02); James handles budget negotiations during the shoot (S091, 2020-10).

## Actors
- **Stance:** Performance first; give actors space and trust; intolerant of vanity demands about lenses or angles.
- **Evidence:** "I think a big part of the cinematographer's job is creating a space where the actor feels comfortable to do their work." (S079, 2011-02); "The downside is when an actor demands a certain style of lighting or to be shot on a specific lens." (S071, 2024-11); "I'm afraid I'm not very tolerant of that sort of thing!" (S079, 2011-02)

## Choosing projects
- **Stance:** Script first, then director's passion and a challenge; passion or nothing.
- **Evidence:** "it’s the script that’s paramount to me." (S043, 2014-11); "I’ve never taken a film unless I felt it’s been some sort of challenge, and felt that the director is very passionate about the project, and obviously, a story that speaks to me." (S081, 2026-02)

## Franchises, superheroes and the state of cinema
- **Stance:** Bored by franchise and action spectacle; studios take few risks; the mid-budget drama is being squeezed, by streaming more than by tech.
- **Evidence:** "There are too many action, superhero movies, which I don't really like." (S066, 2020-02); "I think it’s changed beyond repair, really," (S037, 2023-01); "Well, the biggest thing now is streaming." (S024, 2026-02); "I agree that that most contemporary big budget movies are quite boring." (S073, 2023-03)

## Independent and low-budget filmmaking
- **Stance:** Nostalgic for passion projects made by small groups; low budgets sharpen work; technology is no barrier for a filmmaker with vision.
- **Evidence:** "I’m a bit nostalgic for those kinds of independent movies, where a group of people get together because they love the project." (S023, 2026-03); "Limitations can be overcome if the film maker has a vision and a passion." (S073, 2023-03)

## Films about real issues
- **Stance:** Wants films that deal with the issues of the day; values cinema that shows another life.
- **Evidence:** "films are not dealing with issues of the day." (S024, 2026-02); documentary empathy, S046 (2017-10).

## Awards
- **Stance:** Nice, not the point.
- **Evidence:** "Well, I never lost, in my opinion, because it's a film being recognized. It's such nonsense." (S031, 2019-09); "The film is still the film. It doesn't make any difference." (S079, 2011-02)

## Directing
- **Stance:** Considered it, chose not to; loves the set and the crew.
- **Evidence:** "I think I'm better at doing what I do than I would be at directing really." (S031, 2019-09); "I have definitely found my niche." (S089, 2020-05)

## Stress, nerves and work hours
- **Stance:** The job is and should be stressful; nerves show you care; experience does not lower it.
- **Evidence:** "And it doesn’t get any less stressful the more experience I get, which is strange, really." (S036, 2022-12); "The job is stressful and it must be stressful." (S074, 2022-10); "In my opinion being a cinematographer requires a high level of fitness." (S073, 2023-02)

## Film school and training
- **Stance:** Not a must; life experience matters more; he was rejected the first time.
- **Evidence:** "Film School is not a must and certainly not the best course for everyone." (S074, 2023-01); "life experience far outweighs any technical training." (S043, 2014-11)

## Retirement and current work
- **Stance (2026):** No feature since Empire of Light (2022); would return for the right challenge, possibly science fiction; stills now satisfy him most.
- **Evidence:** "I’ve had such a good run, I don’t feel the pressure to carry on." (S081, 2026-02); "I’ve got to say, I get more satisfaction from my still photographs," (S083, 2026-05)

## Topics with no recorded position
- Pricing a DP's own fee, equity or profit share, raising money, debt, marketing spend, paid ads, remote work beyond COVID protocols: no recorded position. He leaves money to production and James (S091).
