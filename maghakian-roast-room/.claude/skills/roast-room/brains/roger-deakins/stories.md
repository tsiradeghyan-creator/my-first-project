# Stories: Roger Deakins

Built from the extract notes on his forum replies, interviews and Team Deakins podcast transcripts. 20 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws, ordered roughly by how many sources tell them. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **1917: the one shot that had to wait for cloud.** "This is envisioned as a single shot" was written on the script and he first feared a gimmick (S077). Nothing could be designed until they knew where the camera would be; they rehearsed with stand-ins and a point-and-shoot camera to size the trenches, and by day one had "a master plan of every shot and every item of equipment" (S077). Day exteriors used no added light, so every take waited for cloud; the first day they did not shoot at all (S073), and Mendes told him the weather was not his responsibility while everyone looked at him (S036, S091). Lesson: radical form demands total prep, and the DP carries the weather risk (S016, S024, S036, S039, S073, S074, S077, S089, S091).

2. **Shawshank: lit everything, and nobody went.** It was not a big budget; every interior had to be lit though people thought it looked like a documentary (S026, S029). His gaffer gathered 45 12K HMIs from across the country, and he saved money with cheap factory plastic instead of film diffusion (S091). The film bombed in cinemas twice and found its audience on video: "That was a disaster" (S029). Lesson: looking simple costs effort, and box office is not the verdict (S011, S026, S029, S071, S091).

3. **Sicario: the parking-lot border crossing.** The real Bridge of the Americas could not be used; Patrice Vermette proposed a minimal build in an Albuquerque parking lot with a fence that removed most VFX, and the money saved paid for the convoy in Mexico City (S071, S045, S073). Lesson: a script-driven minimal build beats spectacle, and saved money should go on screen (S041, S045, S071, S073).

4. **Sicario: choose your battles.** He overspent on the fertilizer-plant holding area (45 Blondes in fabricated fixtures, two nights, 15 to 20 shots a night) and underspent on the stage raid set, where he used false perspective because a full match would have cost too much (S041). Lesson: spend where the scene carries the film (S041).

5. **Blade Runner 2049: one camera when production wanted nine.** The line producer expected up to nine camera units; they shot single camera, as on Skyfall (S032, S091). He reused one rig of 250 space lights for two sets and argued for a painted backing over greenscreen (S075). Lesson: big films can be shot like small ones if the plan is precise (S032, S075, S091, S073).

6. **No Country for Old Men: "I'll never talk to you again".** When the Coens said they might not direct, he told them he'd never speak to them again if they didn't (S028, S029, S042). They shot almost nothing that was not used (S070). Lesson: back the material you believe in, and plan so nothing is wasted (S028, S029, S042, S070, S079).

7. **No Country: the dawn river patchwork.** He studied the river locations at dawn and dusk for days and built a minute-by-minute timetable of shots with a second rig ready, shooting each piece in backlight (S057, S092). Lesson: a light window can be scheduled like an actor (S057, S092).

8. **The Coens and coverage.** Boarded overs and singles were often never shot because the wide played; on Barton Fink only five shots went unused (S092, S070, S057). Lesson: confidence in the scene saves days (S057, S070, S092).

9. **Empire of Light: build on the empty lot.** No seaside cinema had the lobby Mendes wrote; an empty lot three doors from the Dreamland in Margate let them build it with the real view outside (S033, S034, S037, S039). The small film was harder than 1917: "you’ve never got enough money and time" (S033). Lesson: build on location to keep the real world, and never assume a small film is easy (S033, S034, S037, S039, S072).

10. **True Grit's opening: the simpler alternative.** Waiting for dawn to finish the planned silhouette, he suggested a side-on track as a spare; that single take opened the film (S042). Lesson: the simplest telling usually wins (S042).

11. **Skyfall: "Where's the light coming from?"** He pushed lion statues out and lit dragon heads in for the Macau casino, and gave the glass prison a built-in ceiling light because a spotlight would be ridiculous (S056). The burning house was the only source, "a bit of a stretch" in the chapel (S056). Lesson: motivated sources should be designed, not added (S056, S013).

12. **Barton Fink revived him.** After an unsatisfying big-budget film he was moving back to Devon when the Coens called; five weeks of storyboarding together showed him a new way to work (S043, S031, S029). Lesson: the right collaborators matter more than budget (S029, S031, S043).

13. **The worst advice.** A powerful producer told him leaving film school that he'd never make it and should get a job as a PA at the BBC in Plymouth; six years later he shot a film that producer made (S057, S051, S067). Lesson: ignore gatekeepers (S051, S057, S067).

14. **Rejected for not being "filmic".** The National Film School turned him down in 1971; he spent a year photographing rural North Devon and got in the next year (S026, S043, S081). Lesson: life experience is training (S026, S043, S063, S081).

15. **1984: finding his place.** Sitting with John Hurt and Richard Burton he realized he was a cinematographer; Burton admitted being nervous on day one (S037, S080). The film was meant to be black and white; with no finance for that, they spent months on bleach bypass (S029). Lesson: nerves mean you care, and when money forbids the look, engineer the nearest one (S029, S037, S039, S080, S082).

16. **Mountains of the Moon at Lake Turkana.** Seven shots on three tracks for one sunset; Rafelson asked to start early, he held, and got the last shot as the sun touched the horizon (S091). Rafelson later warned him that "anything you want" stifles imagination (S081). Lesson: hold your nerve for the light, and value constraint (S081, S091).

17. **Prisoners and Unbroken: the weather gamble.** On Prisoners the road was closed for only two weekends and they needed cloud; it rained on day one (S074). On Sicario a storm closed the set and he drove out to photograph lightning hitting a bar (S083, S063). Lesson: build flexibility, then use whatever the sky gives (S053, S063, S074, S083).

18. **To the White Sea, unmade.** A Coen project scouted in Japan that never got made: "That was probably the biggest disappointment in my work life ... not getting that film done." (S080) Lesson: even the best plans fail to get financed (S080, S092).

19. **The old forum, hacked.** Years of answers were lost when the site was hacked; he defended James's unpaid work running the site and podcast (S074). Lesson: credit the people who do the hidden work (S074, S010).

20. **The War Game in a Torquay film club.** Two elderly ladies fainted at Peter Watkins's film; he still calls it more terrifying than any apocalyptic movie since, though technically rough (S034, S062, S043). Lesson: truth beats polish (S034, S043, S062).
