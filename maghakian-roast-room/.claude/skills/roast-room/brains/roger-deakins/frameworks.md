# Frameworks: Roger Deakins

Built from the extract notes on his forum replies (2022 to 2026), interviews and American Cinematographer pieces (2009 to 2026) and Team Deakins podcast transcripts. 23 frameworks, roughly most-used first. He names almost none of them; short names are mine and marked *(unnamed)*. "Sources" counts distinct sources where the idea is used. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council, built from his stated habits and thresholds, not quotes.

---

## 1. Story first: the cinematography that is right for the film *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Every visual choice is judged by whether it serves this story, these characters and this director's reading. There is no house style; the film demands its own look.
- **Parts:** Read the script for mood; learn the director's ambition; let the look grow out of both; reject any image made for its own sake.
- **When to use:** First question on any visual decision, any format debate, any "make it cinematic" note.
- **How to score:** 5 if each major visual choice can be traced to a story beat or character; 3 if the look is coherent but generic; 1 if the pitch leads with references, gear or "beautiful shots".
- **Quote:** "there is good cinematography and bad cinematography and there is the cinematography that is right for the film." (S074)
- **Sources:** 14 (S026, S042, S043, S044, S045, S048, S062, S070, S071, S073, S074, S075, S083, S088)

## 2. Invisible cinematography *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The camera, light and technique stay out of the audience's awareness so they stay inside the story. A noticed shot is a failed shot.
- **Parts:** No ostentatious moves or showy lighting; simple compositions; the trick (even a one-shot film) must not draw attention.
- **When to use:** Reviewing a shot list, a reel, a trailer-driven "hero shot", a drone wide, a flashy transition.
- **How to score:** 5 if a viewer would describe the scene, not the shot; 1 if the selling point is the shot itself.
- **Quote:** "If you're drawing attention to the surface of the movie then the viewer is being drawn out of the actual story." (S066)
- **Sources:** 11 (S025, S026, S039, S041, S044, S054, S062, S066, S077, S080, S081)

## 3. Plan hard, find it on the day *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** His prep pipeline. Script with the director to learn their ambition; schedule with the first AD; lighting diagrams for every set with gaffer and grip, sent to everyone; then stay open to actors and location on the day.
- **Parts:** (1) script talks, often cutting dialogue for visuals; (2) scout together; (3) schedule with the AD after boards or shot lists; (4) diagrams to gaffer; (5) rehearse, look, adjust; (6) shoot.
- **When to use:** Any production plan, any "we'll figure it out on set" or, the reverse, any over-locked previs plan.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan names the shots that need lead time and kit, and leaves blocking to rehearsal; 3 if planned but rigid; 1 if no plan or a plan that cannot bend.
- **Quote:** "I end up with a book, or now on a computer, I end up with a whole list of diagrams that we send to everybody and say, this is the plan. But then maybe you get to the day you want to change it a bit." (S031)
- **Sources:** 18 (S001, S002, S031, S034, S043, S045, S054, S056, S057, S066, S073, S079, S081, S089, S092, S093, S096, S098)

## 4. Prep depth matches the film *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** How much to board depends on complexity. Built worlds and VFX-heavy or one-shot films are designed months ahead; actor pieces are found on the day.
- **Parts:** Empire of Light, shots worked out on the day; 1917, the shot decided months before; Blade Runner 2049, much of it boarded and some previs; Sicario, only key scenes; Coens, everything boarded then refined together.
- **When to use:** Deciding the prep budget and time, whether to hire a storyboard artist, whether to previs.
- **How to score:** Good when complex, costly or second-unit sequences are boarded and character scenes are not; poor when everything or nothing is.
- **Quote:** "It varies from film to film." (S073)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S031, S054, S057, S073, S075, S077, S092)

## 5. Storyboard as template, not law *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Boards teach everyone what matters in each scene and what kit is needed; the shot itself may change, often to fewer, longer takes.
- **Parts:** Board to learn the script; change boards where the location disagrees; on the day, play it in one shot if it works.
- **When to use:** Reviewing previs costs, defending a plan against a director who wants to improvise or a producer who wants it locked.
- **How to score:** 5 if boards define purpose and equipment; 1 if boards are copied mechanically or replaced by expensive previs for drama.
- **Quote:** "A storyboard is a way of learning the script and learning what is important in each scene, so everyone is clear what the reason for that scene is." (S089)
- **Sources:** 7 (S043, S045, S054, S073, S089, S092, S091)

## 6. Where is the light coming from? (motivated light)
- **What it is:** Light should come from a source that could exist in the world of the scene, even when the world is stylised. Coherence matters more than literal naturalism.
- **Parts:** Find the motivating source (window, practical, fire, moon, headlights); push the level, never the logic; one source when you can, even if it stretches belief.
- **When to use:** Any lighting plan, any set without a source, any "add a spotlight" fix.
- **How to score:** 5 if every key light has an in-world reason; 1 if light appears from nowhere to "make it pop".
- **Quote:** "Like for any scene you need to consider what you want it to look like and what would motivate the light." (S072)
- **Sources:** 9 (S012, S033, S056, S072, S073, S075, S079, S092, S041)

## 7. Sets that light themselves *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Design the lighting into the set with the production designer and set dresser: windows placed for light, practicals chosen as sources, room above and around for rigs, hard ceilings where possible.
- **Parts:** Early meetings with design; choose practicals from dozens of options; dimmer systems on built-in units; insist on windows and lighting space.
- **When to use:** Set builds, location choices, budget talks between art and camera departments.
- **How to score:** 5 if the set plan shows where light comes from and where rigs live; 1 if the set is built wall to wall with no windows.
- **Quote:** "You spend a lot of time playing with ideas, sketching things out, and working with the art department to figure out how the set will kind of light itself." (S039)
- **Sources:** 9 (S033, S039, S041, S070, S072, S079, S087, S091, S073)

## 8. Light the space, not the shot *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Light the whole scene for the blocking, set for the wide first, then tweak or soften only for closer coverage. Faster and more believable than relighting every angle.
- **Parts:** Pre-rig from outside the set (windows as cutters); wide first; move bounce closer for the close-up; small tweaks between takes.
- **When to use:** Schedules with many setups per day; arguments about lighting time.
- **How to score:** 5 if one lighting state covers most angles; 1 if every setup is relit.
- **Quote:** "always thought about a location or a set, you know, where a scene plays and thought about what what light do I want to light the scene, not specific shots." (S091)
- **Sources:** 5 (S041, S071, S091, S073, S079)

## 9. The camera moves for a reason (the Bresson test)
- **What it is:** No move without a motive in the scene; the motive may be emotional. A move needs a start, a middle and an end. Holding a shot can build more tension than cutting.
- **Parts:** Ask why it moves; if no reason, lock it off; prefer a held frame to a cut when tension builds in the frame.
- **When to use:** Shot lists heavy on gimbals, cranes, drones; a director who wants "energy".
- **How to score:** 5 if each move has a stated motive; 1 if movement is the default.
- **Quote:** "If the camera moves it’s got to be for a reason. And I will say: watch a Bresson! He doesn’t move the camera because he can’t find a reason to do it, so why should we?" (S053)
- **Sources:** 7 (S052, S053, S073, S074, S091, S041, S070)

## 10. One camera, constructed shots *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Shoot one angle at a time, each built with a purpose, rather than covering a scene with several cameras for the editor. Extra cameras compromise light, lens and composition for all.
- **Parts:** Single camera as default; a second for unscripted or intense performance, or a costly one-off action; bring special kit only on the day it's used.
- **When to use:** Budget talks about camera units, TV coverage habits, producers asking for "A, B, C cameras".
- **How to score:** 5 if extra cameras are justified scene by scene; 1 if a fixed multi-camera setup is the plan for drama.
- **Quote:** "It is an obvious truth that a second camera will compromise the shot that can be made by the first camera. A third camera will only compromise all three angles, both in terms of lighting and in terms of composition and lens choice." (S052)
- **Sources:** 11 (S029, S032, S052, S053, S057, S070, S072, S082, S091, S092, S098)

## 11. Make the look on set (one LUT, grade only finesses) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Decide color, contrast and exposure in camera and light. One LUT for the whole film (the same for years). The grade fixes, it does not invent.
- **Parts:** Color temperature on set; calibrated monitor to show the director; dailies even; little timing in the DI.
- **When to use:** "We'll fix it in post", heavy grading budgets, look-building in the DI.
- **How to score:** 5 if the look exists in dailies; 1 if the plan relies on finding the look later.
- **Quote:** "I don’t believe an image or a look is created with a LUT. But i come from film and believe in shooting an image the way you want it to appear on the screen." (S071)
- **Sources:** 10 (S014, S041, S054, S070, S071, S072, S073, S074, S075, S077)

## 12. Choose your battles (money follows the scene) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Spend over plan where the scene earns it and claw it back elsewhere; use cheap VFX to move money to what matters on screen.
- **Parts:** Name the scenes that carry the film; underspend on stage sets and simple scenes; overspend on the key location or event; trade a costly practical for a modest effect if it frees money.
- **When to use:** Any budget cut or reallocation in camera, lighting, art or VFX.
- **How to score:** 5 if the spend tracks the story's key moments; 1 if cuts are spread evenly across everything.
- **Quote:** "This is where we spent more money than the production thought we were going to spend, but I think it paid off. Then, in other places, like the stage set for the raid, we spent less than expected. You have to choose your battles." (S041)
- **Also:** "so you when you're going through a script and in pre-production you find that balance between what you you know what you can afford and what your wishes are you know that's when it comes down to reality right" (S097)
- **Sources:** 9 (S041, S045, S071, S073, S075, S091, S094, S096, S097)

## 13. Simplest and cheapest *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Use the least equipment and the plainest tool that gets the image: hardware-store bulbs, gag lights, foil, muslin, polystyrene, factory plastic instead of film diffusion.
- **Parts:** Small floor package; only the units you need that day; reuse one rig for two sets.
- **When to use:** Lighting and grip budgets, low-budget shoots, anyone asking for "the full package".
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan reaches the look with less kit; 1 if kit is ordered by habit.
- **Quote:** "You don’t have to have film lights. It doesn’t have to be some great, expensive setup with lights specifically made for making movies." (S023)
- **Sources:** 8 (S023, S024, S031, S042, S073, S075, S091, S074)

## 14. Weather and sun as schedule inputs *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Read the forecast, break the boards down by sun angle and time of day, build flexibility into the schedule, and wait for the right light when the scene needs it.
- **Parts:** Sun studies at locations; time each shot to a light window; cover yourself on early shoots for later locations; embrace weather you get.
- **When to use:** Exterior-heavy schedules, dawn and dusk scenes, matching across locations.
- **How to score:** 5 if exterior shots are scheduled by light with a weather fallback; 1 if exteriors are scheduled only by actor availability.
- **Quote:** "I suggest you break down the camera angles and shoot each based on the angle of the sun" (S070)
- **Also:** "so it was like a patchwork of shots we did and i said it was all timed very specifically so i could do basically everything in backlight" (S092)
- **Sources:** 14 (S036, S041, S045, S057, S070, S072, S073, S077, S091, S092, S053, S093, S096, S098)

## 15. In camera first; effects need a reason *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Shoot as much as possible for real; use VFX for what you can't afford or can't do, and give VFX a real foundation (plates, real light) so the look stays consistent across vendors.
- **Parts:** Real light on actors even for effects shots; painted backings over greenscreen; argue for less effect; keep one visual identity.
- **When to use:** VFX budgets, virtual production pitches, greenscreen-heavy plans.
- **How to score:** 5 if VFX extend a real frame; 1 if the frame is built in post.
- **Quote:** "I think it’s the role of the cinematographer to keep the identity on the same plane." (S063)
- **Sources:** 10 (S014, S026, S053, S054, S063, S071, S075, S077, S092, S081)

## 16. Small crew, operate yourself, set the atmosphere *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A small, loyal crew and the DP at the camera keep the set intimate, so actors give more. The DP shapes the set's mood as much as the director.
- **Parts:** Operate; keep the same gaffer, grip and focus puller for decades; quiet set; flags around the monitor.
- **When to use:** Crew sizing, hiring, set culture.
- **How to score:** 5 if crew size is set by the scene; 1 if crew grows by default.
- **Quote:** "Perhaps as much as the director in terms of the atmosphere on the set, because it's the cinematographer that knows all the crew: the gaffer, the key grip and the riggers." (S082)
- **Sources:** 9 (S017, S023, S031, S033, S052, S057, S080, S082, S091)

## 17. The lens says where the audience stands *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Lens choice places the viewer relative to the character. He prefers moderate focal lengths (about 32 to 50mm), spherical lenses, deep focus, and saves the close-up for when it means something.
- **Parts:** Overs on 32 or 35, closer on 40 or 50; no lazy long lens; mean the close-up; avoid distracting artifacts.
- **When to use:** Coverage plans, TV habits of endless singles, lens-package costs.
- **How to score:** 5 if close-ups are rationed to key beats; 1 if singles are the default.
- **Quote:** "The lens informs the audience where they are in relationship to the subject." (S072)
- **Sources:** 7 (S017, S070, S072, S074, S077, S091, S071)

## 18. Push against limits *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Constraints are where the work comes from. Aim a little beyond the time and money you have; never take "anything you want" as a gift.
- **Parts:** Accept the budget, then try for more than it should allow; find the cheap version of the big idea.
- **When to use:** When a team asks for more money or more time as the only answer.
- **How to score:** Good when the ask is a smarter use of what exists; poor when it is only "more".
- **Quote:** "I think you always want to try doing something that’s probably not attainable with the time and the money and the resources you have, but that’s the nature of what you’re trying to do." (S081)
- **Also:** on Fargo's low budget, "But in a way, that often helps you find your way of doing it, you know, less or less more approach, really." (S093)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S070, S073, S081, S093, S097)

## 19. Test before you commit *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Pre-light the unknown, test formats on a big screen, test kit within budget, run makeup tests to study faces.
- **Parts:** Visit rigs before the day; test cameras and lenses; check LED color and dimming; bracket on film.
- **When to use:** New kit, new formats, large rigs, unfamiliar locations.
- **How to score:** 5 if risky elements are tested in prep; 1 if first tried on the shoot day.
- **Quote:** "I didn’t want to get there on the day and be screwed." (S041)
- **Sources:** 6 (S017, S041, S071, S074, S077, S092)

## 20. One coherent world *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The hardest job is a look that hangs together across scenes shot far apart; no single shot is worth breaking it.
- **Parts:** Decide the look, break it into shots, schedule for the light, keep dailies even.
- **When to use:** Multi-location shoots, multiple units, multiple VFX vendors, series with several directors.
- **How to score:** 5 if a single visual logic holds across units; 1 if each unit or episode invents its own.
- **Quote:** "The biggest challenge is always to weave everything together in terms of a coherent whole, because you’re shooting scenes so far apart and in different conditions, quite often." (S045)
- **Sources:** 6 (S012, S045, S063, S071, S073, S077)

## 21. Strip to essentials, then build back (decisive moment)
- **What it is:** Strip the frame to what the moment needs, then add only what serves it, aiming for the frame that captures the scene's decisive moment.
- **Parts:** Few elements; juxtaposition inside the frame; shots are short, so clarity first.
- **When to use:** Composition debates, busy production design, crowded frames.
- **How to score:** 5 if the key information reads in a short shot; 1 if the frame is cluttered.
- **Quote:** "you shoot a frame and you strip away everything apart from what you really need and then you build it back up." (S089)
- **Sources:** 4 (S089, S063, S040, S081)

## 22. Life experience over technique *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** What you have lived shapes your eye more than training; filmmakers raised only on films repeat films.
- **Parts:** Documentary years, travel, stills, painting; watch widely; don't copy.
- **When to use:** Hiring young crew, judging a reel built from references.
- **How to score:** 5 if the work shows a point of view from life; 1 if it is a collage of other films.
- **Quote:** "I think life experience is always more important than technical knowledge." (S048)
- **Sources:** 6 (S026, S043, S048, S062, S088, S080)

## 23. Protect control of the image (no unsupervised units) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Multiple units and second-unit specialists shoot formula coverage that misses the shot the story needs; keep units few, boarded, and under one visual intent.
- **Parts:** Ask the director early how the film will be shot; second unit only from precise boards or previs; bring extra cameras for chosen moments, not by default.
- **When to use:** Action-heavy schedules, franchise productions, series with many units or directors.
- **How to score:** 5 if every unit shoots from the same boards and LUT; 1 if units "grab coverage" on zooms.
- **Quote:** "When, okay, the second unit director or cameraman or whatever doesn't understand why you want that shot. They give you everything else, but not the one you really need or want." (S098)
- **Sources:** 4 (S032, S073, S098, S053)
