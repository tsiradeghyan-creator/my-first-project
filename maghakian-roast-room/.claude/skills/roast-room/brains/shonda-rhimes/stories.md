# Stories: Shonda Rhimes

Built from the extract notes on 113 sources (2009 to 2026). 20 stories and cases she reuses, with the lesson she draws. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **"What does Bob Iger want?"** Her first pilot, about competitive women war correspondents, died because the country was at war. She asked what the network chief wanted, heard "a medical show", and moved the same kind of women into surgery. Lesson: a failed pilot is a test result; serve the buyer's need without giving up your vision (S015, S073, S081, S101, S109).

2. **Casting Cristina.** Everyone, including her producing partner, wanted another actress. Casting director Linda Lowy warned that if she cast someone she did not want, it would not be her show. She said no; a day later Sandra Oh walked in to read for Bailey and Rhimes knew she was Cristina. Lesson: power you do not use is not power; trust the key that turns the lock (S015, S073).

3. **The ABC meeting about Meredith.** Older executives said no one would watch a woman who slept with a stranger the night before her first day as a surgeon. She kept the scene. Lesson: the buyers did not know the world; write the women you actually know (S033, S102, S109, S101).

4. **The firing-proof first year.** On Grey's she did not know a creator could be fired from her own show, so she pushed back on every note she disliked. Lesson: act as if you cannot be fired (S015, S081, S109).

5. **"Cyrus is gay."** Watching an edit of Scandal she saw a repression in Jeff Perry's performance, ran upstairs to the writers' room and changed the character's whole trajectory. Bellamy Young's guest role as Mellie became a lead the same way. Lesson: write the words, watch the actors, then let what they found change the story (S073, S080, S104).

6. **Va-jay-jay.** Standards limited how often Grey's could say "vagina" while "penis" ran free; with no time left before a Super Bowl episode she invented a euphemism, then made a point of saying the real word as often as possible. Lesson: fences breed invention, and then you push the fence (S073).

7. **Shot for shot.** When standards objected to a Scandal sex scene and later Olivia's abortion, she showed them the identical Grey's scene they had approved. Same with a gay love scene on How to Get Away With Murder. Lesson: prove the objection is to the people, not the scene (S015, S080).

8. **The ectopic pregnancy.** Cristina was meant to have an abortion in Grey's season one; network concerns scared her, so she wrote an ectopic pregnancy. Years later she gave Olivia Pope the abortion, inside the Christmas episode. Lesson: her own admitted failure of nerve, then the fix: put the issue inside a loved character, not an issue episode (S015).

9. **Denny's death and return.** She planned "Dying young guy dies young" on the board at the start of season two, sobbed for an hour after the scene, and later brought dead Denny back, which angered fans. Lesson: kill for the story; she admits the return was her own grief (S015, S080).

10. **Judy Smith and four hours.** Betsy Beers brought her a real Washington crisis manager; after four hours of talk she knew she could write hundreds of episodes. Lesson: test a premise by how much story you can see in it; Olivia Pope is built from a real job, not a real person (S015, S101, S102).

11. **The Russian finale.** Scandal's planned season ending had Russians controlling an American election; when the real election made that headline, she had to change it, and later said life had surpassed what they could invent. Lesson: a political show must stay ahead of reality or get out of its way (S100, S103, S104, S012).

12. **Oprah's verdict.** In 2006 Oprah told her she was not enjoying any of it. She started going to the soundstage and celebrating; much later she called not enjoying success her biggest early mistake. Lesson: fear is fuel but also a cost (S033, S040, S078, S101).

13. **The Year of Yes.** At Thanksgiving 2013 her oldest sister said "you never say yes to anything". She spent a year saying yes to what scared her: the Dartmouth speech, Jimmy Kimmel, her health, hard conversations. Lesson: doing the feared thing undoes the fear; later, learn to say no to the right things (S015, S019, S020, S077, S093, S101).

14. **The hum stopped.** At four shows and about 70 hours a season she burned out; saying yes to 15 minutes of play with her daughters brought the work's "hum" back. Lesson: work doesn't work without play (S093).

15. **The Disneyland pass.** After making Disney about 2 billion dollars on Grey's, an executive asked "Don't you have enough?" over a park pass. She called her lawyer and moved to Netflix. Lesson: feeling undervalued and creatively stuck is the signal to leave (S029, S031, S078, S112).

16. **"Not Grey's Anatomy in a cornfield."** At breakfast with Ted Sarandos she said she would not make another Grey's and only wanted to be left alone to make shows; he agreed and kept the promise, which made her more collaborative. Lesson: never sell a new buyer a copy of your hit; buy freedom, then honor it (S035, S078, S079, S111).

17. **Culture shock and the empty drawer.** After signing she had no idea what to write and panicked until she read the Anna Delvey article and "knew exactly what the show was"; it took over a year to find her footing at Netflix. Lesson: commit when you can see the show, and expect a new engine to take time (S035, S049, S078).

18. **Sick in a hotel with The Duke and I.** She had disdain for romance novels, read one while ill, got out of bed to buy the series and told Betsy Beers she had found a show. She saw a workplace drama about women's only arena of power. Lesson: find the angle that makes a genre yours (S044, S050, S068).

19. **The sister who is not impressed.** She shows every edit first to her oldest sister Delores, who doesn't care that she is famous, and changes things where her sister is lost. Lesson: test on one blunt, ordinary viewer before the buyer (S019).

20. **The Isaiah Washington incident.** An early Grey's crisis (an actor's homophobic slur) with no one helping them; she and Beers thought it would kill the show, and it shaped how they work with people. Lesson: the creator is the leader whether she wants it or not (S033).
