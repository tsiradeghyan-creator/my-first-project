# Positions: Shonda Rhimes

Built from the extract notes on 113 sources (2009 to 2026). 36 topics, weighted to what a studio brings to the showrunning seat, most-used first. Every quote is verbatim with its source id and date (year-month). Where her view changed, both versions sit side by side with dates and the newer one is marked **(newer)**. Topics with no evidence are listed at the end.

## How long a series should run, and when to end it
- **Stance:** Know the end of a finite story and keep to it; an open-ended engine runs as long as the characters still interest her. End a show while it is good, "all in", and do not stretch a story that does not need more.
- "I feel like there is a finite amount of "Scandal" to be told." (S102, 2013-11)
- "It's going to go as long as I feel interested in what happens to those characters." (S102, 2013-11)
- "Deciding how to end a show is easy. But actually going through with it? Actually standing up to say, 'This is it?' Not so much," (S110, 2017-05) "So next year we are going all out. Leaving nothing on the table," (S110, 2017-05)
- Grey's: "I have written the end of the show at least six times." (S092, 2018-09) **(newer)** "I'll have a feeling of like, 'We've done what we needed to do here.'" (S060, 2023-05)
- Bridgerton is fixed at eight seasons, "Exactly eight." (S050, 2025-08)
- Sources (8): S050, S060, S080, S092, S102, S104, S110, S032.

## Series versus limited series, and season length
- **Stance:** Let the story set the length. Streaming lets her make six episodes or eight; network demanded 22 to 24 and years of reasons to keep couples apart. A limited series is "a very different animal" and a character study.
- "That's not true with Netflix," (S085, 2017-11) "You can make a season that's six episodes." (S085, 2017-11)
- "I can make something that is an hour and a half and I can make something that is 15 minutes long." (S086, 2017-10)
- "In network television, you have to come up with 15 years of why a couple has to be apart," (S041, 2021-11)
- "a limited series is just a very different animal" (S048, 2022-08)
- Sources (5): S019, S041, S048, S085, S086.

## Network versus streaming (choosing the platform)
- **Stance:** Different engines. Network: bureaucracy, "no" first, a fixed brand and audience, a script every eight days. Streaming: "yes" first, the world as the audience, freedom from standards and length, but slow, write-everything-first production.
- "They're completely different engines," (S042, 2023-05) "They start with 'yes' and then figure out how to make something happen." (S042, 2023-05)
- "their specific audience is the world, which is fascinating" (S019, 2023-05)
- "it's very counterintuitive to the way I learned to make television" (S094, 2024-05)
- On streaming economics: "It works for Netflix, but doesn't work for anybody else." (S111, 2023-11)
- 2016: unsure network TV is sustainable: "I don't know if that model is sustainable." (S100, 2017-04)
- Sources (9): S019, S041, S042, S076, S085, S086, S094, S100, S111.

## Leaving a long-time home (ABC to Netflix)
- **Stance:** She left because she had stopped learning and wanted to be left alone to make shows, not for money; the trigger was feeling undervalued.
- "I felt like I was dying," (S078, 2020-10) "Like I'd been pushing the same ball up the same hill in the exact same way for a really long time." (S078, 2020-10)
- "I felt like I wasn't learning anything new." (S101, 2025-04)
- "I made the move because I realised that we had become pros at making network television - where is the challenge?" (S055, 2026-03)
- After the move: "We had real culture shock." (S078, 2020-10) Later: "Creatively, I feel very fulfilled in a way that I haven't felt fulfilled in a long time." (S041, 2021-11) Renewed for five more years in 2026 (S057).
- Sources (8): S033, S041, S055, S057, S078, S086, S101, S111.

## Pay, ownership and deals
- **Stance:** Creators are underpaid relative to what they earn studios; get a stake, know your value, say it out loud, and never negotiate without a walk-away point.
- "Disney has made $2 billion on 'Grey's Anatomy,' " (S014, 2017-10) "I don't have $2 billion." (S014, 2017-10)
- "I've had to learn not to get screwed in this town," (S014, 2017-10)
- "It's important to have a stake in what you make," (S086, 2017-10)
- "I am the highest-paid showrunner in television." (S078, 2020-10, quoting her 2018 speech)
- "If you walk into that room without knowing what your bottom line is, then you've already lost." (S101, 2025-04)
- Sources (6): S014, S015, S078, S086, S101, S035.

## Notes from buyers and executives
- **Stance:** Notes are diagnoses, not prescriptions. She will hear what does not work; she will not take a fix. In early network years she declined notes outright and later called that immature.
- "Every once in awhile, we'll get a letter from the network suggesting something. But I always ignore that stuff." (S089, 2009-06)
- "Many times I say, that's a lovely note and I decline to take it." (S073, 2015-11)
- **(newer)** "For me, it's don't walk into my office and hand me your solutions. I just need to know what the problems are" (S019, 2023-05) "And now, I'm definitely at a place where I feel more open to hearing criticism, to understanding why something's not working, to hear that people wish I'd done something else." (S019, 2023-05)
- Sources (6): S019, S033, S073, S081, S089, S101.

## Fans, social media and audience reaction
- **Stance:** She does not let fans steer story and does not read coverage; strong reactions mean it works. She used social media to build community (live-tweeting Scandal).
- "I think I'm pretty famous for being a person who says I don't pay attention to fans," (S042, 2023-05)
- "So, to me, the audience can't dictate story, because the audience doesn't know what the story is." (S080, 2025-03)
- "How people react and take in the show is none of my business." (S054, 2025-08)
- Live-tweeting: "we just did it on our own until they saw it" (S042, 2023-05)
- Sources (7): S042, S054, S068, S074, S080, S089, S100.

## Likeable leads and women characters
- **Stance:** Women leads should be as selfish, powerful, sexual and flawed as men; "watchable" beats "sympathetic". She hates "smart, strong women" as praise.
- "I wasn't really interested in whether or not the characters felt sympathetic in traditional ways." (S102, 2013-11)
- "But I don't know any dumb and weak women." (S011, 2016-10)
- "I really hate the phrase 'smart, strong women,'" (S035, 2018-07)
- "There were no women who felt like real women on television at that point in time." (S058, 2025-07)
- No woman's existence hinges on a man: "none of our characters base their existence on whether nor not they have a guy" (S092, 2018-09)
- Sources (8): S011, S035, S058, S076, S092, S102, S037, S081.

## Representation and casting the world
- **Stance:** Normal, not brave: cast and staff as the world looks, include yourself, give no one the "Only One" burden. She is tired of being asked about it.
- "I am making TV look like the world looks." (S006, 2015-03)
- "This is not the Jim Crow south," (S038, 2015-04)
- "My world doesn't function around making sure that people get included," (S014, 2017-10)
- "If you can look around the table and only see yourself, something is wrong with your table." (S063, 2023-05)
- "I'm never going to write a show that doesn't include me," (S066, 2025-09)
- Period pieces too: "If a black woman in 21st century America can see herself in regency England, it's a good story." (S050, 2025-08)
- Sources (12): S006, S011, S014, S027, S038, S050, S063, S066, S067, S079, S084, S100.

## Casting leads and actors
- **Stance:** Cast by gut, not consensus; casting is a marriage of many seasons; no chemistry reads; leave physical description open.
- "She turned the key that fit the lock, you know, that made the story happen in my brain." (S073, 2015-11)
- "And so it's a marriage. It's a family." (S108, 2014-09)
- "For me, really great actors can have chemistry with a telephone book." (S080, 2025-03)
- She keeps working with the same people: "I would attach any of the actors I've worked with before to it." (S060, 2023-05)
- Sources (7): S015, S060, S073, S076, S080, S087, S108.

## The writers room and staffing
- **Stance:** Hire writers you can spend long hours with and who challenge you. Scandal broke every script in the room week to week; Grey's was more traditional. Her company is writer-driven.
- "I'm not looking for writers who sound like me, I'm looking for writers who challenge me." (S081, 2013-10)
- "There was no planning of that show. We sit in that room, we talk about everything, and it is a week-to-week situation." (S087, 2017-04)
- "We're a writer-driven company, we're not crossing picket lines." (S079, 2024-06)
- Sources (6): S027, S063, S067, S079, S081, S087.

## Handing a show to a new showrunner
- **Stance:** Pick one person with her sensibility, then let go fully: no script sign-off, no early cuts. Stay available for network talks and the season pitch.
- "Krista is the only person that I've ever known who has my sensibility and understands the voice of what I've always been saying about the show" (S092, 2018-09)
- "So, I'm here if she needs me, and it'll always be my show." (S079, 2024-06)
- Sources (4): S079, S080, S092, S013.

## Producing shows other people create
- **Stance:** She is the "grandmother" who holds the baby and gives it back, and the "dragon" who protects the creator's vision (S067). Shows she created all hit; shows others wrote did not all take off, and she studied why.
- "It's more like being the grandmother - you hold the baby, but then you get to give the baby back" (S073, 2015-11)
- "I was always really comfortable with the shows that I created being fine and taking off and being good." (S015, 2025-10)
- Producing Bridgerton let her "help shape the story" of a show she did not write (S076, 2023-05, paraphrase).
- Sources (5): S015, S037, S067, S073, S076.

## Pitching
- **Stance:** She was a terrible pitcher and let scripts sell her. Ask what the buyer wants and bring them the answer. Now she no longer pitches.
- "I couldn't pitch a story to anybody to save my life." (S019, 2023-05)
- "I would pitch by staring at a piece of paper, shaking the entire time, and if someone interrupted me, I'd have to start over." (S055, 2026-03)
- "It's nice to bring them an answer to that question right at the beginning." (S087, 2017-04)
- Notes she gives on a pitch: "I don't know what your conflict is, so you gotta tell me what the conflict is." (S101, 2025-04)
- Sources (6): S019, S055, S080, S087, S101, S109.

## Adaptation: books, articles and true stories
- **Stance:** Honor the source's tone, invent where the story needs it, keep distance from real subjects, and use a reliable narrator when the center is unknowable.
- "The Bridgerton books have a certain tone and tale that we really wanted to honor because that's what makes them so popular." (S095, 2023-05)
- "We needed a reliable narrator, and Anna was definitely not going to be our reliable narrator." (S047, 2022-02)
- "I knew we needed that separation of church and state." (S047, 2022-02)
- "there was also stuff that we invented because it needed to be invented to make the story really sing" (S048, 2022-08)
- Sources (6): S047, S048, S049, S050, S068, S095.

## Spinoffs, prequels and franchises
- **Stance:** Spinoffs tend to give fans more of the same; she wants shows that stand on their own, prefers a prequel or a character's private side, and refuses to repeat a hit in a new setting.
- "It just didn't appeal to me to rebuild the same world." (S080, 2025-03)
- "If you spin off a character, what people want is more of the same." (S080, 2025-03)
- "Scandal is done." (S104, 2018-04)
- Sources (6): S032, S078, S080, S094, S095, S104.

## Sex, standards and censorship
- **Stance:** Fight standards on double standards with shot-for-shot evidence; work creatively inside the fences; network sex should not shock a child who wanders in before 10 pm (S073, paraphrase).
- "we're incredibly creative within our fences" (S073, 2015-11)
- "The standards have changed to match the American people." (S012, 2016-10)
- "So it's not the scene, it's the people having the sex." (S015, 2025-10)
- Sources (5): S012, S013, S015, S073, S080.

## Social issues and politics inside drama
- **Stance:** Entertain first; put issues inside loved characters, not issue episodes; real events can overtake a political show.
- "I wanted people that somebody knew and loved to be going through this and for it not to be the abortion episode." (S015, 2025-10)
- "And now life has surpassed anything that we could come up with." (S103, 2018-04)
- "I think you can entertain people and at the same time make some points." (S032, 2024-04)
- 2025: "I'll be honest, I'm still figuring that out right now." on how the political moment shapes stories (S033, 2025-03)
- Sources (6): S012, S015, S032, S033, S100, S103.

## Tone for the moment
- **Stance:** In dark times audiences want escape and joy, with substance tucked inside.
- "I do believe, when the lights are off, everybody wants a warm campfire story, and when the lights are on, everybody loves a good nightmare." (S033, 2025-03)
- "I feel like right now we're tryin' to mine a lot more joy and a lot more enjoyment," (S058, 2025-07)
- "it's much more important to me to watch something that lets me escape life for a little bit and to enjoy it" (S076, 2023-05)
- Sources (4): S019, S033, S058, S076.

## Budget and production economics
- **Stance:** Envies big budgets but spends responsibly to last; big event episodes break an efficient system; permanent sets speed streaming up.
- "Think about what you could do with a much bigger playground," (S042, 2023-05)
- "We could do anything, but I want us to be responsible because we want to go for a long time." (S079, 2024-06)
- "By season 2, we had it down pat, how you can make a show and make it in a decent economic way." (S080, 2025-03)
- Network scale: about 70 hours a season, 3 to 6 million dollars an episode (S093, 2016-02).
- Sources (5): S042, S079, S080, S093, S094.

## Writing everything before shooting
- **Stance:** She learned TV as writing while shooting and still prefers seeing actors before finishing; streaming's write-all-first model slows output and leaves less room for performance. She also likes writing a whole limited season first.
- "It leaves no room for the actors' performance," (S078, 2020-10)
- "you can sort of write them completely, see what the whole pictures is, and then hand it over to production" (S019, 2023-05)
- "To me, we should be able to write and produce shows at the same time" (S094, 2024-05)
- 2024 plan: write one episode, take three weeks off, evaluate, "pilot-style" (S079, 2024-06).
- Sources (5): S019, S078, S079, S080, S094.

## Process: outlines, research, writer's block
- **Stance:** No outlines since the Grey's pilot; research the world, picture scenes, think for a long time, then write fast. She does not believe in writer's block.
- "I really just start writing and see where it takes me." (S019, 2023-05)
- "And then I'll spend a year thinking about something, and then two days writing a script" (S015, 2025-10)
- "I don't believe in writer's block." (S015, 2025-10)
- Sources (5): S015, S019, S034, S076, S087.

## Working hours, burnout and email
- **Stance:** She burned out at network scale; now she protects time and delegates. No work email after 7 pm or weekends.
- "I don't read work emails after 7 PM or on weekends. And if you work for me, may I suggest you put down your phone." (S073, 2015-11)
- "And then the hum stopped. Overworked, overused, overdone, burned out." (S093, 2016-02)
- "But I feel like it almost killed me. It was too much. Getting to slow down was probably the best thing for my creativity." (S032, 2024-04)
- Sources (6): S013, S032, S073, S079, S087, S093.

## Work-life balance
- **Stance:** Balance is a lie; succeeding at one means failing at another; you can have it all but not at once.
- "Work/life balance is a lie that we are fed to make us feel bad about ourselves." (S087, 2017-04)
- "If I am succeeding at one, I am inevitably failing at the other." (S001, 2014-06) (also S098)
- **(newer)** "It's not that you can't do it all. It's that you can't do it all at once." (S015, 2025-10)
- Sources (5): S001, S015, S087, S098, S101.

## Leadership
- **Stance:** Learned on the job, from books, through mistakes; empower people, own what happens when you are absent, and now (2025) work on relationships, not only tasks.
- "I was terrible at being a leader when I started." (S015, 2025-10)
- "to empower the people around me to do their jobs instead of trying to do them myself" (S019, 2023-05)
- "If work isn't fun, we are doing it wrong, because it is a creative endeavor and it should be for everyone who is doing it." (S013, 2016-10)
- Sources (6): S013, S015, S019, S033, S079, S101.

## Hiring and firing
- **Stance:** Hire for voice and trust; fire directly. She admits she once softened firings so much people came back the next day.
- "If you hire people and you don't trust them, then why have you hired them?" (S080, 2025-03)
- "I have fired people who did not know they were fired and came to work the next day" (S077, 2025-11)
- Sources (4): S077, S080, S081, S101.

## Mentors
- **Stance:** She had none in Hollywood; books were her mentors. Do not wait to be picked.
- "So my mentors were books." (S101, 2025-04)
- "You can be your own best mentor." (S074, 2025-10)
- Sources (4): S040, S063, S074, S101.

## Directing and being on set
- **Stance:** Not for her; her presence on set distorts decisions. Write, then see what comes out the other side.
- "this is literally the last thing I want to do" (S079, 2024-06)
- "So, I learned that if I was going to set, the only thing I should do was walk through and say very loudly what a great job everybody was doing and leave." (S079, 2024-06)
- Sources (2): S034, S079.

## Watching other television
- **Stance:** Avoids narrative TV while writing so it does not creep in; watches documentaries and sports.
- "I pretty much try not to watch very much television at all, even American television, until I'm done with a season, because things start to creep into my head otherwise." (S100, 2017-04)
- "I have not watched narrative programming in a while. And I think it's because I wanted to give my brain a break." (S111, 2023-11)
- Sources (3): S032, S100, S111.

## The future of TV, short form and attention
- **Stance:** Uncertain; storytelling survives, form changes. Short form works if it feels like narrative. A new writer now would be afraid.
- "if you do it right and it actually feels like narrative, people will hang on for it no matter what you do" (S100, 2017-04)
- "I think there will always be storytelling; I just don't know what form it's going to take." (S032, 2024-04)
- "Now we're in the place where the bust is starting." (S032, 2024-04)
- Sources (4): S032, S065, S072, S100.

## Interactive and choose-your-own-adventure stories
- **Stance:** Against it for drama; an authored ending is what makes it art.
- "a story is not as good when I have control over exactly what's going to happen to somebody else's character" (S100, 2017-04)
- "The story is the story that is told" (S100, 2017-04)
- Sources (1): S100.

## Global audiences
- **Stance:** Stop making shows for America and shoving them at the world; streaming's audience is the world.
- "America is not it" (S100, 2017-04)
- "Now I can't wait to show everybody what a Shondaland show is that we make for the world." (S035, 2018-07)
- Sources (3): S019, S035, S100.

## Brand extension, merchandise and live events
- **Stance:** Build Shondaland bigger than her, the way Marvel lives inside Disney (S035, reporter's paraphrase); extend from what the show is about (romance into wedding gowns, tea, balls).
- "It needs to be bigger than me." (S035, 2018-07)
- "This is not a lifestyle site." (S059, 2017-09)
- Bridgerton merchandise, balls and a Flonase ad came from "looking at what our show is about" (S101, 2025-04, paraphrase).
- Sources (4): S010, S035, S059, S101.

## Ratings and data
- **Stance:** Not driven by ratings; the goal is a show she is proud of. Early test scores told her Grey's was special.
- Never concerned with ratings, per Variety's report (S014, 2017-10, paraphrase).
- "The goal is to make a great show that I'm proud of." (S074, 2025-10)
- Sources (3): S014, S074, S080.

## Writers' pay and strikes
- **Stance:** Writers must earn a living wage; she struck and did not write during 2023.
- "But for writers to not be able to make a living wage while making a television show or making a movie is a problem." (S063, 2023-05)
- "I consider myself to be a writer, first and foremost." (S111, 2023-11)
- Sources (3): S063, S079, S111.

## Competition and fear
- **Stance:** She chose work with no competition; industry fear of choosing the wrong show is pointless.
- "I've chosen a job in which there's no competition allowed." (S026, 2015-11)
- "everyone's afraid of being the person who makes the mistake choosing the wrong show. Which is always pointless." (S032, 2024-04)
- Sources (2): S026, S032.

## Success and legacy
- **Stance:** Refuses to discuss legacy while working; her biggest mistake was not enjoying success; now she writes for love.
- "I certainly don't spend any time thinking about legacy because we are still doing this." (S037, 2015-08)
- "The biggest mistake I made was not enjoying any of my successes." (S040, 2025-10)
- **(newer)** "The point is, is I want to really love what I'm doing while I'm doing it." (S077, 2025-11)
- Sources (6): S037, S040, S077, S078, S104, S019.

## Remote work and location
- **Stance:** Leaving LA gave her boundaries and autonomy for her team; she lives in Connecticut and works across LA and London.
- "I left L.A., which helps a lot, because you can't be in the office all the time if you're not in the same state, right?" (S032, 2024-04)
- "But it became a necessity, so everybody's much more autonomous now and it's great." (S079, 2024-06)
- Sources (3): S015, S032, S079.
