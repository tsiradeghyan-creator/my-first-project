# Frameworks: Co-production

Composite archetype. Fund and programme details change; always check the current guidelines.

## COP-F1 Finance plan puzzle
What: a table of every funding piece: source, amount, status (idea, applied, letter of intent, signed), what it costs (rights, territory, credit, control), and when cash arrives.
Score: under 30 percent signed before production = high risk.

## COP-F2 Rights split map
What: who owns the underlying IP, the format, each territory, each window, and for how long, after all deals.
Score: studio keeps IP and format rights = strong. Studio keeps only a fee = treat as work for hire.

## COP-F3 Eligibility check
What: for each fund or treaty: nationality of producers, minimum share and spend, language, creative points, deadlines. Typical places to check for an Armenian studio: the national cinema centre, bilateral co-production agreements, European co-production funds Armenia takes part in, festival development funds and labs. Check current membership and calls; do not rely on memory.

## COP-F4 Waterfall read
What: the order in which income pays back sales agents, distributors, investors and producers. Find the line where the studio gets paid.

## COP-F5 Market and lab circuit
What: co-production markets, pitching forums, script labs and festival industry days where partners meet. Pick two a year that fit the project and go prepared.

## COP-F6 Minority versus majority co-producer
What: as majority you control creative and own more; as minority you earn credits, learn, and get paid for services. Both are valid, for different goals.

## COP-F7 Diaspora partner map
What: Armenian communities in Russia, the United States, France, Lebanon and elsewhere: channels, cultural organisations, private investors and audiences. A partner can be a buyer, a co-financier or a distributor.

## COP-F8 Scale to the money
What: if the plan cannot close, reshape the project (fewer episodes, smaller cast, animation instead of live action) rather than wait years.
