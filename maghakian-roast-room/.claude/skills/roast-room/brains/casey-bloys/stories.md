# Stories: Casey Bloys

Built from 129 public sources (2016 to 2026). The cases he and the people around him retell, one or two lines each, with the lesson he draws. Where the account is someone else's (a creator or a reporter), it says so.

1. **Eastbound & Down (2009).** As young head of comedy he pushed a raunchy Danny McBride show that senior HBO people doubted. Lesson: an executive's job is advocacy, "How to explain to people why something is good, why we should put our name and our money behind something." (S008) McBride recalls Bloys flying to Puerto Rico to ask for one shot to go, then keeping the light hand (S112, McBride's account).

2. **The Last of Us (2023).** He had never heard of the game; he greenlit Craig Mazin after Chernobyl. "I don't even play video games, but we were betting on Craig, not the game" (S008) Mazin recalls his one big note: merge the first two episodes so the stronger ending pulls viewers forward (S008, Mazin's account).

3. **Chernobyl (2019).** "Craig [Mazin] pitched it as a monster story in which the nuclear reactor was the monster," (S112) Lesson: a serious subject sells when it is pitched as a gripping genre story.

4. **Vinyl (2016), his first big decision.** He reversed a season 2 renewal after a showrunner change because a retool would only move the needle a little. Lesson: limited resources go where a show can be great; also, do not renew too early. "I'd like to not have to do that again." (S031) (S030, S036, S071, S133)

5. **Confederate (2017).** HBO announced a slavery alternate-history drama by press release and drew a firestorm. He owned the mistake as HBO's and defended the writers. Lesson: sensitive material needs the creators to explain it in their own words. "I would have rolled it out with the producers talking on the record so people understood where they're coming from." (S120) The show never went ahead (S078). (S047, S077, S120)

6. **Game of Thrones ends (2016 to 2019).** He accepted Benioff and Weiss's plan for eight seasons though he wanted more, refused the fan petition to remake the finale, and planned 2019 years ahead to replenish the slate. "A very deliberate attempt to replenish the slate." (S112) (S030, S086, S112, S132)

7. **The first Thrones prequel pilot (2019 to 2020).** HBO shot Jane Goldman's pilot set thousands of years earlier, then dropped it and made House of the Dragon. "It required a lot more invention; it was higher risk, higher reward," (S142) Lesson: a pilot can be good and still not right; the pilot exists to find out. (S118, S142, S143)

8. **Succession (2018 to 2023).** A "very deliberate attempt to pivot away from Thrones" (S112) with a contemporary family show. When Jesse Armstrong chose to end it after four seasons, Bloys said he would have taken more but left it to him, with one note: "So just really, really be sure that it's time to go," (S095) Lesson: the creator ends the story; no multiverse spinoffs. (S055, S061, S085, S095, S111)

9. **Euphoria (2019).** He wanted a younger-skewing show and asked what the HBO version of a teen show would be; Sam Levinson's lens on addiction answered it. "I don't think we fully appreciate as a culture what we're doing to teenagers," (S112) (S019, S112)

10. **Watchmen (2019 to 2020).** Damon Lindelof pitched a superhero show about the Tulsa massacre and race. Bloys: "If Damon said he wanted to do anything, I'd say, 'Great,' because I believe in him," (S112) He did not push for a second season after Lindelof told a complete story (S103).

11. **Westworld (2018 to 2023).** He defended season 2's divisive reception as the kind of feeling HBO wants (S028, S073), then later pulled the show off the platform and licensed it to free ad-supported outlets after talking to its creators. Lesson: an expensive show needs a life in more windows. (S008)

12. **Big Little Lies season 2 (2019).** Director Andrea Arnold's cut was reworked by Jean-Marc Vallee and the producers; Bloys called reports "misinformation" and said a TV director does not have final cut (S097, S098, S138). Critics read it as the network overriding a director; this is where his creator-first talk stops at the network's final say.

13. **The Max years (2020 to 2025).** HBO Max, then Max with Discovery reality, then back to HBO Max. He backed dropping HBO from the name in 2023 to protect the brand (S058), then admitted the broad service failed: "We just came back from trying to offer too much" (S062). Lesson: breadth that changes the value proposition fails; breadth that keeps it (the Warner library, The Pitt) works (S065).

14. **The Pitt (2025 to 2026).** A cheaper, 15-episode, annual hospital drama launched as a Max original experiment, won the Emmy for best drama and pulled in library viewers. "The success of The Pitt is because of what was missing in the marketplace." (S002) It became the model for new cop and family dramas (S010, S069).

15. **Heated Rivalry (2025 to 2026).** A finished Canadian show he watched in full and acquired as an "easy yes" (S039), which grew "like a hockey stick" (S101). Lesson: a buyer can win with a finished show it did not develop, and then stay out of its creative.

16. **The burner accounts (2023).** A lawsuit revealed he had staff reply to critics from fake accounts in 2020 to 2021. He opened his slate event with an apology: "And I come up with a very, very dumb idea to vent my frustration." (S082) Lesson he draws: passion for the shows; the critics read it as thin skin.

17. **The Steel Town lesson.** Growing up in Bethlehem, Pennsylvania taught him what happens to companies that do not see the future, yet he still refuses AI in storytelling. "So I am very, very aware of what happens when a company does not think about the future or is not aware of the future." (S095)

18. **The writers' room on Less Than Perfect (early 2000s).** As a development executive he watched Mitch Hurwitz help writers fix a story. "It was interesting to see how you could help a writer unblock." (S008) Lesson: the executive's note should unblock, not rewrite.

19. **Insecure (2013 to 2016).** Developed from Issa Rae's web series when he ran comedy. "It introduces us to new talent," (S023) Lesson: new voices from outside the system are worth developing.

20. **Two mergers and a third (2018 to 2026).** AT&T, then Discovery, then Paramount Skydance; each time he told the team to focus on the work, and he ended up running HBO Max and Paramount+. "You have to understand I've been through two mergers, I don't think there's any announcement that would have shocked me," (S065) (S028, S055, S067, S087)
