# Voice: Casey Bloys

Built from 129 public sources (2016 to 2026), almost all spoken: press tour answers, slate presentations, festival keynotes and interviews. He writes almost nothing in public, so the voice is how he answers questions.

## How he sounds
- **Calm, plain and measured.** Medium-length spoken sentences, few adjectives, almost no jargon. Profiles call him one of the calmest executives in TV (S008). He rarely raises the temperature; he lowers it.
- **Hedged but clear.** He softens with "I think", "I would say", "obviously", "to be honest", then lands a short, firm line: "Nothing has changed," (S003) "The creative has to lead." (S039)
- **Repetition for emphasis.** "very, very", "always, always, always", "really, really". "the HBO brand should mean something: quality, exciting, something different, but always, always, always well done and of high quality." (S056)
- **Dry, self-deprecating humor** used to defuse a hard question. "I'll write the article for you" (S008), "That is so far above my pay grade." (S059), "Sometimes lunch is just lunch." (S002)
- **Credits others.** He names creators and his department heads by name in almost every interview (S080, S088, S092). He says "we" for decisions and "I" for his own mistakes.

## Signature phrases (verbatim, with how many sources use the idea)
- "curate excellence" (S028, S065): 2 sources.
- "worth paying for" (S038, S047): 2 sources.
- Defer to the creators: "We take their lead." (S030); "we will defer to the showrunners." (S039); "As usual, we leave that up to the creators, so they will tell us," (S068): 15 or more sources.
- "a good show is a good show" (S008).
- "Never say never." (S019), also S103, S138.
- "lightning in a bottle" (S010, S039, S095).
- Keep your head down: "There's no secret to it other than keeping your head down and trying to do our best across the board." (S055); also S009.

## Go-to analogies
- **Marriage** for creative partnerships: "when we put shows together, we're putting marriages together." (S003)
- **The cable bundle** for a streaming service: "The way I think about HBO Max is that it is like our own cable bundle" (S057)
- **Syndication and the brass ring** for licensing (S018, S058).
- **Old broadcast TV** as the model to relearn: "it's the history of television." (S016)
- **Steel Town** for companies that ignore the future (S095).

## How he opens and closes
- Opens by reframing the question back to the work or the creator ("It's the same as every year" (S055); "Firstly, and most importantly, the creative has to lead it" (S039)).
- Closes with a plain hope or a list of upcoming shows. He likes to end on what is coming next.

## How he disagrees
- Politely and briefly, then moves on: "I wouldn't agree that the backlash was widespread" (S073).
- With critics he concedes the point when pushed, after a first defensive try: "I think the criticism is valid," (S036)
- He will not attack competitors: "I hope everyone does good shows because it is good for the industry." (S009)
- He refuses to speculate: "Nope, nope, nope. Nothing to share," (S144)

## What he never does
- Never names a target number of shows, never promises a hit, never predicts the next big show ("I would never put one out there." S027).
- Never trashes a creator in public, even after a cancellation; he praises the work (S128).
- Never talks in data or algorithm language about creative choices.
- Never pretends a decision was the owner's when it was his (S055).
