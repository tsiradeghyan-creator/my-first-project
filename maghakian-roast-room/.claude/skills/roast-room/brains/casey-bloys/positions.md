# Positions: Casey Bloys

Built from 129 public sources (2016 to 2026). 34 topics, weighted toward what a studio or producer would bring to a premium buyer. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id and date. Where his view changed, both versions sit side by side with dates; the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

## What gets a greenlight
- **Stance:** A good script from a creator with a point of view and real passion, judged script by script.
- 2022-02: "We will greenlight something when we feel like something is exciting and well done and tells a story that we care about." (S052)
- 2025-11: "What we're usually looking for is a point of view," (S062)
- 2026-03: "you have to do it based script by script." (S011)

## Pitching and access
- **Stance:** He buys from creators HBO trusts and from established producers, agents and partner networks; he defines the bull's-eye as very small. No recorded advice on cold or unsolicited pitches.
- 2025-05: "The bull's-eye has always been very, very small," (S038)
- 2019-12: "So there's a pretty good shot that if there's something that is getting someone excited, we're going to be excited as well." (S112)
- Inferred from S008, S112, S100: the routes in are a creator's track record, a producer HBO already works with, or a co-production partner.

## Why now (timing)
- **Stance:** Timing comes from the creator's readiness and from gaps in the slate and the market, not from trends or the news. He refuses to commission to a cultural moment.
- 2024-11: "I don't think it works when we say, 'We should do a show about this.'" (S003)
- 2026-03: "The success of The Pitt is because of what was missing in the marketplace." (S002)
- 2017-07: "The good news about someone like Steve Zaillian is he will come to you when he's ready," (S117)

## Volume versus quality
- **Stance:** Volume can rise only if every show would have been made anyway.
- 2018-07: "There are no plans to dilute the HBO brand in favor of volume of programming" (S028)
- 2019-04: "we have not sacrificed quality just to get the numbers up." (S056)
- 2025-11 (newer, as an industry lesson): "In retrospect, we can all see that the streaming industry's race for volume, years ago, found many brands losing their identity." (S062)

## Budgets and cost
- **Stance:** Every show must be made "at a price that is reasonable" for its format; big swings are paid for by profit; strike-driven cost rises do not kill shows.
- 2023-11: "I don't see a scenario where I go, 'Well, I was going to make another tentpole show, but now I'm not going to because we have to pay X amount more.'" (S058)
- 2025-06: "Everyone must manage budgets. That has always been the reality of our business." (S009)
- 2025-07: "it does require, in the case of The Pitt , a fixed location in order to do that at a price that is reasonable." (S041)

## Streaming economics
- **Stance:** Streaming is where viewers are, but it is less profitable than pay TV was; the question is how to make money doing it.
- 2022-07: "How do you make the streaming model the streaming model work? How do you make it profitable?" (S088)
- 2023-03: "for HBO to make the transition we have to figure how to make streaming profitable." (S061)
- 2023-11: "It's more about on a macro level, what's the right amount of spending in streaming to make money?" (S058)

## The content bubble (peak TV)
- **Stance (2026-10, newest):** The drop from about 600 to 400 shows is a catastrophe for jobs, and greenlighting on an idea was never sustainable.
- "People were greenlighting shows based on an idea, it was not sustainable; you couldn't make that many shows as an industry and monetize it properly," (S066)
- "That's a catastrophe. That is very hard across the board. That is what it looks like when a bubble bursts. It's ugly." (S066)

## Competition (Netflix, Apple, big tech)
- **Stance:** Do not chase the volume player; stay in your lane and keep your head down.
- 2020-09: "Because of the volume game, they may beat us in total nominations. But that won't change the kind of shows we do," (S103)
- 2024-12: "Netflix is big and established and is doing everything. We are leaning into what we do well as a company." (S015)
- 2025-06: "I hope everyone does good shows because it is good for the industry." (S009)
- 2026-10: "We are now competing against companies that are in very different spaces, have different economics." (S066)

## Brand breadth (HBO name on the platform): changed twice
- 2023-11: for taking HBO out of the app name: "One of the reasons why I was for taking HBO out of the title is because I want HBO shows to be HBO shows," (S058)
- 2025-06 (newer): the "something for everyone" Max failed, so the HBO name came back: "Kids, tween shows, food, lifestyle…was viewed as too big a stretch for the brand." (S009) "We just came back from trying to offer too much" (S062)
- Consistent core: protect HBO; what changed was the platform strategy around it.

## Second labels and line extensions (Max originals)
- 2021-06: Max originals by demographic: "demographic distinction we do use as a guide to make sense for what works where," (S057)
- 2023-04: too close to HBO: "The kind of shows they were going to were a little bit too HBO-adjacent" (S008)
- 2025-11 (newer): a clear job: "We are leaning into more cost efficient yet elevated and high quality series with a greater number of episodes that can return each year" (S010)

## IP and franchises
- **Stance:** Use the studio's IP, but the creator's vision decides; avoid saturation; each extension must be its own show.
- 2021-02: "On HBO Max, I don't want to do all DC shows; I don't want to do all Game of Thrones spinoffs." (S004)
- 2022-02: "In most of those cases, they weren't based on existing IP." (S054)
- 2025-11: "That affinity and immediate awareness paired with HBO's singular approach to storytelling has proven very valuable" (S062)

## Reboots and revivals
- **Stance:** A few, with care; original stories matter more; some shows should stay finished.
- 2021-02: "I don't want to do too many. I think there are original stories to tell, and we don't want to lose that, so it's always a balancing act." (S070)
- 2022-02: "I think there are some shows that are better left, in Six Feet Under 's case, dead, or finished." (S054)

## Spinoffs
- **Stance:** Only when the world naturally supports them and the creator wants it.
- 2022-07: "There's no scenario where a Succession multiverse is in the cards." (S055)
- 2023-06: "But if Jesse's not feeling it, I'm not going to go down that road." (S095)

## Renewals and more seasons
- **Stance:** Creator-led, performance-checked. Prepare early, decide after airing.
- 2021-06: "It is not a foregone conclusion that just because something does well that we have to do more." (S057)
- 2022-02: "we tend to like to see how a show performs" (S054)

## Cancellation
- **Stance:** A confluence of factors and the numbers; help the creator take it elsewhere.
- 2016-07: "With limited resources, we didn't think the retooling was worth the producers' time if it would only move the needle a little bit." (S071)
- 2021-09: "When you make the decision to not go forward with a show, it's usually a confluence of factors," (S128)
- 2024-01: "whenever we cancel a show - if a creator can set it up elsewhere, we support them." (S129)

## Big Little Lies season 3: changed his mind
- 2019-07: "To me, there's no obvious place to go, no obvious story," (S098)
- 2024-01 (newer): "I think it could be great." (S084), once the producers and the author had a new story.

## Limited versus ongoing series
- **Stance:** Want both; never a service of only one kind.
- 2021-06: "I would not want a service that is only ongoing series, and I would not want a service that is only limited series." (S057)

## Episode counts and annual return
- 2016 to 2023: prestige model of 8 to 10 episodes every 18 months to 2 years was the norm.
- 2025 to 2026 (newer): "the annual return is really, really important," (S069) "That is a really great thing for a programmer to have." (S002)
- Limit: "But from a production point of view, it's just not possible." (S002) for big world-building shows.

## Release model
- **Stance:** Weekly, consistently since at least 2019.
- 2019-12: "I am a huge, huge proponent of week-to-week viewing," (S112)
- 2025-06: "we strongly believe in the weekly rollout model" (S009)

## International, local-language and co-production
- **Stance:** Local originals for local audiences, co-productions welcome, foreign language is more accepted, breakouts cannot be engineered.
- 2021-09: "audiences, because of streaming, are a lot more open to foreign language shows than they were two or three years ago." (S100)
- 2021-09: "I would be hard-pressed to tell you a co-production that we didn't have a good experience with and that's across the board," (S100)
- 2023-03: "The best-case scenario is a Squid Game , something produced for a local audience and no one else," (S061)
- 2024-06: "The job then, in different regions, is what do you put around those shows?" (S108)
- 2026-03 (newer caveat): once the platform launches in a country, "we will probably have to figure out what the streaming rights look like." (S002)

## New buyers entering a market
- 2026-01: "Anytime somebody new enters the market, it's good for the creative industry in that country" (S101)

## Acquisitions
- **Stance:** Happy to acquire finished shows and stay out of their creative.
- 2026-01: "It's not for us to be involved creatively," (S101)
- 2026-02: "It is rare for an acquired original to blow up like this." (S039)

## AI: softened at the edges, firm at the core
- 2023-06: "that's not something I want to be a part of." (S095) and "I hope to God I'm retired by that point." (S095)
- 2024-06: open to production tools and dubbing: "For things that make production easier , sure." (S108)
- 2025-06 (newer): "I have no desire to bring it into the creative process of storytelling." (S009)

## Data, testing and research
- **Stance:** No testing of shows; data and research used for the platform, not for choosing stories.
- 2021-09: "I think we've always been a creative-driven company, not a data-driven company" (S100)
- 2025-05: platform research: "We did an assessment of what subscribers were watching. We did a lot of research and focus groups," (S038)

## Metrics of success
- 2022-07: "some combination of ratings, of reviews, of buzz, all of the above. But there's no threshold for it's gotta hit this many viewers or anything like that." (S088)

## Awards
- 2016-07: "It's always best to win more Emmys," but "that's not the main goal." (S035)
- 2024-01: "It's nice to be nominated, it's really nice to win but it's not why we do the shows," (S090)

## Licensing, windows and free TV
- 2023-04: "I think you have to kind of dip your toe in and see what's out there." (S008)
- 2023-09: "You're introducing it to more people, it's marketing." (S017)
- 2023-11: "we're never giving anybody an exclusive right to a show." (S058)

## Mergers and owners
- **Stance:** Business as usual; focus on the work; mergers have not changed performance.
- 2019-04: "I don't feel restrained in any way, in either creative ambition or how that translates financially." (S056)
- 2025-11: "it's kind of a waste of energy because I don't know what's going to happen." (S007)
- 2026-07: "I don't automatically see a merger changing our performance." (S063)

## Creator control and notes
- **Stance:** Light touch with trusted creators, but the network has the final say; directors do not have final cut in TV.
- 2019-07: "But as anybody who works in TV knows, a director does not have final creative control." (S097)
- 2019-07: "It's not like we're noting them to death." (S118)

## Controversy and sensitive subjects
- **Stance:** Provocation is welcome; sensitive material needs care in how it is presented; listen to criticism.
- 2016-07: "I think the criticism is valid," (S036)
- 2017-07: "If I could do it over again our mistake...was the idea that we would be able to announce an idea that is so sensitive and requires such care and thought... in a press release," (S047)
- 2023-06: "Obviously, I knew it was going to be controversial," (S095)

## Representation
- 2016-07: "and by diversity I mean not just ethnicity, but in scale, scope, location, tone," (S115)
- 2023-10: "That's a sign of progress." (S025) (leads in big shows not commented on)

## Young audiences and Gen Z
- 2021-02: "any brand has to continually redefine what it is and ask who it's appealing to." (S004)
- 2025-06: "I am confident that the art of long form scripted will be just fine." (S009)

## Short-form and new formats
- 2023-06: resisted pressure to pivot to short-form scripted: "I think I was right about that. And I think I'm right about this." (S095)

## Late night and new voices
- 2025-09: "it's really tough to break through and have viewers trust a new voice and stick with them week in and week out." (S059)

## Production location and incentives
- 2026-03: "I think there was this idea that the entertainment industry was a captive audience in California, and plenty of other states have shown it's not." (S002)

## Critics and social media
- 2023-11: "And I come up with a very, very dumb idea to vent my frustration." (S082) He now argues with critics by direct message.
