# Frameworks: Casey Bloys

Built from 129 public sources (2016 to 2026): TCA executive sessions, slate presentations, festival keynotes (Series Mania, RTS, Variety TV Fest, Bloomberg Screentime), and long interviews in THR, Variety, Deadline, TheWrap, The Ringer and Status. Bloys names few frameworks himself; most names below are short descriptive ones marked *(unnamed)*. "Sources" counts distinct sources where the idea appears. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated habits, not quotes.

---

## 1. Bet on the creator, not the IP *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The greenlight is a bet on a person with a specific point of view and deep command of their world. The property, the genre and the format come second.
- **Parts:** Who is the creator; do they have "an unshakable knowledge" of the world and its themes; are they passionate about this exact story; have they delivered before; will they take notes.
- **When to use:** Every pitch. Above all when the material is IP, a genre HBO has not done, or a risky subject.
- **How to score:** 5 if a proven or clearly singular creator is obsessed with this story and can explain its world and themes; 3 if the creator is talented but the drive comes from the IP or the market; 1 if the package is a logline plus auspices with no author at the center.
- **Quote:** "I don't even play video games, but we were betting on Craig, not the game" (S008)
- **Also:** "These four writers are at the top of their game, they can do anything they want, and this is what they feel passionately about. I'll bet on that," (S077)
- **Sources:** 12 (S006, S008, S043, S061, S062, S067, S077, S090, S103, S112, S120, S123)

## 2. Script by script, never a quota *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Every decision starts from the material in front of you. Output targets (shows per year, spinoffs per franchise, ratios between brands) are refused because they push you to greenlight something not good enough.
- **Parts:** Is it a good script; is it interesting creatively; does it make sense as a show; only then fit it to the slate.
- **When to use:** Franchise planning, annual volume talks, any "we need X shows" pressure.
- **How to score:** High if the case rests on pages that are good now; low if it rests on a slot to fill, a date to hit, or a count to reach.
- **Quote:** "you have to do it based script by script. Is it a good script? Do we think it's interesting creatively? Do we think it makes sense as a show?" (S011)
- **Also:** "I think once you set a formula, you get into, well, maybe this isn't great enough, but we need X number of shows, so I try to stay away from any sort of guidelines and let the creative guide." (S069)
- **Sources:** 10 (S004, S011, S030, S052, S057, S062, S069, S111, S117, S132)

## 3. The creator decides length, seasons and endings *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** More seasons, spinoffs and finales come from the creator's sense of story, never from the network's appetite after a hit.
- **Parts:** Ask late in a run where it ends; take as many seasons as the creator wants to give; do not pound the desk for more; let a creator rest before the next project; leave the door open.
- **When to use:** Renewals of hits, spinoff pressure, a creator who wants to stop.
- **How to score:** Good sign if the creator says what story is left and why; warning sign if the push for more comes from performance alone.
- **Quote:** "I don't think it ever works for the platform or network to say to the creator, 'We need five seasons, we need six seasons.'" (S111)
- **Also:** "I promise you I am not pounding on the desk saying 'get us more,'" (S057)
- **Sources:** 17 (S027, S030, S036, S039, S051, S055, S057, S061, S068, S084, S085, S088, S092, S095, S111, S117, S135)

## 4. Is this TV worth paying for? *(his phrase)*
- **What it is:** HBO has always had to make people choose to pay. The brand test is whether a show is something a subscriber would pay extra for, and whether the brand still stands for something in a crowded market.
- **Parts:** Distinctive, high quality, "something different"; a brand that means one thing; a small bull's-eye.
- **When to use:** Brand fit, pricing a premium product, deciding what a label should carry.
- **How to score:** 5 if a viewer would name this show as a reason to subscribe; 3 if it is good but interchangeable; 1 if it is lean-back filler.
- **Quote:** "The mission has always been, 'Is this TV worth paying for?'" (S038)
- **Also:** "in a crowded marketplace like we're seeing now, the more that your brand stands for something I think that becomes more even more important." (S047)
- **Sources:** 7 (S009, S028, S038, S047, S056, S062, S112)

## 5. Curate excellence, not volume *(his phrase: "curate excellence")*
- **What it is:** More money can mean more shows, but never a lower bar. Every show must be one HBO would have made before the increase.
- **Parts:** No filler hours; growth without a factory culture; each show must work harder when you have fewer of them.
- **When to use:** Scaling output, a new owner asking for more, a platform hungry for hours.
- **How to score:** Test each title: would it have been made two or five years ago at the old budget? If no, it fails.
- **Quote:** "There are no plans to dilute the HBO brand in favor of volume of programming" (S028)
- **Also:** "There's not one show we aired that I wouldn't have aired two years or five years ago." (S049)
- **Sources:** 12 (S028, S029, S049, S055, S056, S062, S098, S103, S109, S114, S124, S125)

## 6. Think in slates *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A year is judged as a slate. Mix sizes, genres, tones and cycles so viewers always have something new and the service is not hostage to one kind of show.
- **Parts:** What is similar; what is missing; big tentpoles plus smaller family or contemporary shows; annual returners plus limited series; not all DC, not all one franchise.
- **When to use:** Choosing between projects; deciding what a studio's next project should be.
- **How to score:** A project scores higher when it fills a real gap in the slate and lower when it duplicates something already there.
- **Quote:** "What kinds of shows are we missing? It makes people prioritize. What do you really love? What do you want to bet on?" (S008)
- **Also:** "you try to have some diversity and variance in the slate so it's not all DC, so it's not all adult animation , it's not all acquired programming." (S004)
- **Sources:** 11 (S002, S004, S008, S047, S057, S067, S099, S112, S114, S115, S124)

## 7. The conversation test *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A premium show must make people talk, argue and have an opinion. Divisive is fine; ignored is not.
- **Parts:** Will critics and fans debate it; does it provoke; does it say something about how we live today.
- **When to use:** Judging an idea's cultural weight, or a show that splits critics.
- **How to score:** 5 if people would argue about it weekly; 3 if admired but quiet; 1 if nobody would mention it.
- **Quote:** "You have to do interesting shows that people do want to talk about." (S112)
- **Also:** "You want shows to be part of the conversation, you want them to provoke." (S095)
- **Sources:** 7 (S025, S028, S073, S095, S103, S112, S125)

## 8. Entertaining plus something to say *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The best HBO shows work on two levels: they entertain, and they say something about how we live now. Neither alone is enough.
- **Parts:** A gripping surface (a "page-turner"); a theme under it; a creator who can weave both.
- **When to use:** Testing a serious subject (is it a homework show?) or a pure genre ride (is there anything under it?).
- **How to score:** High when both levels are present and named; low when only one is.
- **Quote:** "A good show is entertaining, but the most successful shows have a lot to say about how we live today." (S025)
- **Also:** "Thematically, getting at something other than just entertaining." (S112)
- **Sources:** 5 (S025, S059, S103, S112, S056)

## 9. Quality beats genre fit: find the HBO version *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Do not ask whether a genre "feels like HBO". Ask what the HBO version of it would be, made with the same quality and a specific take on humanity.
- **Parts:** Name the genre (fantasy, teen, superhero, procedural); find a creator whose take lifts it; keep the common traits: well made, well written, well acted.
- **When to use:** A pitch in a genre the buyer has never done.
- **How to score:** High if the pitch says why this is the elevated version of the genre; low if it is the genre as usual.
- **Quote:** "if something is of high quality… [don't] get too hung up on, 'Does this genre feel like HBO?' That's where you can take creative chances." (S043)
- **Also:** "What's the HBO version of that?" (S112)
- **Sources:** 6 (S019, S043, S056, S062, S108, S112)

## 10. The pilot as an instrument; take your time *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Development and pilots exist to find out what the show really is. Recast, reshoot, merge episodes, or drop it. Never work backwards from an air date.
- **Parts:** Develop more slowly than rivals; shoot a pilot when the risk is high; read the cut; be willing to kill a good but not great pilot.
- **When to use:** Big or risky projects, first seasons, franchise extensions.
- **How to score:** Good if the plan leaves room to correct before full commitment; poor if a date or a deal forces a straight-to-series launch.
- **Quote:** "The pilot process is important and instructive," (S043)
- **Also:** "There wasn't anything glaringly wrong with it. Development and pilots are hard." (S142)
- **Sources:** 9 (S009, S029, S043, S056, S132, S142, S143, S144, S054)

## 11. Develop many options, greenlight few *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Develop several takes on a world or genre so the one you make is the best one. Development is not production, and most of it should not go.
- **Parts:** Multiple scripts per franchise; expect one in five to excite you; say no when it "just didn't come together."
- **When to use:** Any project in development with a buyer; any franchise.
- **How to score:** A slate with options scores higher than one project that must go.
- **Quote:** "out of five we'd be lucky to get one that we [would be] very, very excited about" (S029)
- **Also:** "The great thing about development is you just give yourself as many options as possible" (S062)
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S029, S040, S052, S062, S069, S111, S144)

## 12. Franchise discipline and scarcity *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A rich world earns a shot, but a slate must never depend on it. Count how many good shows the world really holds, make each its own show, and stop before it stops being special.
- **Parts:** Continue only if good; no spinoff to launch off a finale; a world must naturally lend itself to spinoffs; tentpole fatigue comes from repeating one universe.
- **When to use:** Sequels, spinoffs, IP extensions, "cinematic universe" plans.
- **How to score:** High if the extension is a different story with its own creator and tone; low if it repeats what worked.
- **Quote:** "if you overdo anything, if you give to people too much of anything, kind of by definition it becomes less special." (S011)
- **Also:** "I think tentpole fatigue comes from when you're telling the same story over and over within the same universe," (S058)
- **Sources:** 11 (S003, S004, S011, S052, S055, S058, S074, S095, S126, S132, S101)

## 13. Every dollar beyond the core needs a job *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Spending outside the flagship brand must push out to new, adjacent audiences, not copy the flagship. Labels exist to give latitude, not to blur the core.
- **Parts:** HBO stays HBO; the second label reaches adjacent viewers; each label has a clear purpose; admit when it was unclear.
- **When to use:** A second brand, a line extension, a cheaper format.
- **How to score:** High if the new line reaches people the core does not without changing the core; low if it is "HBO-adjacent" and only competes with the core.
- **Quote:** "any money I'm spending beyond that, I want to make sure that those shows have a job to do" (S008)
- **Also:** "These originals are designed to attract audiences that are adjacent to the typical HBO fans, while also feeding our strategy to deliver 52 weeks of new programming a year." (S010)
- **Sources:** 8 (S002, S008, S009, S010, S057, S062, S065, S123)

## 14. A world that generates story: the annual returner *(unnamed; 2025 to 2026)*
- **What it is:** The Pitt lesson. Build a setup where stories walk in the door each episode, at a cost that allows 15 episodes, run by people trained in long seasons, so the show returns every year and builds a habit.
- **Parts:** A fixed location; closed-end episodes you can watch alone; contained budget; experienced producers; annual return.
- **When to use:** Any series where volume and regularity matter more than spectacle; a mid-budget studio's pitch.
- **How to score:** 5 if the format can produce 15 or more episodes a year at a contained cost with a team that has done it; 1 if each season needs new invention, effects, or stars' schedules.
- **Quote:** "You know you have to set up first, a world that generates the story, and you also have to work with people who know how to do that, who've worked on it for years." (S062)
- **Also:** "That kind of habitual viewing, not only week to week, but on an annual basis, is really important in terms of setting habits," (S062)
- **Sources:** 11 (S002, S005, S009, S016, S039, S041, S059, S062, S067, S069, S092)

## 15. Find what is missing in the market *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** When rivals all chase the same thing, the opening is the thing they abandoned. In 2025 that was network-style drama, because everyone was copying HBO.
- **Parts:** Look at the landscape; see what nobody is making; check whether you have the people to make it.
- **When to use:** Positioning a project or a company against crowded competition.
- **How to score:** High if the project fills a real gap that buyers are not serving; low if it joins the crowd.
- **Quote:** "The success of The Pitt is because of what was missing in the marketplace." (S002)
- **Also:** "They've been busy chasing us doing that type of show, and there was an opportunity in the marketplace to go back to what networks historically did incredibly well." (S002)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S038, S062)

## 16. Be the must-have add-on, not the utility *(unnamed; 2024 to 2025)*
- **What it is:** Netflix is the utility, the basic cable of today. A premium service accepts that viewers also have Netflix and Amazon and wins by being specific about what only it offers.
- **Parts:** Stay in your lane; lead with originals and what people value; do not try to be everything.
- **When to use:** Competing with a much bigger player; positioning a small studio against giants.
- **How to score:** High if the offer is specific and hard to get elsewhere; low if it tries to match the giant on breadth.
- **Quote:** "[Netflix] is the basic cable of today" (S062)
- **Also:** "Netflix is big and established and is doing everything. We are leaning into what we do well as a company." (S015)
- **Sources:** 6 (S015, S038, S057, S058, S061, S062)

## 17. Local original first; you cannot engineer a traveler *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Make international shows for their home audience. A global breakout is a bonus. US tentpoles travel; local originals are built around them, and genre is the likeliest to break out.
- **Parts:** Serve the local market; partner with local broadcasters through co-production; watch for the rare show that crosses.
- **When to use:** Any non-US project seeking a premium buyer.
- **How to score:** High if the show is strong for its own country and has genre or a singular voice that could travel; low if it is built to please an imagined global viewer.
- **Quote:** "You can be aware of what travels historically, but you can't engineer it." (S061)
- **Also:** "For local people, local original programming is always going to be almost as important as the big tentpole shows." (S108)
- **Sources:** 8 (S002, S015, S061, S085, S100, S101, S108, S067)

## 18. Weekly release builds the talk *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Release weekly so critics and fans discuss each episode and word of mouth grows the audience.
- **When to use:** Release planning, and judging whether a show has enough in each episode to be talked about.
- **How to score:** A show with weekly talking points fits; one that only works as a binge fits less.
- **Quote:** "I am a huge, huge proponent of week-to-week viewing," (S112)
- **Also:** "we strongly believe in the weekly rollout model" (S009)
- **Sources:** 3 (S009, S062, S112)

## 19. No "next" hit, only the next great show *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Do not chase a replacement for the last hit. Replenish the slate years ahead, try more things, and accept that most will not be the next Game of Thrones.
- **Parts:** Plan for the end of a big show early; spread bets; resist the fear that paralyzes.
- **When to use:** After a hit, or when a studio wants to copy someone else's success.
- **How to score:** High if the plan spreads bets across different shows; low if it is a copy of the last success.
- **Quote:** "There's no next Sopranos , there's no next Game of Thrones , there's the next great show," (S029)
- **Also:** "Once you kind of accept that not everything is going to be Game of Thrones , between that and also having more resources to do more, by definition, you have to try more." (S112)
- **Sources:** 6 (S008, S029, S064, S068, S074, S112)

## 20. Good to great: the renew or cancel test *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** With limited resources, the question for a struggling show is whether it can go from good to great. If it would only move the needle a little, put the money elsewhere. Numbers matter at renewal.
- **Parts:** Is it landing; can a retool make it great; what else could the money do; if cancelled, help the creator find a new home.
- **When to use:** Continuing or stopping any project.
- **How to score:** Continue only with a credible path to great; stop if the best case is "a little better."
- **Quote:** "The tough decision is are we going to get this from good to great?" (S036)
- **Also:** "Nobody likes to cancel a show. But the fact of the matter is the numbers weren't there for a renewal," (S129)
- **Sources:** 8 (S028, S030, S032, S036, S071, S128, S129, S141)

## 21. Two of three: writer, director, performer *(his rule for comedy)*
- **What it is:** In comedy the tone is so specific that one person should control at least two of writing, directing and performing. HBO's comedy history is writer-performers with a point of view.
- **When to use:** Judging a comedy package or a personal series.
- **How to score:** High if one author controls two of the three; low if the voice is split across hired hands.
- **Quote:** "We try to get at least two of the three," (S037)
- **Also:** "What we're usually looking for is a point of view," (S062)
- **Sources:** 3 (S037, S061, S062)

## 22. Creative-driven, not data-driven *(his phrase)*
- **What it is:** HBO does not test pilots or pick shows by data. Data shows how people use the service; it does not choose stories. Success is judged by a blend of ratings, reviews and buzz, with no hard threshold.
- **When to use:** When someone proposes testing, algorithms or AI to choose or write shows.
- **How to score:** A plan that leads with the creative scores higher than one that leads with data about what "works."
- **Quote:** "I think we've always been a creative-driven company, not a data-driven company and I don't see that changing at all," (S100)
- **Also:** "We don't test shows at HBO. It's all about the quality of the show," (S025)
- **Sources:** 6 (S025, S038, S088, S095, S100, S009)

## 23. Windows: a show's life outside the house *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Expensive shows should earn in several windows, as syndication used to. License older seasons co-exclusively, keep recent hits home, and test free ad-supported outlets.
- **When to use:** Rights, licensing, a show's second life.
- **How to score:** High if the plan keeps the current hit exclusive and sells the library non-exclusively later; low if it gives away exclusivity or locks a library away forever.
- **Quote:** "The idea that they're going to sit in a library forever and ever for $15 a month, that's never how TV has operated." (S008)
- **Also:** "we're never giving anybody an exclusive right to a show." (S058)
- **Sources:** 4 (S008, S017, S018, S058)
