# Frameworks: Children's Publisher

Composite archetype. All costs are assumptions to check with printers and distributors.

## PUB-F1 Unit economics
What: print cost per copy at a given run, plus shipping, against retail price minus channel discount (bookshops and distributors commonly take 30 to 50 percent). The margin per copy pays back the fixed costs.

## PUB-F2 Break-even run
What: fixed costs (author, illustration, editing, design, proofs, ISBN, launch) divided by margin per copy. If break-even is above what the market can absorb in 12 months, reshape.
Score: break-even under half the first run = healthy. Above the first run = losing title unless pre-sold or funded.

## PUB-F3 Channel map
What: bookshops, chains, online, schools, libraries, fairs and events, diaspora organisations and shops abroad, direct sales via social media. For each: discount, payment terms, volume, returns.

## PUB-F4 Age-band fit
What: board books 0 to 3 (few words, sturdy format); picture books 3 to 6 (around 32 pages, read aloud); early readers 6 to 9 (short chapters, simple sentences); middle grade 9 to 12 (longer, series); young adult 12 to 16 (identity, stakes). Format and price follow the band.

## PUB-F5 List plan
What: the first five titles, how they connect (character, series, age band), and the release rhythm. A house is a list.

## PUB-F6 Print route
What: offset for larger runs (lower unit cost, cash up front), digital or print-on-demand for small runs and tests (higher unit cost, low risk). Choose by expected demand.

## PUB-F7 Pre-sale test
What: before printing, sell to schools, libraries or via crowdfunding or a pre-order post. Real orders beat opinions.

## PUB-F8 IP bridge
What: books as proof of a screen IP and screen projects as marketing for books. Keep both sets of rights together.
