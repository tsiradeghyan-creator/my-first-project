# Stories: Tom Quinn

19 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws, built from 79 sources (2003 to 2026). Source ids in parentheses. Numbers are as reported in those sources.

1. **Parasite, bought on the script.** Quinn had backed Bong Joon Ho since The Host (bought on a napkin at 3 or 4am after Directors' Fortnight, 2006), rescued Snowpiercer at Radius and lost Okja to Netflix. He committed to whatever Bong made next, bought Parasite before it was shot, held his bladder through the Cannes premiere, then released it the way Rugoff released Z: tiny opening, record per-screen average, wide later. About $20M spend for $54M in the US, the Palme, and four Oscars including best picture. Lesson: follow the director, protect the film, and refuse the foreign-film box (S053, S055, S066, S009, S072).

2. **I, Tonya: one shot, all the equity.** With about 15 people and all their equity in the company, Neon paid $6M at Toronto 2017 while Netflix offered three times as much. They planned the Oscar campaign before the deal was signed, led with a red-band trailer, bought LA TV as if it were a wide release, and grossed about $30M with an Oscar for Allison Janney. Lesson: win on understanding the film, then treat a small film as a big commercial one (S013, S015, S036, S070, S037).

3. **Ingrid Goes West opened the door.** A Sundance buy where Neon learned to market; Margot Robbie noticed the campaign, which put Neon at the table for I, Tonya. Lesson: do small films so well that the next filmmaker notices (S013, S036).

4. **Titane: signing a script he did not understand.** After Raw he flew to Paris, read Titane, told Julia Ducournau he did not understand it but believed in her, and signed a year and a half before Cannes. It won the Palme. Lesson: back the voice, not the page (S021, S025, S015).

5. **Longlegs: the boss said no.** Jason Wald showed Quinn a two-minute clip; Quinn said "You gotta be kidding. We're not doing this." (S013) Wald won. Neon hid Nicolas Cage (do not show the shark), used one phone-number billboard that drew about 1.5 million calls, opened on under $10M with no TV, and made $74M+ in the US, Neon's biggest. Forecast rose from $10M at pre-buy to $25M after seeing it. Lesson: let champions overrule you, and let signals set the spend (S013, S031, S036, S075, S060, S007).

6. **It Follows: the audience picked the window.** Bought at Cannes Critics' Week, opened in about 20 markets with no announced VOD date; a $6,000 Thursday at the Arclight said go wide, so Radius cancelled VOD. Grossed about $15M. Lesson: plan a switch and read the first nights (S015, S016, S050).

7. **Snowpiercer: Bong's cut, early VOD.** Harvey Weinstein wanted cuts; Bong refused; the film was handed to Radius, and Quinn insisted on Bong's cut. Opened against Transformers in eight cities, went to VOD after two weeks; he argues France comps proved the route right. Lesson: protect the director's cut and find a middle path between platform and wide (S066, S051, S019, S018).

8. **Citizenfour: risk you take as a team.** A sales agent asked him to lunch in Chinatown with no phones; code names, encryption, a boss who called Snowden a traitor. Quinn gave his ten-person team an equal vote to opt out; everyone raised their hand. Back-to-back doc Oscars at Radius. Lesson: some films are worth personal risk, and the team chooses it together (S020, S026).

9. **Bachelorette: not a dumping ground.** A $2M Sundance buy that grossed $4M on VOD before opening in cinemas and topped iTunes. Lesson: premium first-window VOD can serve theatrical-quality films if the cast backs it like a wide release (S032).

10. **Ferrari: a rescue that counts.** Michael Mann's self-financed film risked a straight-to-streaming dump; Neon paid $15M, spent over $17M on P&A, and got $18M in the US. Quinn says the model still worked with later windows and he would do it again. Lesson: stepping in for a master is a win, but he admits he wanted more (S007, S059, S064).

11. **Hit Man: the one that got away.** Neon offered $10M for US rights, a would-be record, forecasting $25M to $40M; Netflix paid $20M+ for several territories and gave it a token release. Lesson: losing a crossover film hurts exhibition, not only Neon (S007, S080, S059).

12. **Assassination Nation: the admitted overreach.** A $10M Sundance 2018 buy with AGBO that made about $2M. "It didn't work," (S069) They should have platformed it; partners limited the loss. Lesson: name the mistake and lay off risk on big swings (S069, S076).

13. **The Brutalist: letting a film go.** Neon wanted it, but Brady Corbet wanted 70mm prints for that year and Quinn felt the plan was rushed. Corbet went to A24. Quinn says Corbet made the right decision. Lesson: do not promise a release you cannot deliver well (S006, S043).

14. **Anatomy of a Fall: around the gate.** France did not submit it for the international Oscar, so Neon campaigned it for picture, director, actress, editing and screenplay, using the dog and a whodunit hook (did she do it?). It won original screenplay. Lesson: if the official route is blocked, run for the general categories (S049, S015, S006, S016).

15. **Anora: platform, then wide.** Bought before Cannes, a merch pop-up from a tow truck, a first screening full of sex workers rather than voters, about $18M on the campaign, then a wide release to reach people who do not read reviews. Five Oscars; post-Oscar home entertainment took it to 125 to 150 percent of plan. Lesson: play to the film and keep to your guns (S006, S067, S041).

16. **Memoria: one screen at a time.** Released as a never-ending tour, one theatre per city per week, never on home video. Lesson: for some films scarcity is the release plan (S047).

17. **Jiro's ten seats.** At Magnolia he bought Jiro Dreams of Sushi at a random Berlin screening; the ten-seat restaurant in a subway station became his model for Neon: never leave the subway, let the line grow. Lesson: depth over growth (S059, S017).

18. **Twenty years without a Cannes win.** As a publicist on Secrets and Lies and an executive on Melancholia (sunk by von Trier's press conference), he spent two decades not winning at Cannes, then won six and seven Palmes in a row. He still says he never expects to win and has regrets, like missing Shoplifters. Lesson: no pressure, no plan for prizes, only for films (S024, S063, S067, S062).

19. **Living in the office.** At 27 he could not afford rent on a Goldwyn salary, moved into an empty floor and learned every job from projectionist to acquisitions; it cost him his first marriage. Earlier he quit acting within weeks of reaching LA. Lesson: commitment and wanting control drove each step (S026, S014, S007, S053).
