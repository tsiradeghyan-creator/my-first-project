# Frameworks: Tom Quinn

22 frameworks, roughly most-used first, built from 79 sources (2003 to 2026): long interviews, festival talks and trade Q&As from his Samuel Goldwyn, Magnolia, Radius-TWC and Neon years. Quinn rarely names his own tools, so most names here are short descriptive ones marked *(unnamed)*. "Sources" counts distinct sources where the idea shows up. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council, built from his stated numbers and habits, not quotes.

---

## 1. The mantra: artistic merit, cultural relevance, a real point of view *(his word "mantra"; also "manifesto")*
- **What it is:** The filter for every Neon film. A film must have artistic merit and cultural relevance at the same time, and something to say. Neither half is enough alone.
- **Parts:** Auteur-driven; has a purpose or point of view; cultural heft (sometimes social impact); artistic merit in equal measure; an eye on the under-35 cinephile.
- **When to use:** First question of any acquisition or project pitch. Also when a slate is drifting toward "the trend of the day" (S006).
- **How to score:** 5 if a distinct director's voice and a reason the film matters now are both obvious from the script or cut; 3 if only one half is there (beautiful but says nothing, or topical but flat); 1 if it is a package built for a market slot.
- **Quote:** "We truly believe that artistic merit and cultural relevance should and must exist in equal measure in everything that we do. One is not distinct from the other." (S021)
- **Also:** "The entire manifesto is that all of our movies are built on artistic merit, cultural relevance and a real purpose of voice" (S010); "If we stick to a very clear mantra of who we are and what we want to represent, we will be successful in the aggregate," (S003)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S006, S010, S015, S021, S025, S059, S070)

## 2. Director first: follow the auteur, early and for life *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Neon buys directors as much as films. Pick the filmmakers you must work with, chase them early (often at script stage, before the festival invite) and stay with them across small and large films.
- **Parts:** Spot the voice on a first or second film (Bong with The Host, Ducournau with Raw); commit to whatever they make next; buy at script stage to beat competition; insist on the director's cut; let them go do a studio film and come back.
- **When to use:** Any project where the director is the main asset, a first-look or pre-buy decision, or a choice between a safer package and a stronger voice.
- **How to score:** High if the director already has one film that proves a singular voice and the team would back them blind; low if the director is interchangeable.
- **Quote:** "It's exceptionally competitive out there, so taking the risk before the film is done is an easier road to home. But more importantly for us, we're driven and attracted to directors." (S021)
- **Also:** "We were going to pursue whatever film Bong made post- Okja ." (S055); "it's your duty as a distributor to hold onto them." (S051); "Anything that has his name on it, we're happy to be a part of. I'll just give you a blank check." (S066)
- **Sources:** 11 (S008, S014, S021, S025, S051, S053, S055, S062, S066, S072, S075)

## 3. Champion buying: no groupthink *(his phrase, S036)*
- **What it is:** A film gets bought because one, two or three people will die on a hill for it, not because a committee averaged out. Unanimous comfort is a warning sign.
- **Parts:** The whole department-head team watches together; strong reactions count, lukewarm ones do not; a champion can overrule the boss (Longlegs); if everyone agrees, ask why.
- **When to use:** Greenlight or acquisition calls; any decision where a group is drifting to a safe middle.
- **How to score:** 5 if someone on the team is fighting for it with real passion; 3 if everyone likes it; 1 if nobody would be heartbroken to lose it.
- **Quote:** "We don't do groupthink," (S036)
- **Also:** "We're not sitting around and deciding by committee." (S013); "We don't always agree, and when we all agree, maybe that's the movie you don't buy." (S063)
- **Sources:** 6 (S013, S031, S036, S059, S063, S067)

## 4. Fit the release to the movie, never the movie to the release *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** There is no house release pattern. Each film gets its own plan, from one-screen tour to day-and-date to 2,000 screens, chosen after the film is understood.
- **Parts:** Be agnostic about model; read the audience; pick size and windows to match; change the plan if the audience says so.
- **When to use:** Every release plan; whenever someone says "this is a streaming film" or "this is a festival film" before anyone has asked who it is for.
- **How to score:** High if the plan follows from a clear audience and the film's nature; low if the plan was copied from the last hit.
- **Quote:** "Not fitting the movie into the release strategy, but doing the exact opposite." (S050)
- **Also:** "It really is a tailor-made distribution strategy for each and every film." (S042); "But every movie is its own film, and every release plan is unique to that film." (S009)
- **Sources:** 8 (S009, S027, S032, S042, S047, S048, S050, S062)

## 5. The Cannes-to-Oscar playbook *(he calls it "our playbook")*
- **What it is:** Launch in Cannes (ideally Competition), let the prize or the reviews build the runway, test in the San Fernando Valley, release in October, then run a long, steady campaign. Cannes juries overlap with the Academy, so Cannes is an early read on voters.
- **Parts:** Cannes premiere; Palme or other prizes (actor, screenplay, jury) as marketing; test screenings to measure the prize's pull; October release; six-month campaign while others are "burning hot and bright and quick" (S067).
- **When to use:** A festival strategy for an auteur film with awards hopes; deciding between May (Cannes) and the fall festivals.
- **How to score:** 5 if the film is a credible Competition title with a director festivals already trust; 3 if it fits a sidebar (Directors' Fortnight, Critics' Week, Un Certain Regard); 1 if it needs a market screening to be seen at all.
- **Quote:** "So, I think it makes perfect sense, combined with having a really nice lead time, to be able to build a significant campaign, to focus on the theatrical window, and position your film properly." (S029)
- **Also:** "I'm happy to share our playbook because it's so obvious." (S029); "That jury represents the Academy." (S067)
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S010, S011, S021, S029, S062, S063, S067)

## 6. The Palme as a ticket driver *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** His test screenings show the Palme d'Or moved from the fifth reason young audiences came (Parasite) to the first reason. A big festival prize is a consumer brand, not only an industry badge.
- **Parts:** Win or place at a top festival; test what drives attendance; put the prize at the front of the campaign for under-35 cinephiles.
- **When to use:** Judging whether a festival win will travel into ticket sales, especially for subtitled films.
- **How to score:** High for a top-tier prize from Cannes, Venice or Berlin aimed at young cinephiles; low for minor festival laurels nobody outside the trade recognises.
- **Quote:** "the number one reason given for why people came to the test screening in Burbank was because the film won the Palme d'Or." (S011)
- **Also:** "And having tested every subsequent Palme d'Or winner, winning that award was the number one reason why people came to see Parasite." (S021)
- **Sources:** 4 (S007, S011, S012, S021)

## 7. Let the audience decide the window *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Open in a limited footprint with no announced home window, read the first nights, then choose: go wide, or move to VOD. It Follows is the model.
- **Parts:** About 20 markets; no fixed VOD date; watch one key night (the Thursday at the Arclight did $6,000); cancel or keep the VOD window; tell partners.
- **When to use:** A genre or crossover film whose ceiling is unknown.
- **How to score:** High if the plan has a clear early signal and a pre-agreed switch; low if the release is locked before any audience has seen it.
- **Quote:** "The plan was: We take it theatrical in 20 markets, with no announced VOD window, and let the audience decide the next window," (S015)
- **Also:** "So look at the hard data and figure out how best to launch these movies. It's purely connected to the data, not something territorial." (S051)
- **Sources:** 5 (S015, S016, S018, S019, S051)

## 8. Theatrical first, and theatrical hits feed each other *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Cinema starts in a dark room with strangers. A theatrical launch is still the best way to cut through the noise, makes the film worth more when it reaches streaming, and every hit becomes a trailer slot for the next film.
- **Parts:** Release in cinemas, even small; use in-theatre trailers; let the film earn its later value in streaming; count rescued theatrical releases as wins.
- **When to use:** Any "should this skip theatres" debate; valuing a film for later windows.
- **How to score:** High if the film gives people a reason to leave home; low if it plays the same on a phone.
- **Quote:** "Cinema for us starts in the theater, a collective body of strangers coming together to see a director's vision ... unedited, uninterrupted ... and with that comes great power." (S039)
- **Also:** "And I firmly believe that the best way to break through the clutter is still a cinematic, theatrical launch for your film." (S066); "The in-theater experience and model is that all studios, all films cross-pollinate their success." (S059)
- **Sources:** 10 (S009, S014, S018, S022, S039, S048, S053, S059, S066, S077)

## 9. Sell it as cinema, not as a foreign film *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A subtitled film is positioned as top-tier cinema for everyone, not boxed into the international category. Give the audience comfort, then aim at every category and every audience.
- **Parts:** Refuse the "foreign" box; use comfort hooks (Parasite's trailer put its only English lines up front); a hook bigger than the genre (Anatomy of a Fall sold as a whodunit, did she do it?); let the director speak their own language; campaign beyond the international slot.
- **When to use:** A non-English film with crossover hopes; any film a country may not submit for the Oscar.
- **How to score:** High if the film has a hook any adult can grasp in one line; low if its only selling point is its origin.
- **Quote:** "I view our entire slate as equally valuable, regardless of whether a movie is subtitled or belongs to a certain genre," (S016)
- **Also:** "we put them front and center in the trailer to give audiences comfort," (S015); "It's important for us to have an intent of not serving the traditional audience for specialized art films," (S059)
- **Sources:** 8 (S015, S016, S049, S053, S055, S059, S066, S007)

## 10. The Rugoff opening: one theatre, then wide *(after Don Rugoff and Cinema 5's release of Z)*
- **What it is:** Open in one or very few theatres to post a huge per-screen average, let word of mouth and press build, then expand wide to reach people who do not read reviews.
- **Parts:** Tiny opening; record per-screen average; expansion; for Anora, a deliberate move from platform to wide.
- **When to use:** A festival winner with strong reviews and crossover potential.
- **How to score:** High if the film can sustain a long run on word of mouth; low if it depends on opening-weekend awareness.
- **Quote:** "Rugoff used to open everything in one theater in New York, and that was the inspiration for us to do the same, hoping to pop a massive per-screen-average." (S055)
- **Also:** "While we platformed it, we knew that we would move into a wide release, that we'd try to reach an audience that wasn't paying attention to the review-driven specialized independent sector" (S067)
- **Sources:** 4 (S009, S053, S055, S067)

## 11. Limitations are the mother of invention: no ego marketing *(his phrase)*
- **What it is:** With no studio P&A, Neon sells through brand, social, the rest of its slate, trailers in cinemas, and one billboard designed to be shared, not seen. Marketing should be its own artifact that adds to the film.
- **Parts:** Hide the shark (no Cage in the Longlegs campaign); breadcrumbs online; one cheap billboard built for social; in-theatre trailers; merch and stunts that cost almost nothing.
- **When to use:** Any launch on a small budget; deciding between broad media buys and one sharp idea.
- **How to score:** High if the campaign idea could travel on its own; low if it relies on spend and star faces.
- **Quote:** "Limitations are the mother of invention. We never had the ability or capacity to do it, so we had to learn and do something else." (S031)
- **Also:** "You don't do ego-based marketing." (S031); "The marketing isn't simply selling the film, it's additive; its own artifact." (S015); "I think marketing for us has to be its own performative entertainment. If we're just selling, it's no fun." (S021)
- **Sources:** 6 (S015, S021, S031, S060, S070, S075)

## 12. The decalogue: ten films a year, every one sold with a straight face *(his word "decalogue")*
- **What it is:** A small slate where every title gets full attention and every title is one the team believes in, so that liking one Neon film leads you to the other nine.
- **Parts:** About ten releases (later more, but never volume); 100 percent straight-face rule; small films as important as priorities; no quota.
- **When to use:** Slate planning; deciding whether to add a "filler" title.
- **How to score:** High if the title would sit proudly next to the best film on the slate; low if it is there to fill a date.
- **Quote:** "Most companies are in the 15 range, but I feel it's an inadequate amount of attention paid to a job that requires real attention to detail." (S037)
- **Also:** "That's not Neon. At this company, it's 100%." (S037); "every year we try to put out the perfect 'ten-film' catalog: If you like one of our movies, I think I can convince you to check out the other nine" (S015)
- **Sources:** 6 (S015, S032, S037, S066, S069, S074)

## 13. Seven reasons it will not work *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Before a release, the team writes down seven reasons the film will fail and shares them with the director.
- **Parts:** Honest list; shared with the filmmaker; campaign designed against each risk.
- **When to use:** Any plan that the room is too excited about.
- **How to score:** High if the team can name seven real risks and an answer to each; low if it cannot name three.
- **Quote:** "It forces us to be honest with ourselves, and forces the director to hear the movie's weaknesses," (S016)
- **Sources:** 1 (S016)

## 14. Triangulate the data set *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** No single number decides. Combine test scores, reviews, the awards prognosticators and comparisons with earlier films; for genre, watch unaided awareness among core fans. Re-forecast at every stage.
- **Parts:** Test every film (since Parasite); compare with prior winners; track core-fan awareness; raise or cut the target as evidence arrives (Longlegs: $10M at pre-buy, $25M after seeing it, more after the campaign).
- **When to use:** Setting a box office target, an awards plan, or a P&A level.
- **How to score:** High if the forecast changed when the evidence changed; low if it was set once and defended.
- **Quote:** "So, all of those things collectively, in any one silo, may not mean a lot. But if you take that data set, and you compare it to four other movies" (S029)
- **Also:** "Unaided awareness for horror fans was a 10 for the film," (S075); "We saw the film and upped that to a $25 million target." (S075)
- **Sources:** 4 (S011, S021, S029, S075)

## 15. The conservative model with full-life value *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Price the buy and the P&A so the film works at a cautious box office once home entertainment, pay-one and TV are counted. Box office is one stream, not the verdict.
- **Parts:** Conservative theatrical target; full-life revenue; know your spend-to-gross ratio (Parasite: $20M spend to $54M US); stay inside an annual budget; partner to lay off risk when stepping outside it.
- **When to use:** Setting an acquisition price or a P&A commitment; judging a "flop" headline.
- **How to score:** High if the deal works at the low case; low if it only works at the hoped-for case.
- **Quote:** "Everything that we do is calculated at a pretty conservative level." (S007)
- **Also:** "[Box office] is not the only revenue stream," (S006); "We operate with an annual budget just like everyone else, but if we wanted to step outside of that, we simply figure out how to mitigate the risk," (S069); "We eat everything that we hunt." (S031)
- **Sources:** 7 (S006, S007, S031, S041, S055, S064, S069)

## 16. Spend what the signals say; invest as you succeed *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** P&A is released step by step against evidence, not committed at the maximum. The awards calendar lets you spend more only when the last round worked.
- **Parts:** Daily tracking of interaction; stop short of the cap when signals are strong (Longlegs opened on $10M with $15M available); local LA TV for voters; consumer-facing over trade ads.
- **When to use:** Launch budgets and awards campaigns.
- **How to score:** High if spend has gates tied to results; low if the whole budget is committed before release.
- **Quote:** "We spent $10M opening that film. We could have spent 15, but we only spent 10 because we knew we didn't need to." (S031)
- **Also:** "The way that the calendar is set up, we could invest as we succeeded." (S070); "Ultimately for me, consumer-facing is more impactful to Academy voters than navel-gazing trade advertising." (S070)
- **Sources:** 3 (S031, S070, S075)

## 17. The campaign commitment: the human decision *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** An awards or festival campaign takes over everyone's life for six months. Ask the director to choose it consciously, then hold steady through losses at other shows.
- **Parts:** Ask for the human decision; set demands and limits up front; conviction and stamina; keep the plan when you lose a precursor.
- **When to use:** Before committing to any festival-plus-awards route.
- **How to score:** High if the director and lead are willing and available for months; low if they are unavailable or half in.
- **Quote:** "I want you to make a human decision about what's important to you," (S055)
- **Also:** "You have to have the conviction and stamina to stick with the whole campaign," (S006); "It's a marathon." (S029)
- **Sources:** 5 (S006, S029, S055, S063, S067)

## 18. Jiro's subway counter: a cap on size *(after Jiro Dreams of Sushi)*
- **What it is:** Do one specific thing very well and let the line get longer, instead of growing at all costs. Neon has a ceiling on budgets (Snowpiercer, under $35M) and on company size.
- **Parts:** Budget cap; no $50M films; independence; no mergers.
- **When to use:** Growth, scale and budget decisions; any "go bigger" proposal.
- **How to score:** High if the plan deepens the core; low if it trades identity for scale.
- **Quote:** "Being one very specific thing and doing that one thing very well was more exciting and interesting than the idea of growing at all costs, of serving everybody." (S059)
- **Also:** "We will never change. We will never leave the subway. But the line will get longer and longer," (S017); "I think if we are large enough as a company to do movies in excess of $50 million, we are going to forget the core mission of doing movies." (S021)
- **Sources:** 5 (S017, S021, S024, S059, S062)

## 19. The amoeba: speed as an asset *(his image)*
- **What it is:** A small, organised team that decides in the room and moves in days (Pig greenlit ten days before launch; I, Tonya's Oscar campaign planned before the deal was signed).
- **Parts:** Department heads at the screening; decision on the call; no black box; ideas from anyone.
- **When to use:** Competitive bids; fast-moving releases; choosing partners.
- **How to score:** High if the decision-maker is in the room; low if the answer "goes back to the committee".
- **Quote:** "I always describe Neon as this very organized, highly-attuned and well-functioning amoeba," (S025)
- **Also:** "The decision doesn't disappear into a black box or go through a morass of corporate red tape." (S059); "And because we can make quick decisions, I think speed is one of our greatest assets." (S021)
- **Sources:** 6 (S007, S013, S021, S024, S025, S059)

## 20. The dead zone between platform and wide *(unnamed; his Radius-era thinking)*
- **What it is:** Specialised film lives at 500 to 600 prints and under. A TV-driven release on 900+ screens for a specialised film rarely pays; find a third way (premium VOD in the first window, platform plus super-wide availability).
- **Parts:** Size the release to the real audience; avoid $10M+ TV spends for $15M grosses; use the first window while the film is being talked about.
- **When to use:** Mid-sized films with no obvious lane; deciding P&A for an art film.
- **How to score:** High if the plan reaches the audience at the moment of reviews without a studio spend; low if it is a wide release on hope.
- **Quote:** "if I have to release a film with a TV spend in order to get to my audience on a minimum 900 prints, it's sort of a dead zone, a dangerous place to be." (S032)
- **Also:** "There can't be just two ways to release a movie," (S018); "One common denominator of what I call specialized film is any release that is six hundred prints or under." (S074)
- **Sources:** 6 (S018, S019, S032, S042, S051, S074)

## 21. The younger art audience: no aversion to violence *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** His target audience is a younger art-house crowd, shaped first at genre festivals: like a traditional art audience but younger, unafraid of violence, open to subtitles and documentaries. Gen Z now over-indexes in cinemas.
- **Parts:** Genre that works as art; festival stamps (Fantastic Fest); go where people say the audience is hardest to reach.
- **When to use:** Judging whether a film can reach under-35 viewers; genre-meets-arthouse projects.
- **How to score:** High if a 25-year-old cinephile would argue about it after; low if it needs a 60-plus art-house crowd to survive.
- **Quote:** "I think it's not unlike a traditional art audience. It's just younger, and it has no aversion to violence and, frankly, has a much wider interest in genre movies" (S050)
- **Also:** "People say that's the hardest age to reach, so I thought that's where the opportunity lies as well," (S037); "Gen Z, Gen Alpha, is the most over-indexing theatrical audience for me" (S023)
- **Sources:** 5 (S023, S035, S037, S050, S062)

## 22. Signs that spell success (advice to filmmakers seeking distribution) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** From the buyer's side of the table: a film sells when it is at the right festival, in the right section, with the right materials and word of mouth before anyone screens it. Marketing is half the job, and the filmmaker must stay involved.
- **Parts:** Festival and section choice; trailer, stills, press kit ready; buzz before the screening; filmmaker engaged in the release.
- **When to use:** When the studio is the seller: festival route, sales agent, timing.
- **How to score:** High if all four are planned before the premiere; low if the plan is "premiere and hope".
- **Quote:** "It's being at the right festival, being in the right section, having the right materials, having the right word-of-mouth before the movie ever gets screened." (S074)
- **Also:** "Making the movie is, unfortunately, half the process. The other half is marketing it." (S074)
- **Sources:** 1 (S074)
