# Positions: Tom Quinn

34 topics, built from 79 sources (2003 to 2026). Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id and date (publication date; see `sources.md`). Where his view moved, the versions sit side by side with dates and the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

## What to buy (taste)
- **Stance:** Auteur films with a point of view, cultural relevance and artistic merit, in any genre or language. "auteur-driven films that have purpose, something to say. Cultural heft, in some cases social impact, but also artistic merit in equal measure." (S003, 2025-03)
- **Consistent since 2003:** at Goldwyn he bought only what fit his taste, no quota: "since we don't have a quota to fill, it's very much in line with our taste, and we will not compromise that." (S074, 2003-06)

## Buying at script stage versus finished film
- **Stance:** Pre-buy the directors you want before the festival; buy finished films on the ground when they knock you out. Parasite and Titane were bought at script stage; Triangle of Sadness and Anatomy of a Fall during Cannes (S004, 2024-05). "None of these movies were bought with an invite to Cannes, and many of the movies that we bought over the years were bought at script stage." (S021, 2026-06)
- **Favourite move:** "My favorite thing is is to buy the movie during the screening, sending an offer by email and and demanding that the deal be closed by the end of the movie." (S013, 2025-10)

## Acquisition price and bidding wars
- **Stance:** Bid what your model and conviction support; lose rather than overpay. Lost Hit Man ($10M offer, US only, against Netflix's $20M+ for several territories), Late Night ($12.5M offer), The Farewell, Talk to Me (S007, 2024-09; S069, 2019-02). "We're able to compete with the Amazons and the Netflixes when we think it makes sense." (S069, 2019-02)
- **On rivals' big buys:** "That's a press release, right? That's not buying movies. And good for them." (S067, 2025-05)
- **Exception:** paid what a foreign film rarely gets when the director mattered: Parasite "for what at the time was quite a large sum for a foreign-language film." (S055, 2025-02)

## Festivals: which one launches a film
- **Stance:** Cannes first. "In this era of streaming and Covid and strikes, [Cannes] still is the beacon which drives this industry," (S004, 2024-05) "France for me represents, I think, the safest harbor for cinema." (S029, 2025-05)
- **Fall circuit:** Venice, Telluride, Toronto and New York set up the season; show up at your screenings (S007, 2024-09).
- **Sidebars and genre fests:** bought It Follows and Blue Ruin at Cannes Critics' Week; Fantastic Fest gives genre films their "stamp of approval" (S050, 2014-09). Sundance for docs and discoveries (S022, 2020-01).

## The Palme d'Or and festival prizes
- **Stance:** A top prize is now a consumer brand for young cinephiles, not only trade currency; but you cannot plan for it. "I had no idea that 'Parasite' was going to win," (S035, 2026-06) "No one ever believes it, but we've never gone to Cannes thinking we were going to win the Palme d'Or," (S062, 2026-05)
- **Prize power (testing):** "We've seen over the years that the Palme d'Or has come to mean something quite significant for young cinephiles." (S011, 2023-10)

## Oscar and awards campaigns
- **Stance:** Long, steady, consumer-facing campaigns; play to the film, not the season. "We never play to the campaign. We always play to the film, filmmaker and audience ... in that order." (S006, 2025-03)
- **Spend:** about $20M for Parasite to $54M US (S055, 2025-02); about $18M for Anora (S006, 2025-03); lead actor campaign minimum about $2M (S070, 2018-03). "Ultimately for me, consumer-facing is more impactful to Academy voters than navel-gazing trade advertising." (S070, 2018-03)
- **Academy membership:** international voters a swing bloc under 25 percent; newer, younger voters watch more (S029, 2025-05).

## The international feature Oscar process
- **Stance:** Broken. "This is not 'Eurovision,' it's not one song represents one country." (S049, 2023-10) His workaround: campaign the film in the general categories (Anatomy of a Fall won screenplay) (S049).

## Foreign-language films
- **Stance:** Treat them as cinema for everyone, not a niche. "I view our entire slate as equally valuable, regardless of whether a movie is subtitled or belongs to a certain genre," (S016, 2024-06) About half of the early slate was foreign or documentary: "Roughly 60 percent of the films that we have released today are equally split between foreign-language films and documentaries." (S007, 2024-09)
- **Patience:** year-one foreign films "didn't work to the level that they have this year" (S009, 2020-02); he kept buying them.

## Documentaries
- **Stance:** A firm believer, contrarian when others pull back. "I want the first documentary in best picture." (S038, 2021-02) Bought 70 Up in 2026: "I defy you not to cry seeing this incredible film." (S023, 2026-06)
- **Exception:** Brats went straight to streaming to move fast (S007, 2024-09).

## Genre and horror
- **Stance:** Always part of the slate; genre is not a lesser category. "We've always been into horror." (S021, 2026-06) "I think dismissing genre films as its own category is a mistake, and it falls in line with our mantra to be agnostic." (S007, 2024-09)
- **Action (newest):** "I can't wait to do our character driven, world building, genre-defying action film." (S035, 2026-06)

## Animation
- **Stance:** Neutral on the form, decides on theme and storytelling (Flee, Robot Dreams): "completely agnostic about animation." (S016, 2024-06)

## Comedy
- **Stance:** Wants comedies back in cinemas (Splitsville, Palm Springs) (S067, 2025-05; S038, 2021-02). Thin evidence beyond that.

## Theatrical versus streaming
- **Stance:** Theatrical first, streaming as a later, more valuable window. "It is a symbiotic relationship. One benefits the other." (S009, 2020-02) "I firmly believe that the best way to break through the clutter is still a cinematic, theatrical launch for your film." (S066, 2019-11)
- **Pandemic:** "There's no such thing as virtual cinema" (S014, 2020-05), yet released through drive-ins, pop-ups and virtual cinemas.

## Windows and VOD (view moved)
- **2011 to 2014 (Radius):** multiplatform and early VOD for art and genre films. "That this is not a dumping ground." (S032, 2012-09) "For me, the independent theatrical space is 500 prints and under," (S042, 2011-09)
- **2014:** windows by data per film: "It's purely connected to the data, not something territorial." (S051, 2014-07)
- **2021:** collapsing windows helped provocative films in the pandemic: "simply by changing the release pattern, being able to collapse the window, we've actually ironically found more success." (S025, 2021-08)
- **2025 to 2026 (newer):** theatrical runs come first, premium VOD later; Anora's post-Oscar bump came mostly from home entertainment (S067, 2025-05).
- **Read:** not a reversal so much as a shift of default. The Radius view was "the first window when everybody is talking" (S032); the Neon view is "the best, longest possible theatrical release" (S003), with home windows used to extend it.

## Netflix and the streamers as competitors
- **Stance:** Netflix, not A24, was the main rival for the first six years. "Netflix has been our biggest competitor from day one," (S001, 2021-03) "There's a slew of movies where I'm the second-highest bidder [to Netflix]." (S006, 2025-03)
- **Fair to them:** credits Netflix for financing Okja (S066, 2019-11).

## A24
- **Stance:** Not a rivalry. "We pick up movies that they walked away from, and vice versa, but we're not the same," (S043, 2025-03) "But I don't understand their business and their valuations." (S043, 2025-03)
- **Merger idea (2026):** "how you would you feel like if A24 and Neon merged? That would be ridiculous," (S024, 2026-05)

## Algorithms and data
- **Stance:** Uses testing and tracking hard, rejects algorithmic taste. "The uberization of entertainment. The algorithm. I want no part in it." (S021, 2026-06) On streamer metrics: "Why not just cut a trailer and call it a day? Take the trailer that wins the most views and go make that movie." (S009, 2020-02)

## Testing and research
- **Stance:** Tests every film since Parasite; tracks unaided awareness and social signals. "Post Parasite , we test all of our movies." (S021, 2026-06)

## Marketing and P&A
- **Stance:** No ego marketing; one sharp idea over broad buys; marketing as its own artifact. "You don't do ego-based marketing." (S031, 2025-09) Longlegs opened on under $10M with no TV (S031, S075).
- **Radius era (2014):** minimum TV campaign $3M to $5M or none: "spend any less and it's a moot endeavor" (S018, 2014-07).

## Production and budgets (view moved)
- **2003 (Goldwyn):** produce films at $5M to $10M (S074).
- **2020:** Neon starts producing, under $10M, Moonlight to Hereditary range (S053, S054, 2020-02).
- **2026 (newer):** a cap near Snowpiercer's under-$35M budget; never $50M+. "I think if we are large enough as a company to do movies in excess of $50 million, we are going to forget the core mission of doing movies." (S021, 2026-06)

## International sales
- **Stance (2024):** Built Neon's own sales arm because big agencies would not represent an eclectic slate well. "with the range of our films and eclectic nature of our slate, I don't know that we would have been accurately represented." (S010, 2024-05)

## Raising money and investors (view moved)
- **2015 to 2017:** two years to raise; $15M from SR Media (S015, S036).
- **2018 to 2019:** 30West majority stake let Neon bid hard: "30West's investment and support has allowed us to be aggressive at festivals" (S022, 2020-01)
- **2025:** "We eat everything that we hunt." No outside equity since (S031, 2025-09).
- **2026 (newest):** sold a significant stake to Department M and launched Neon TV; Quinn stays CEO (S052, S078, 2026-07). No recorded quote from him on that deal.

## Selling the company
- **Stance:** For sale like everyone, but no exit plan. "Every independent company is for sale," (S006, 2025-03) "Sadly, we're all for sale," (S031, 2025-09) "This is what I love, this is my passion, it's my pursuit" (S028, 2026-01)

## Debt and scale
- **Stance:** Debt and size kill creative risk. "I don't know how you can make good creative decisions when you're dealing with billions of debt looming at your door." (S062, 2026-05)

## Consolidation and competition
- **Stance:** Against mergers; contraction hurts the business but opens a lane. "The lack of competition is bad," (S024, 2026-05) "I'm greatly concerned about the amount of contraction in the industry or lack of competition, but it has opened a lane for us." (S028, 2026-01)

## Slate size (focus)
- **Stance:** Not the volume business. Magnolia did 40 a year, too many; Radius aimed at 15 to 18; Neon at launch ten. "We don't want to be in the volume business." (S032, 2012-09) "We're doing 10 movies a year," (S037, 2018-02)

## Team, hiring and culture
- **Stance:** Small, senior, cross-trained team that decides in the room; hire people you have worked with; empower champions. "comfort and a certain amount of synergy that I think is super important for any startup." (S036, 2025-09) Grew from 12 to 52 people by 2024 for production, wide releases and sales (S007).
- **Weak spot:** early Neon outsourced accounting and marketing to Alamo Drafthouse; a lawsuit exposed errors (S057, 2026-04).

## Young audiences and creators
- **Stance:** The under-35 and Gen Z audience is the opportunity. "Gen Z is the most over indexing theatrical audience" (S035, 2026-06) On YouTube-born hits: "I don't see it as YouTube or otherwise; I see it as cinema," (S023, 2026-06)

## Exhibition and theatres
- **Stance:** Partner and champion of cinemas; wants windows and VOD players to cooperate with them. "The magic of cinema begins in your cinemas." (S048, 2019-04) "sometimes the best character in the movie is an energized audience." (S016, 2024-06)

## Box office reporting
- **Stance:** Report it, including VOD (Radius, 2014) and for every Oscar contender (2021). "You do have to launch it theatrically and should be required to report your box office." (S001, 2021-03)

## Television and series
- **2014:** a fan of longform, open to it, not core: "It's not our core business, but there are definitely opportunities based on the filmmakers we work with." (S018, 2014-07)
- **2026 (newest):** Neon TV launched with the Department M deal (S052, 2026-07). No recorded view from Quinn on what Neon TV will do.

## Free expression and politics
- **Stance:** Film is sacred and political; executives must hold the line. "We think it's sacred at Neon. We think it's a church." (S026, 2025-09)

## Working with difficult talent and disputes
- **Stance:** Meets contracts and goes beyond, defends his team in public, does not engage in fights. On Origin: "not only did we fulfill all of our contractual obligations, but we exceeded all of our contractual obligations." (S007, 2024-09) On Amazing Grace: "I recall that we urged Alan to audit us," (S057, 2026-04)

## AI
- No recorded position.

## Pricing tickets, remote work, work hours
- No recorded position. On campaign hours he says only that a campaign "takes over your life" (S055).
