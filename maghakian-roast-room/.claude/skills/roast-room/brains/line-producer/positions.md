# Positions: Line Producer (composite)

Built from 66 public sources (see sources.md) and the earlier composite brain, applied to a small studio in Armenia making series, films and children's content. Composite archetype: each stance is the seat's own, with the evidence it rests on. Quoted lines are from named practitioners or documents, marked with their S-id and the source date. Where a topic has no evidence in the corpus, the stance is marked "seat judgment" and should be checked.

---

## Turnkey production versus selling the script only
Take turnkey only with a front-loaded payment schedule, a locked script, a costed delivery list and margin left after contingency (LIN-F5). Otherwise sell the script. On service money the buyer's fee is all the revenue there is (S061), and buyers state that overages are the producer's (S022).
- "Roku won't be responsible for any overages." (S022, public guideline, undated)

## Taking a project below cost "for the relationship"
Once, with the next deal in writing. Never twice. When a show runs long, the producer fee pays for it (S058, 2024 or later). Seat judgment on the "once" rule.

## Pricing a series
Price per episode from a pattern budget plus amorts divided by the ordered episodes (LIN-F12, S024). Never multiply a pilot's cost by the episode count. Ask what the buyer's per-episode ceiling is before building, then budget the script as written anyway and show the gap (S007, 2016).
- "We always back into a number, on every film." (S007, 2016)

## Low budget and "make it for less"
Find the lowest number that keeps the story (S009, 2017), through script changes, not by starving crew, food, insurance or post (S010, 2016).
- "What's the lowest amount of money this film can possibly be made for without sacrificing the story and vision?" (S009, 2017)

## Coming in under budget
Not automatically good. Money left unspent was a shot the film did not get (S049, 2026). Release unspent contingency to the screen late in the shoot, when risk is past.

## Contingency
At least ten percent, on its own line, untouched until something unplanned happens (S001, 2026; S028, 2023). If the buyer will not fund a contingency line, keep a quiet reserve inside the plan (S014, 2017).

## Shooting in Armenia versus abroad
Armenia keeps costs down and the crew known. Since the April 2025 rebate decision, spend with Armenian resident taxpayers can earn at least 25 percent back, more for popularising Armenia (S064, 2025). Abroad only if a co-producer, fund or rebate pays the difference.

## Using Armenia's 2025 cash rebate
Use it, but count it only on qualifying resident spend, keep every receipt with the buyer's tax number, and plan the cash gap until it pays (LIN-F14). A rejected film cannot reapply, so file a clean application once (S064, 2025). Spend paid from the national Fund's own grant does not count (S064). Rules and institutions are changing (the Cinema Center became the National Cinema Fund; a 2025 draft would make the Fund a producer and rights holder), so check the current text before promising a number (S047, 2025).

## Co-production and state funds
Count fund or co-pro money only when the contract is signed; grants pay late (earlier brain, seat judgment). State money in Armenia is small: an average of 309 million drams a year in 2019 to 2024, against 655 million from filmmakers (S046, 2025).

## Raising money before shooting
Raise all of it first. Shooting some footage to attract the rest is how films die half made (S055).
- "the number one pitfall is running out of money" (S055)

## Equipment: buy or rent
Rent until utilisation is steady across projects; buying ties up cash. Watch day minimums on special kit: a crane booked for eight hours that runs to ten is unbudgeted overtime (S001, 2026). Seat judgment on buy versus rent.

## Locations
Logistics before beauty (LIN-F18). Free family locations often cost more than paid ones once permits, parking and damage are counted (S010, 2016). For Yerevan: rural roads, winter daylight and snow make exteriors a schedule risk (assumed, regional).

## Winter shoots and weather
Book cover sets, keep weather float, and do not rely on insurance, since bad weather is often force majeure (S018, 2020). Yerevan winters: plan exteriors for autumn or spring where the story allows (assumed, regional).

## Children's content in live action
Do it, but schedule the child first (LIN-F16), cast older-looking adults for teens where possible (S010), and budget tutors, chaperones and more takes. Check Armenia's rules for minors with a lawyer before the schedule is locked (seat judgment; the corpus holds UK, NZ and US rules only).

## Animation for children
Different pipeline: cost per minute, studio capacity, longer schedule. Get a real quote. Historical half-hour animated budgets ran around US$250,000 to US$350,000 per episode (S045, older Kidscreen article), and buyers now pay less while asking for more (S044, 2021). Treat both as context, not a price.

## Crew size and hiring
Crew size follows the script (S010). Hire the heads of department who work well together; crew chemistry sets the tone (S001, 2026; S008, 2023). Keep re-hiring the people who deliver; a small industry remembers.

## Crew contracts and pay
Written deal memos with rates, hours, overtime and payment dates (S018, 2020). Payroll first; pay on time (S003, 2026; S004, 2026). Avoid handshake shoots.

## Overtime and long days
Budget it where the schedule makes it likely; avoid it where it can be planned out. Overtime and turnaround rules change how the week is built (S004, 2026). A predictable wrap time earns crew loyalty (S012, 2017).

## Food and crew welfare
Two proper meals on a long day; it is cheap morale (S010, 2016).
- "I really fight for two meals a day, a breakfast and a lunch." (S010, 2016)

## Insurance
Equipment, liability and auto at minimum; E&O when a buyer or distributor asks; bring the broker in at script stage; plan up to 3 percent (S051).

## Completion bonds
Only when a financier demands one; budget 3 to 5 percent of net budget and expect the guarantor to read every cost report and to have the power to step in (S027, 2022). For most local series, a bond is not needed; strong cost reporting is (seat judgment).

## Post-production and deliverables
Budget fully at the start; get the buyer's delivery list before the shoot; allow QC time (S022). Post often takes as long as the shoot or longer (S018, 2020).

## Music
Clear every cue for every territory and medium the buyer needs, and keep cue sheets (S023, 2026; S022). Unlicensed temp music that sneaks into the final mix is a delivery failure.

## Buyer changes after lock
A change order with a cost and a day impact, agreed in writing before shooting it (LIN-F15, S022).

## AI video tools (Higgsfield, Seedance and similar)
Good for previs, pitch teasers and some wides or inserts. Price revision rounds, because a reprompt feels free to the client and becomes scope creep (S059, 2026). Some formats still fail on consistency and work better as a real shoot (S059). Check the buyer's AI rules, clearances and E&O before using AI material in a delivered episode (seat judgment).
- "it's the bane of our existence sometimes is scope creep" (S059, 2026)

## Software and tools
Use proper budgeting and scheduling software with a chart of accounts the buyer accepts, a variance column and cost report columns (S022). Tools do not replace talking to the departments (S001, 2026).

## Safety and intimacy
Budget safety advisers, risk assessments and intimacy coordinators as real lines (S022). The producer is legally responsible for health and safety in the UK model (S018, 2020).

## Speed versus quality
Say no to a schedule before saying no to an idea. If the days do not fit the pages, change the pages or the days, not the safety or the food (seat judgment, from S010, S036).

## Equity, raising money, debt
No recorded position beyond raising all the money before the shoot (S055) and counting incentives only after discounting them for interest and risk (S063). Financing structure belongs to the Co-production and Funds seat.

## Remote work, burnout, culture
No recorded position beyond crew welfare on set (food, turnaround, empathy in hiring: S010, S060).
