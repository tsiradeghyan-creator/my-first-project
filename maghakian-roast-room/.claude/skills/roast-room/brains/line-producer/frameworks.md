# Frameworks: Line Producer (composite)

Built from 66 public sources (293k words, 2013 to 2026): interviews with working line producers, UPMs, production accountants and finance executives; guild and institution guides (PGA, ScreenSkills, PMA); a streamer's public production guidelines; budgeting, cost report, bond, insurance and clearance guides; child performer rules; and Armenia's 2025 cash rebate decision. See sources.md.

This seat is a composite archetype, not a person. The seat speaks in its own words and cites these LIN ids. Quoted lines below are evidence from named practitioners or documents, marked with their S-id, so each method is traceable. The seat never voices them as its own words. Numbers marked "assumed" are working assumptions for Armenia and the region, to be checked against real quotes.

20 frameworks, roughly most used first. Ids LIN-F1 to LIN-F8 keep the numbering of the earlier brain; LIN-F9 onward are new. "Sources" counts distinct sources that use the idea. "How to score" lines are scoring guidance for the council, not quotes.

## LIN-F1 Day count (script to shooting days)
- **What it is:** The first number. Convert pages and format into shooting days, then let days drive cost.
- **Steps:** Read once for the story, once to tag elements. Break down every scene (cast, locations, props, costume, SFX, VFX, kids, animals, vehicles). Strip it into a one-line schedule. Count days, company moves, nights, and day minimums on special kit. Only then price it.
- **Working assumptions (assumed, regional):** daily or volume series on standing sets 12 to 20 pages a day; weekly drama 6 to 10; feature 3 to 5; children's live action fewer, because of child hours (LIN-F16). The same 10 or 12 hour day can hold 2 pages or 5, so pages per day is a target, not a law.
- **When to use:** Every production question. A budget without a day count is a guess.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if a 1st AD or line producer has stripped the script and the pages per day sit inside the range for the format; 3 if days are estimated from page count alone; 1 if the days were set by the money available.
- **Evidence:** "once the schedule is made, now you have all the elements and the information to then put it into a budget and assign numerical financial values to that" (S001). "That might mean 2 pages one day, 5 the next ." (S035). "The most common reason I've come across for scheduling and shooting ten or more pages in a single day comes down to budget ." (S036)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S010, S012, S018, S026, S033, S034, S035, S036)

## LIN-F2 Top sheet: above and below the line
- **What it is:** The budget's one-page summary in four levels: above the line (writers, director, producers, lead cast), below the line (crew, equipment, locations, transport, catering), post, and other (insurance, legal, amorts, contingency, bond, overhead).
- **Parts:** Know each level's share before negotiating. Rough shares from one guide: above the line about 30 percent, below the line about 45 percent, plus post and other (S028). Contingency and bond sit on their own lines.
- **When to use:** Reading any budget handed to the studio, and pricing a turnkey offer.
- **How to score (guidance):** high if every level has detail behind it; low if the top sheet has round numbers with no detail. A streamer's rule: "Avoid large flat allowances. Provide details instead (cost per day/week/month etc.)" (S022)
- **Evidence:** "it's a budget, every line, every dollar is somewhere that makes up that bigger number" (S001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S018, S022, S024, S028, S031)

## LIN-F3 Cash-flow calendar
- **What it is:** A week-by-week table of money in (buyer milestones, rebate, grants, co-pro installments) against money out (prep, shoot weeks, post, deliverables). The lowest week is the real risk.
- **Parts:** Payment milestones as the deal sets them (for example on start and on completion of principal photography, with a premium or holdback released only after paper deliverables and the final cost report: S022). Weekly outflow from the schedule and PO log. Bridge for every negative week.
- **When to use:** Any deal where the buyer, fund or rebate pays after the crew must be paid.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if every week is positive or bridged by signed money; 3 if one negative week is bridged by a named source; 1 if the plan needs money that is not yet signed.
- **Evidence:** "it doesn't matter where the money comes from, as long as it's in the bank when we need to access it" (S011). "The big key to production cash flow and PO log success on any project is a thorough script breakdown ." (S026)
- **Sources:** 6 (S011, S022, S025, S026, S055, S063)

## LIN-F4 Risk register
- **What it is:** The ten things that stop a shoot, each with likelihood, cost and plan B: lead cast unavailable, location lost, weather, permits, equipment, illness, late script, late payment, key crew leaves, power or transport.
- **Parts:** Weather is often not insured (force majeure), so it needs cover sets or schedule float (S018). Cars are the most common insurance claim (S051). Locations fall through at the last minute (S010).
- **When to use:** Before committing a date or a fee.
- **How to score (guidance):** count the top risks with no plan B. Zero is good; three or more is a no.
- **Evidence:** "things always go wrong at various points during a shoot" (S018). "bad weather comes under the terms "force majeure" and therefore you may not be covered if filming has to cease due to inclement weather" (S018)
- **Sources:** 6 (S008, S010, S015, S018, S033, S051)

## LIN-F5 Turnkey margin check
- **What it is:** Turnkey fee minus full production cost, minus contingency, minus a price for delivery risk (overages, QC failure, late payment, buyer changes). What is left is the real margin.
- **Parts:** On a turnkey or service deal the buyer's money is the total revenue; production cost is the cost of sales; what is left is profit (S061). Many buyers state that overages are the producer's (S022), and when a show needs more days the only place it can come from is the producer fee (S058).
- **When to use:** Every turnkey or commissioned series offer.
- **How to score (guidance):** remainder under 10 to 15 percent of the fee means the deal is a loan to the buyer (assumed threshold, carried from the earlier brain).
- **Evidence:** "Roku won't be responsible for any overages." (S022). "you need more days and the only place it can come from is the producing fee" (S058)
- **Sources:** 4 (S022, S044, S058, S061)

## LIN-F11 Cost report (actuals, commitments, estimate to complete)
- **What it is:** The central money document, weekly. For each account: budget, revisions, actuals, open commitments, estimate to complete, estimated final cost, variance.
- **Parts:** Estimated final cost equals actuals plus commitments plus estimate to complete. Accrue open POs or the report lies. Monthly cost report meeting with the producer and buyer: spent, variance, committed spend to finish, the plan if over (S018). Final cost report has every PO and estimate zeroed out (S022).
- **When to use:** From the first week of prep to the final report. Bond companies and financiers read it to release money (S025).
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if issued weekly, accrued, with an honest estimate to complete; 1 if costs are known only after wrap.
- **Evidence:** "The cost report is the central financial document of any production." (S025). "A cost report that ignores open purchase orders understates the real position and erodes trust in the numbers." (S025). "Cost report must include the following columns: actuals to date, committed costs, total costs, estimate to complete, estimated final costs, approved budget, and variance (over/under)" (S022)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S004, S018, S022, S025, S052)

## LIN-F7 Deliverables and clearance checklist
- **What it is:** Everything owed at the end: masters to spec, QC passes, sound files, subtitles and dubbing, music cue sheets and licences, chain of title, talent and location releases, E&O insurance if the buyer asks.
- **Parts:** Deliverables include the paperwork that proves rights (S018). Cue sheets and fully cleared music are mandatory for one streamer (S023). Build QC time into the schedule: one streamer advises 2 weeks per episode, 3 for a feature (S022). Late deliverables delay the last payment.
- **When to use:** At budget stage, not at delivery.
- **How to score (guidance):** high if the buyer's delivery list is in hand and costed before the shoot.
- **Evidence:** "Get everything on paper, early and always (not just often)." (S037). "The chain-of-title is a condition for the circulation of the film in the market" (S038)
- **Sources:** 7 (S018, S021, S022, S023, S029, S037, S038)

## LIN-F8 Pilot-first production
- **What it is:** Shoot one episode, a test day or a teaser before committing to a season, to measure real cost per finished minute and real pages per day with this team.
- **When to use:** New format, new team, new buyer, children's live action, or any AI-assisted pipeline not yet proven at length.
- **How to score (guidance):** high when the pilot's actual cost and pace feed the season budget before signing it.
- **Evidence:** inferred from S010, S059 (first passes get re-costed after changes; AI tools fail on some formats until tested).
- **Sources:** 2 (S010, S059)

## LIN-F9 Hot cost (the daily loop)
- **What it is:** A next-morning estimate of yesterday's variable cost (labour, overtime, meal penalties, fringes, catering, day-rate kit) against the budgeted day.
- **Parts:** In and out times, breaks, travel from the production report; compare to the budgeted day; change tomorrow's call times or order before the overage becomes permanent.
- **When to use:** Every shoot day on any show over a handful of days.
- **How to score (guidance):** high if the line producer sees yesterday's overtime by the next call.
- **Evidence:** "Waiting for the weekly cost report to reveal a labor overage means a full week of the same mistake repeating." (S025). "Hot costs catch a single bad day before it repeats, and the weekly cost report rolls those days into a trend and a revised forecast." (S025)
- **Sources:** 3 (S004, S024, S025)

## LIN-F10 Most realistic first pass, then script fixes
- **What it is:** Budget the script as written, giving every element what it needs. Then look for script changes that save money without losing the story (combine characters, a train scene on a bus), and re-cost.
- **When to use:** When the first number is higher than the money, which it always is.
- **How to score (guidance):** good if the cuts come from the script and the plan; bad if they come from squeezing crew, food, insurance or post.
- **Evidence:** "I budget the script for what it is. I always want to know what the current version of the script is going to cost." (S007). "On a first pass, I always do what I feel is the most realistic budget." (S010). "What's the lowest amount of money this film can possibly be made for without sacrificing the story and vision?" (S009)
- **Sources:** 4 (S007, S009, S010, S014)

## LIN-F6 Cut list (when money is short)
- **What it is:** The order to cut: locations and company moves, night shoots, extras, shooting days, then cast size. Never cut contingency, insurance, food or post first.
- **Parts:** Script fixes from LIN-F10 come before any cut. Performance time is the last thing to lose (S010).
- **Evidence:** "When you skimp, the first thing sacrificed is the performance of the actors." (S010). "Not budgeting for post is a common mistake among first time filmmakers." (S030)
- **Sources:** 4 (S010, S011, S012, S030)

## LIN-F12 Pattern and amort (series budgeting)
- **What it is:** A series budget is a pattern budget (the repeatable episode) plus amorts (costs shared across all episodes: standing sets, stage rent, titles, rights, prep). Episodic budgets adjust the pattern per episode and lock on the last day of that episode's prep.
- **When to use:** Every series, and every turnkey offer priced per episode.
- **How to score (guidance):** high if amorts are listed and divided by the ordered episodes; low if one episode's cost is multiplied by the episode count.
- **Evidence:** "if all amorts total $1,000,000 and there are 10 episodes ordered for the season, each episode would carry $100,000 in amortization costs" (S024). "Episodic budgets are often locked on the last day of prep for that episode." (S024)
- **Sources:** 2 (S017, S024)

## LIN-F13 Clean fringes
- **What it is:** Model every on-cost per person (taxes, social payments, insurance, union) with its own rate and ceiling, not one flat percentage.
- **Why:** A flat fringe either hides an overage or parks money that could have said yes to an extra camera day.
- **Working figures from US guides:** about 30 percent on non-union crew, about 47 percent on union actors (S031). Armenia's payroll taxes are different: check with the accountant (assumed).
- **Evidence:** "doing flat fringes is really not a good way to do it" (S001). "for every $1 dollar you pay an actor, you'll have about 47 cents in fringes" (S031)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S024, S031)

## LIN-F14 Incentive as a system
- **What it is:** A rebate is not a discount on the whole budget. It applies only to qualifying spend, with qualifying vendors, in the qualifying period, and arrives after audit. Tag every budget line as qualifying or not, model the uplift only on the days and items that earn it, and plan the cash gap until it pays.
- **Armenia (S064, Decision 412-N of 10 April 2025):** at least 25 percent of spend paid to Armenian resident taxpayers; an uplift for popularising Armenia of up to 5 percent for resident filmmakers and 10 percent for non-residents or co-productions; spend paid from the national Fund's own money does not count; a rejected film cannot apply again; the rebate is paid on actual, documented expenses after the film is made.
- **Parts:** Independents must finance or wait for the rebate, so discount it for interest and risk in the finance plan (S063). Keep receipts with the buyer's tax number; sloppy records mean disallowed costs (S025, S064).
- **Evidence:** "getting back 10 % on $10 million doesn't equate to $1 million all the time, unless you're spending every dollar there and every dollar is good" (S001). "Lately, it is all about rebate. We look at states and countries that can offer the best rebate . Then we make it work." (S007). "It's never too early to engage a tax credit consultant." (S063)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S007, S022, S025, S046, S049, S063, S064, S065)

## LIN-F15 Change order protocol
- **What it is:** Any change after lock, from the buyer, director or showrunner, gets a cost and a day impact in writing before it is shot.
- **Parts:** Talk first, then memorialize by email (S001). Buyers say the producer must raise the money question before a buyer-requested change is made (S022). Spend over the approved budget needs written approval (S022). Schedule changes reported fast (one streamer asks within 24 hours: S022).
- **Evidence:** "it's the responsibility of the Prod Co/Studio to reach out to Roku Production to discuss the financial implications before any decisions/changes are made" (S022). "If it's important, call me, right?" (S001)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S022, S059)

## LIN-F16 Children's day
- **What it is:** Schedule child performers first, because their hours set the shape of the day.
- **Benchmarks from published rules (not Armenian law):** NZ ScreenSafe: up to 8 years, 6 hours door to door including travel; 9 to 11, 8 hours door to door; 12 to 16, 10 hours plus travel; no more than five days running; 12 hour turnaround (S041). England: licence from the local authority, may be refused if applied for under 21 days ahead; a 45 minute break when present four hours or more; 12 hour overnight minimum, 14 expected (S043). US studios: teacher, parent and safety review for any physical activity; no firearms or pyro (S042).
- **Armenia:** check the Labour Code limits for minors with a lawyer before scheduling (assumed; no child-performer rule in this corpus).
- **Fixes:** cast adults who read younger for teens, twins, fewer child scenes (S010).
- **Evidence:** "Children are not to work more than five consecutive days, and no more than five days in a calendar week." (S041). "cast actors who are 18+ instead of actual teenagers. This opens up your whole schedule and saves you cash." (S010)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S009, S010, S018, S039, S040, S041, S042, S043)

## LIN-F17 Insurance and bond stack
- **What it is:** The layers that protect the money: production package (cast, equipment, third party, faulty stock), auto, E&O for delivery, weather if buyable, and a completion bond when financiers need one.
- **Parts:** Bring the broker in at script stage; plan 1 to 3 percent of budget for insurance, starting at 3 (S051). A bond costs about 3 to 5 percent of the net budget; the strike price includes contingency and the fee; the guarantor can take over and replace people (S027).
- **Evidence:** "too often filmmakers wait until the very last minute to try to fit their insurance budget in" (S051). "Insurance fees are sometimes not included in the producer's preliminary budget but they are an essential budgetary part of the production." (S011)
- **Sources:** 5 (S011, S018, S022, S027, S051)

## LIN-F18 Scout logistics test
- **What it is:** Before loving a location, ask: where do the trucks park, how long is the drive, how many shuttles, where does the crew eat, what permits and police, what if it falls through.
- **Parts:** Over about 40 minutes of travel, a perfect location usually loses (S001). One or two company moves a day at most (S012). Free locations are rarely free (S010).
- **Evidence:** "where are the trucks parking?" (S001). "don't do more than one or two company moves in a day if you can help it Try not to do any company moves in it because you kill your day" (S012). "skimping on a $400 port-a-potty resulted in $12,000 of damage to someone's pipes" (S010)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S009, S010, S012, S018)

## LIN-F19 Post and delivery budget
- **What it is:** Budget edit, sound, colour, music, VFX, subtitles, dubbing, QC, deliverables and festival or launch costs at the start.
- **Parts:** Post and distribution can be 15 to 30 percent of the total (S029). Post takes at least as long as the shoot (S018). More decision makers in post means more revision rounds (S029). Unscripted can need up to 12 weeks of edit per episode (S061).
- **Evidence:** "post-production often takes at least the same amount of time (if not longer) than the actual shoot" (S018). "Associated costs typically eat up between fifteen and thirty percent of a film's total budget" (S029)
- **Sources:** 6 (S015, S018, S022, S029, S030, S061)

## LIN-F20 AI insert test
- **What it is:** Decide shot by shot whether an AI-generated element is cheaper, safe on rights, and accepted by the buyer. Price revisions, because a reprompt feels free to a client and becomes scope creep.
- **Parts:** Use AI where consistency is not critical (wides, previs, pitch teasers); keep real performers for close-ups; lock characters and cap revision rounds; check the buyer's AI and clearance rules and E&O before delivery.
- **Evidence:** "it's the bane of our existence sometimes is scope creep" (S059). "We rarely charge over a hundred thousand dollars, but we can charge a hundred thousand dollars or more for some AI ads." (S059)
- **Sources:** 2 (S049, S059)
