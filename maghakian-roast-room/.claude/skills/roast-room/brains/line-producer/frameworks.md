# Frameworks: Line Producer

Composite archetype from physical production practice. Numbers are rules of thumb to check.

## LIN-F1 Day count
What: convert script pages into shooting days by format. Working assumptions: daily drama on standing sets 12 to 20 pages a day; weekly series 6 to 10; feature film 3 to 5; children's live action fewer. Add company moves and night shoots.
Score: a schedule that assumes more pages a day than the format allows = high risk.

## LIN-F2 Above and below the line
What: above the line (writers, director, producers, lead cast), below the line (crew, equipment, locations, transport, catering, post). Know the share of each before negotiating.

## LIN-F3 Cash-flow calendar
What: week-by-week table of money in (buyer milestones, grants, co-pro installments) and money out (pre-production, shoot weeks, post). The lowest point is the real budget risk.
Score: a negative week with no bridge = ruin risk for a small studio.

## LIN-F4 Risk register
What: the ten things that stop a shoot (lead cast unavailable, location lost, weather, permits, equipment, illness, script late, payment late, key crew leaves, power). For each: likelihood, cost, plan B.

## LIN-F5 Turnkey margin check
What: turnkey fee minus full production cost minus contingency minus a price for delivery risk. If the remainder is under 10 to 15 percent, the deal is a loan to the buyer.

## LIN-F6 Cut list
What: when money is short, cut in this order: locations, night shoots, extras, shooting days, then cast. Never cut contingency or post first.

## LIN-F7 Deliverables checklist
What: masters, subtitles, dubbing, music cue sheets and licences, chain of title, E&O insurance if the buyer needs it. Late deliverables delay final payment.

## LIN-F8 Pilot-first production
What: shoot one episode or a teaser before committing to a full season, to test cost per minute and the team.
