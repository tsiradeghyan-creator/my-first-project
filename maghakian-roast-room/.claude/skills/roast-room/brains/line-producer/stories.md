# Stories: Line Producer (composite)

Built from 66 public sources (see sources.md). Two kinds of entries. **Practitioner cases** are told by the named person in the cited source; the seat may retell them as "a line producer I learned from" or by name with the S-id, never as its own experience. **Typical cases** are composite situations from the earlier brain, not real events; the seat says so when it uses them.

## Practitioner cases

1. **The perfect location 55 minutes away.** On a scout abroad, a line producer and director realised they had driven 40 minutes and had 15 to go; both said no before they saw it. Lesson: travel time and truck parking decide a location (LIN-F18). (S001)

2. **Vetting other people's budgets at a studio.** A line producer brought in by studio executives to check other UPMs' budgets found the same sin again and again: one flat fringe rate. Lesson: model fringes per person, or the money hides (LIN-F13). (S001)

3. **The contingency that was already spent.** Producers saying "take it out of contingency" for known items. Lesson: earmarked contingency is no contingency (LIN-P2). "Leave your contingency for, I don't know, contingency." (S001)

4. **Ant-Man in Atlanta.** A studio UPM put most of a San Francisco story in Atlanta for the rebate, with three days in San Francisco and VFX for the rest. Lesson: the rebate picks the city, then you make it look right (LIN-F14). (S007)

5. **The $400 toilet.** A production skipped a portable toilet at a house location and paid $12,000 for damage to the owner's pipes. Lesson: free locations are not free (LIN-F18). (S010)

6. **The executive producer's dog.** An untrained pet on set cost hours on a simple shot and risked overtime and an extra day. Lesson: trained animals and handlers cost less than delay (LIN-F6). (S009, S010)

7. **The helicopter shot nobody budgeted.** A commercials client asked on the day for a helicopter shot; the line producer could say yes only because other departments were padded. Lesson: on client work, keep reserve inside the plan (LIN-P2). (S012)

8. **The director who never ran late.** Crews still tell stories about a director who always wrapped at the same time and always served second meal. Lesson: a predictable day buys loyalty that money does not (LIN-P23). (S012)

9. **Sixty days of action on two days of budget.** A filmmaker pitched an action script at 100 grand; the line producer counted 60 shooting days. Lesson: count days before you agree a number (LIN-F1). (S012)

10. **Always under budget.** A studio finance executive was proud every film came in under, then saw the cost: cranes and extras the directors never got. Lesson: under is not automatically a win (LIN-P14). (S049)

11. **The remake that cost far more to insure.** A filmmaker who insured a tiny first film cheaply was shocked by the quote for a $2 million remake. Lesson: insurance scales with risk; bring the broker in early (LIN-F17). (S051)

12. **The half-made film.** Producers started shooting before the money was raised, hoping footage would attract the rest; a million or two was spent with nothing to sell. Lesson: raise it all first (LIN-P15). (S055)

13. **The AI restaurant ad.** An AI-native studio charged a fair fee for a contained scene with several actors and cross-cutting; the tools could not hold consistent looks. Lesson: test AI on the hard shots before pricing the job (LIN-F20). (S059)

14. **The extra days paid from the fee.** When a show needs more days, the money comes out of the producer's fee. Lesson: price delivery risk into turnkey (LIN-F5). (S058)

15. **Armenia's 2025 rebate.** The government approved a cash rebate in April 2025, with a 2023 draft at 20 percent and the final at a minimum of 25 percent plus uplifts. Lesson: the region now competes on rebates, and the rules can still change (LIN-F14). (S046, S064, S066)

## Typical cases (composite, not real events)

16. **The 60-day payment.** The buyer paid on delivery plus 60 days; the crew wanted paying on Friday. The studio bridged it with the founders' savings. Lesson: cash-flow calendar (LIN-F3).

17. **The winter exterior.** A snow day in the hills above Yerevan cost three shooting days. Lesson: risk register and weather float (LIN-F4).

18. **The free rewrite on set.** The buyer changed scenes during the shoot with no change order. Lesson: lock the script, price the changes (LIN-F15).
