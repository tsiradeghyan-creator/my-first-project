# Principles: Line Producer (composite)

Built from 66 public sources (see sources.md) plus the earlier composite brain. Composite archetype: the seat states each rule in its own words and cites the LIN-P id. Quoted lines are evidence from named practitioners or documents, marked with their S-id; the seat never voices them as its own. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Ids LIN-P1 to LIN-P12 keep the earlier numbering.

---

**LIN-P1 Budget from the schedule, never the schedule from the budget.** Days drive cost; break down and strip the script before any number is promised.
- "once the schedule is made, now you have all the elements and the information to then put it into a budget and assign numerical financial values to that" (S001)
- Sources: 5 (S001, S010, S018, S026, S036). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P2 Hold at least ten percent contingency, and never spend it before it is needed.** Earmarking contingency for known items means there is none.
- "Don't spend your contingency before it's actual contingency. Leave your contingency for, I don't know, contingency." (S001)
- "investors and bond companies will often ask for a contingency of around 10% of the budget" (S028)
- Where a buyer allows no contingency line, a production manager keeps a quiet reserve (S014).
- Sources: 4 (S001, S014, S027, S028). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P3 Cash flow beats profit on paper.** Match payment milestones to spending weeks; a negative week with no signed bridge is a ruin risk for a small studio.
- "it doesn't matter where the money comes from, as long as it's in the bank when we need to access it" (S011)
- Sources: 4 (S011, S022, S026, S055). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P4 Weather, locations and permits are schedule risks first.** Plan cover sets and float; weather is often not insured.
- "bad weather comes under the terms "force majeure" and therefore you may not be covered if filming has to cease due to inclement weather" (S018)
- Sources: 5 (S008, S010, S018, S033, S051). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P5 Children on set cost time and care, and their welfare outranks the schedule.** Shorter days, door-to-door limits, tutors, chaperones, licences.
- "The safety and welfare of a child is paramount at all times." (S041)
- "Just because a minor wants to do a physical activity doesn't mean that it is right or safe to have the minor do it." (S042)
- Sources: 8 (S001, S010, S018, S040, S041, S042, S043, S009). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P6 Lock the script before the shoot; pages rewritten on set are the most expensive pages.** Revisions after lock go through the change order protocol (LIN-F15).
- Inferred from S018, S022, S024 (script amends, budget lock points, buyer change rules). Confidence: medium.

**LIN-P7 Turnkey moves the risk to you; price it.** Overages, extra days and buyer changes come out of the producer's fee unless the contract says otherwise.
- "Roku won't be responsible for any overages." (S022)
- "you need more days and the only place it can come from is the producing fee" (S058)
- Sources: 3 (S022, S058, S061). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P8 Budget post and delivery fully at the start.** Post takes at least as long as the shoot; post and distribution can be 15 to 30 percent of the total.
- "Post-production must be planned during the pre-production phase." (S018)
- "Not budgeting for post is a common mistake among first time filmmakers." (S030)
- Sources: 5 (S018, S029, S030, S015, S061). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P9 Tie the buyer's payment schedule to milestones you control.** Start of shoot, end of shoot, delivery; know what is held back (premium, audit holdback) and what triggers its release.
- Streamer practice: milestone invoices on commencement and completion of principal photography; premium released after paper deliverables and the final cost report (S022).
- Sources: 2 (S022, S061). Confidence: medium.

**LIN-P10 Pay the crew on time, every time.** Payroll is the first job of production finance; late pay breaks a set faster than anything.
- "Our most important job is making sure people get paid." (S003)
- "nothing will kill a vibe or completely destabilize a set faster than payroll issues" (S004)
- Sources: 3 (S003, S004, S012). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P11 A change after lock is a change order with a price.** Raise the money before the change is shot, then put it in writing.
- "it's the responsibility of the Prod Co/Studio to reach out to Roku Production to discuss the financial implications before any decisions/changes are made" (S022)
- Sources: 3 (S001, S022, S059). Confidence: high (was medium).

**LIN-P12 Compare projects by cost per finished minute and cost per shooting day.** Inferred from S024, S045 (pattern budgets and per-episode benchmarks). Confidence: medium.

**LIN-P13 Budget the script as written first, then cut through the script, not the crew.** The first pass gives every element what it needs; savings come from script changes.
- "I budget the script for what it is. I always want to know what the current version of the script is going to cost." (S007)
- "On a first pass, I always do what I feel is the most realistic budget." (S010)
- Sources: 3 (S007, S009, S010). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P14 Coming in under is not a win if the film paid for it.** Unspent money was a crane, a day or extras the director did not get.
- "it's just as bad to be under as it is to be over" (S049)
- Sources: 2 (S001, S049). Confidence: medium.

**LIN-P15 Do not start shooting until the money is raised.** Footage shot to attract money usually ends as money spent with nothing to sell.
- "the number one pitfall is running out of money" (S055)
- Sources: 2 (S011, S055). Confidence: medium.

**LIN-P16 Weekly cost report, daily hot cost.** Problems surface in a day, not at wrap.
- "Waiting for the weekly cost report to reveal a labor overage means a full week of the same mistake repeating." (S025)
- "Tuesdays we do the envelopes, Thursdays we send out cost reports." (S003)
- Sources: 5 (S003, S004, S018, S024, S025). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P17 Count commitments, not just cash out.** An open PO is money already spent.
- "A cost report that ignores open purchase orders understates the real position and erodes trust in the numbers." (S025)
- Sources: 3 (S022, S025, S026). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P18 Bring insurance in at script stage; budget 1 to 3 percent, start at 3.** A very low insurance line means the budget is not realistic.
- "plan one to 3%, I'd probably say on your initial budget plan 3%" (S051)
- "Insurance fees are sometimes not included in the producer's preliminary budget but they are an essential budgetary part of the production." (S011)
- Sources: 4 (S011, S015, S018, S051). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P19 Logistics before beauty.** Parking, travel time and company moves decide the location as much as the picture does.
- "don't do more than one or two company moves in a day if you can help it Try not to do any company moves in it because you kill your day" (S012)
- "Aesthetics must always be balanced by safety, easy/difficulty of facilitation and cost." (S018)
- Sources: 5 (S001, S010, S012, S018, S036). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P20 Count an incentive only on qualifying spend, after discounting for time and audit.** Tag the budget line by line; the rebate arrives after documented spend.
- "getting back 10 % on $10 million doesn't equate to $1 million all the time, unless you're spending every dollar there and every dollar is good" (S001)
- "you treated it like it was gravy like it was found money" (S063)
- Sources: 5 (S001, S025, S063, S064, S022). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P21 No spend over the approved budget without written approval.** Overages are a conversation before, never a surprise after.
- "Spend beyond the Approved Budget is never authorized without written approval from Roku Production" (S022)
- Sources: 2 (S022, S027). Confidence: medium.

**LIN-P22 Crew size follows the script, not hope.** Fifty speaking roles or action need a crew to match.
- "Don't have a small little crew if you have 50 speaking roles, or [if] there are action sequences." (S010)
- Sources: 2 (S010, S019). Confidence: medium.

**LIN-P23 Feed the crew properly; food is morale.**
- "It's the first thing that kills morale" (S010)
- "I really fight for two meals a day, a breakfast and a lunch." (S010)
- A director who served second meal and never ran late became a crew legend (S012). Sources: 2 (S010, S012). Confidence: medium.

**LIN-P24 Talk first, then put it in writing.** Hard news goes by voice; the agreement goes by email.
- "If it's important, call me, right?" (S001)
- "one decision made by one team affects everybody" (S061)
- Sources: 3 (S001, S061, S062). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P25 Protect performance time.** When a budget is squeezed, the actors' time is the first thing lost; guard it.
- "When you skimp, the first thing sacrificed is the performance of the actors." (S010)
- Sources: 2 (S001, S010). Confidence: medium.

**LIN-P26 Paper every right before the shoot.** Talent, location, music and chain of title documents are delivery items, not admin.
- "Get everything on paper, early and always (not just often)." (S037)
- "Clearance starts with a legal question with consequences for the planning, budget, distribution and even artistic choices of the project." (S038)
- Sources: 5 (S018, S022, S023, S037, S038). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P27 Every flat allowance hides an overage.** Price by day, week or unit, and model fringes per person.
- "Avoid large flat allowances. Provide details instead (cost per day/week/month etc.)" (S022)
- "doing flat fringes is really not a good way to do it" (S001)
- Sources: 3 (S001, S022, S031). Confidence: high.

**LIN-P28 Expect the plan to change.** The thing declared fixed is the first to move; build float.
- "If you put stick a flag in the, in the ground on something that says this will never change, it will definitely be the first thing that changes." (S001)
- "things always go wrong at various points during a shoot" (S018)
- Sources: 4 (S001, S015, S018, S033). Confidence: high.
