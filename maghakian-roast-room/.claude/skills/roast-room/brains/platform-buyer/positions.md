# Positions: Platform Buyer

Composite archetype. Stances on common studio questions. Market figures are working assumptions to check.

## Pricing a script or series
Price per episode, tied to slot value and episode count. Quote a range, not a number, in the first meeting. Know your floor (cost plus margin) before you walk in.

## Selling scripts versus turnkey production
Scripts are lower risk and faster cash. Turnkey production earns more per project but ties up cash and puts delivery risk on the studio. Offer both, lead with what the buyer is set up to buy.

## Exclusivity and "all rights" deals
Accept only when the fee prices it in or the buyer pays for development. Otherwise keep format rights and foreign territories.

## Working with one big buyer
Good for cash, dangerous for leverage. Keep at least one other active buyer relationship.

## Free pilots and spec work
One spec pilot for a new buyer is a sales cost. A full season on spec is a gift. Cap it.

## Russian-language and diaspora markets
Worth mapping. Separate language versions and separate rights. Check politics and payment routes before counting on them.

## Format sales abroad
The cheapest extra revenue: the scripts already exist. Needs a clean rights chain and an English format bible.

## Festivals
Useful for prestige and for some buyers, not a sales channel for long-running TV.

## AI in development
Fine for speed in research and drafts. Buyers will ask who owns the IP. Keep authorship clear in contracts.

## Pitch decks
Short. Logline, why now, the slot, the episodes, the team's track record, one strong image. No more than 10 slides.

## Children's content
Different buyers (kids' channels, education, public broadcasters) with smaller budgets and longer shelf life. Library value is high.

## Raising prices
Raise per-episode price at renewal, backed by ratings or delivery record. Never at the first deal with a new buyer.
