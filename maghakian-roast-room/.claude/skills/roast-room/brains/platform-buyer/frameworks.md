# Frameworks: Platform Buyer

Composite archetype built from how commissioning and acquisitions work in TV and streaming. Scoring lines are guidance, not rules.

## BUY-F1 Slot fit test
What: five checks a buyer runs in the first minute. Slot (day, time, daily or weekly), audience (who watches that slot), episode count and length, budget band, delivery date.
Use: any series or film pitch to a broadcaster or platform.
Score: 5 of 5 fit = 8 to 10. A miss on episode count or budget = 4 or lower until reworked.

## BUY-F2 Buyer map
What: a table of every realistic buyer. Columns: platform, what they are short of this year, who decides, when budgets open, what they paid last time, relationship strength.
Use: before choosing what to develop, and before sending any pitch.
Score: fewer than three realistic buyers = high market risk.

## BUY-F3 Rights ladder
What: sell rights in an order that keeps value for the next sale. Typical order: first-window domestic broadcast, then local streaming, then diaspora and Russian-language territories, then format/remake rights abroad, then library.
Use: any deal with exclusivity, worldwide or "all media" language.
Score: a deal that takes all rights forever for a one-territory fee = 3 or lower unless the fee prices that in.

## BUY-F4 Commission, license, or format sale
What: three deal shapes. Commission: platform pays to develop and produce, usually keeps most rights. License: you own it, they rent a window. Format sale: someone abroad buys the right to remake it.
Use: whenever a buyer offers a deal, ask which shape it is and what the other two would earn over five years.

## BUY-F5 The 10-page test
What: a busy executive reads the logline, the one-pager and the first ten pages of the pilot. If those do not sell it, nothing else will be read.
Use: every pitch package.

## BUY-F6 Proof stack
What: what the buyer can show their boss. Track record (projects, episodes, ratings), attached talent, a finished pilot or teaser, audience data from past shows, a co-financier already in.
Score: two or more strong proofs = the buyer can say yes alone.

## BUY-F7 Volume pricing
What: for long-running dailies and weeklies, price per episode and per season, with a discount curve for volume and a delivery schedule the writers' room can hold.
Use: telenovela-style, sitcom and daily drama pitches.

## BUY-F8 Three-buyer rule
What: pitch to at least three buyers in parallel. One buyer sets your price; three buyers create one.
