# Positions: Jason Blum

Built from 97 public sources, 2013 to 2026. 32 topics. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (see `sources.md`). Where he changed his mind, both views sit side by side with dates; the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Budget ceilings
- **Stance:** Keep originals as cheap as the market allows; the number has risen, the principle has not.
- **Over time:**
  - 2013: "About five million bucks. That's the top. For us that's like Transformers" (S024)
  - 2015: "Our idea model at Blumhouse is a movie that costs $4 million and change." (S019)
  - 2017: "Almost everything we do is under $5 million dollars; that's our ceiling on our budgets" (S008)
  - 2022: "I wish the movies were still $2 million." (S062) "now we're closer to ten million dollars than five. So low budget is more expensive." (S057)
  - 2024 (newer): "Now we're more like $10-15 million to try and remain competitive in what is a very, you know, difficult theatrical marketplace." (S088)
- **Read:** the ceiling followed talent costs and the theatrical bar up. He says of the rise: "I don't think it's good for the business" (S062, 2022).

## Big budgets and tentpoles
- **Stance:** Tentpoles are a sound model, there are too many, and he will not make them.
- 2017: "It's extremely sound. I just think there are too many of them right now" (S051)
- 2020: "I could make expensive movies now but I don't have any interest in making expensive movies" (S013)
- 2023: Exorcist: Believer ($30M, paid up front, no back end) broke his rules. "It's the kind of deal that I've just spent this entire morning telling you I hate" (S018)

## Talent pay and back end
- **Stance:** Scale up front, share the upside, pay transparently. "it's very easy to get famous people to work for nothing upfront because we've paid we have a great track record of paying" (S013, 2020)
- He calls net profits a con he suffered himself: "I have net profits of 10 movies I've never gotten a single penny" (S013, 2020)

## Producer fees
- **Stance:** Zero. "Our fee on 'M3GAN' was zero. Our fee on 'Black Phone' was zero." (S097, 2023) "It's, like, hypocritical to tell a financier, 'Hey, we're all going to do great, but I need a million dollars to produce" (S072, 2015)

## Streaming pay model (pay everyone up front)
- **Stance:** Unsustainable and bad for quality.
- 2021: "they pay everyone as if the movies have already come out in our home runs" (S014)
- 2022: "I've always thought it fundamentally unsustainable for a company to treat all participants as if they had made a hit before the project is even produced." (S077)
- 2023: "I'm squarely on the side of the writers" (S097); wants streamers to "let the people who are really responsible for making the TV shows and movies participate" (S097)

## Streaming as an outlet
- **Stance:** Good for more films, worse for producers.
- 2019: "streaming generally has provided much more opportunity to filmmakers and production companies, so streaming is a great thing for us" (S007)
- 2020: "Streaming, you can take more risks and do things that are more unusual." (S020) "you can feel you're one of a million Model Ts" (S020)
- 2023: "All the streamers totally don't value producers in the way that they value writers, directors, and actors" (S097)

## Theatrical windows and day and date (changed his mind)
- 2014: "I think it's a losing proposition to control how the consumer consumes content, and it drives me crazy when people try it" (S049)
- 2017: windows narrowing "makes no difference to me" (S096)
- 2021: pushed Halloween Kills day and date to Peacock himself: "It was my idea to do it" (S028)
- 2022: "I'm totally open to theatrical day and date; I think there's no one size fits all" (S043)
- 2025 (newer): "I have to start by saying I was a big proponent of day and date. I think I was very wrong." (S005) "I think it's created confusion in the marketplace." (S040)
- 2026: a "big believer in a long, consistent theatrical window" (S041); "People cherish the theatrical experience" (S041)
- **Read:** a clear reversal. Cite the 2025 to 2026 view.

## The future of theaters
- 2016: "Cinema's changing, for sure; it's never been more in flux than it is now, but I certainly don't think it's dying" (S094)
- 2020: "I don't think theaters are ever gonna go away" (S013)
- 2021: "The theatrical moviegoing experience matters. To our creative community. To the audience. And to our investors" (S012)
- 2026 (newer): horror is "so participatory" it joins sports and concerts as a reason to leave the house (S107).

## Original vs IP vs sequels
- 2017: about half franchises, half originals (S051).
- 2022: "We know IP horror works, but we don't know if original horror is working well" (S029)
- 2024: slate moved to about two thirds sequels because audiences show up for them (journalist paraphrase, S092).
- 2025: "People are going to the things that they know." (S005)
- 2026 (newer): online creators' originals (Obsession, Backrooms) broke out; "The audience was out there, it's just that we were making the wrong movies for them" (S082)

## Horror saturation and cycles
- **Stance:** The audience is steady; the industry overproduces and then retreats.
- 2018: "you get a few good years and then you get too many horror movies and then you get a few bad years" (S086)
- 2023: "The audience can take about eight or 10 horror titles a year." (S097) "The audience is fairly consistent. The industry is what's inconsistent." (S097)
- 2025: "Hollywood is like Charlie Sheen" (S005)

## Competition and copycats
- 2016: "It's had less of an impact than I would've thought." (S010)
- 2021: competitive about rival horror hits; A Quiet Place "almost send me to my grave" (S014)
- Why few copy him: "The reason people don't make low-budget movies is that it's completely not sexy" (S072, 2015)

## Hiring directors
- **Stance:** Experienced, undervalued, entrepreneurial. "We do best with directors who are very entrepreneurial" (S087, 2021). First-timers rarely on theatrical films (S059, 2020), more often in TV and streaming.
- 2026 (newer): backs online creators who honed their craft on YouTube: "directors who really honed their skills as creators online" (S053); but "It's not just about finding people who are popular on YouTube or TikTok and throwing a bunch of money at them" (S082)

## Stars
- **Stance:** Optional in horror. "With most other genres you need movie stars" (S096, 2017) "With horror, you just need a story." (S096) Unknowns are scarier (S010, 2016).

## Firing and managing people
- **Stance:** Give executives freedom; let go those whose picks keep failing (paraphrase, S060, 2021). Leadership: "Transparency is very important. Try and be as transparent as possible. Optimistic." (S060)
- No recorded position on firing talent mid-production.

## Partners and mergers
- **Stance:** Partner with a creator to grow without losing quality. "the only way we could increase our output without our quality going down was to have another creator as part of the company as opposed to hiring more executives" (S083, 2024)

## Raising money, equity, selling the company
- **Stance:** Not raising, not selling. "if someone offered me a trillion dollars, I would sell the company, but not for anything less than that" (S057, 2022). 2025: no deck, no banker hired (S005).
- No recorded position on outside equity for small companies.

## Debt
- No recorded position.

## Marketing
- **Stance:** Half the job; plan it at script stage. "the movie is 50 percent of the job and the marketing is the other 50 percent of the job" (S081, 2017) "we think about how we are going to market at the script stage" (S066, 2015)

## Data and testing
- **Stance:** Test screenings yes; data dashboards no. "I hate data" (S018, 2023). "Just to be clear, Jen has data. I have, like, feelings." (S044, 2020) But: "I think test screenings are incredibly important" (S100, 2022)

## Message and politics in films
- **Stance:** Entertainment first; meaning underneath is welcome, preaching is not. "what I don't like is when Hollywood gets preachy" (S013, 2020) "I don't mandate it, but I think we're more drawn to those movies" (S030, 2021)

## Diversity
- **Stance:** Good business because the audience is diverse. "The audience is diverse" and "horror skews 55 percent female" (S078, 2018). Prefers incentives to mandates: "It hits you in the pocketbook. That's more effective than forcing you." (S078)

## AI (changed tone)
- 2022: "Pumping the brakes is impossible, but I do think M3GAN is a cautionary tale." (S052)
- 2024: tested Meta's tools: "I want to understand it and know about it" (S083); would wave a magic wand to make AI vanish (S092).
- 2025 (newer): "Whatever you feel about AI, it's here to stay." (S005) "The consumer does not care if what they're looking at is AI." (S005)
- 2026: confident AI will not beat filmmakers for a long time; more a rival to social scrolling (paraphrase, S098).

## Television
- **Stance:** The low-budget model does not work in TV, because TV money is plentiful.
- 2017: "There's so much more money in TV" (S051)
- 2018: "the movie company is built on inexpensive budgets, and the TV company is not" (S050)
- 2021: "Trying to replicate my movie model in television did not work" (S074) "I was an adult in the movie company and a kid in the TV company." (S074)
- 2023: "So if that existed also in movies, I would quit, because it's not interesting to me." (S097)

## Awards
- **Stance:** Would love an Oscar, will not chase one. "If I was in the business to win awards, I wouldn't be making primarily horror movies." (S102, 2019) "I wanna win an Oscar." (S057, 2022)

## Timing and release dates
- **Stance:** Space scary movies apart; avoid crowded dates (S109, 2015). Releasing M3GAN 2.0 in summer was part of the 2025 miss (S093).

## Ancillary value
- 2022: "The amount of ancillary value a movie makes is almost twice what it was five years ago" (S104)

## Work hours and burnout
- Starts around 8:30, home for the kids (journalist, S072, 2015); refuses 14-hour days (journalist, S074, 2021). Watches the company's films at night (S059).

## Remote work, culture
- Office culture: everyone in one building, no trailers, theater-troupe feel (S075, 2013). Halloween party instead of a holiday party (S074).
- No recorded position on remote work.

## Pitching and passes
- Pitch through an agent or lawyer, never on the street (S014, 2021). On being passed: "Please, if you're going to pass, keep it short and say it's not for me." (S081)

## Found footage
- **Stance:** Only if the story demands it. "i always tell filmmakers they should shoot their movies traditionally unless it HAS TO BE DONE because of the story in FOUND FOOTAGE" (S066, 2015)

## Gore and violence
- "Movies are scarier actually when they aren't gross" (S072, 2015). "if it's violent all the way through you kinda become numb to it" (S086, 2018). 2024: fans want "old-fashioned, tough, gritty, scary, gory horror" (S036), a market read, not his taste.

## Building a company vs a career
- "I look at myself more as an entrepreneur than a producer" (S112, 2020). "The minute you think of yourself as a big success, you may as well throw in your chips." (S026, 2020)
