# Stories: Jason Blum

Built from 97 public sources, 2013 to 2026. 20 stories and case studies he reuses, with the lesson he draws and source ids.

1. **Passing on The Blair Witch Project (Sundance 1999).** At Miramax he passed on the $60,000 film, overpaid in a bidding war for Happy, Texas, and watched Blair Witch become a phenomenon. Lesson: older, wiser people pass on the new thing too; never let it happen twice. "i also passed on blair witch! and the answer to why is because i made a dumb mistake!" (S066) Sources: S023, S047, S066, S067, S072.

2. **Paranormal Activity (2007 to 2009).** A $15,000 film sent as a directing sample, headed for a $100,000 to $150,000 DVD deal. He asked Oren Peli to cancel it for a back-end deal, showed it to buyers dozens of times, got rejected by Sundance, played Slamdance, then used a DreamWorks "remake" deal that required a test screening of the original. The Burbank audience went berserk and the remake was never mentioned again. Lesson: get the decision maker in a room with a real audience. "the word remake is never uttered it's never uttered again but it's never uttered" (S047) Sources: S013, S016, S019, S047, S072, S089.

3. **The soup cans.** His father Irving Blum bought all 32 of Warhol's soup cans for $1,000 when no one wanted them and later sold them to MoMA for $15M. Lesson: believe in what others laugh at. "Paranormal Activity was my soup-can moment" (S018) Sources: S018, S019, S072.

4. **The Immoral Mr. Teas.** His father took $1,500 cash instead of 10 percent of a film that made $1M. Lesson, as the journalist tells it: take the back end. Sources: S072.

5. **The Tooth Fairy.** His only studio film: 40 meetings on The Rock's costume, a soul-crushing process, but a dazzling distribution machine. Lesson: independent production, studio distribution. "The movie was a success. But the process of making it was soul-crushing and I considered quitting the movie business altogether" (S089) Sources: S004, S005, S013, S073, S089.

6. **Thrown off the Paramount lot.** After the most profitable film ever, Paramount let his deal lapse without a call; he hired a mover. Lesson: never depend on one system; then Donna Langley at Universal took a small deal. "I was thrown off the lot at Paramount and I had nowhere to fucking go" (S050) Sources: S014, S050, S089.

7. **The Boy Next Door.** A $35M thriller remade for $4.2M with Jennifer Lopez and Rob Cohen at scale. After a weak test he refused more money; Universal gave Cohen $300,000 for reshoots; the film grossed over $50M. Lesson: never break the budget. Sources: S016, S072, S007.

8. **Ouija.** Hasbro's $105M version stalled; he remade it for $5M. The first cut tested around 30 to 40; Mike Flanagan rewrote and reshot 10 days; it reached about 70 and made $102.5M. Lesson: test, then fix. Sources: S072, S087, S100.

9. **Get Out's ending.** The first ending left the hero in jail. He told Jordan Peele the audience loved the character too much for that; Peele wrote the new ending. Scores barely moved; they trusted their gut. "You can't, you can't rob the audience of this experience." (S100) Sources: S014, S021, S050, S100.

10. **The Get Out deer.** A car hitting a deer cost about $10,000 to $15,000 and scares because the audience is absorbed in the couple's talk about race. Lesson: cheap scares work when the drama works. Sources: S014, S016.

11. **The Purge.** A concept nobody would fund at $20M, made for about $3M inside one house; the first test scored very low; it became a $535M franchise. Lesson: prove the idea small, then show the streets. Sources: S014, S023, S060, S087, S018.

12. **Sinister, day one.** A stunt with the hanged family went wrong; he shut down, replaced people, and the $3M budget rose 25 percent. His worst day on set. Sources: S014, S023.

13. **The Bay.** Barry Levinson's pollution documentary became a horror film. Nobody saw it. Lesson: too much message, not enough entertainment. "we got too much message and not enough entertainment" (S023) Sources: S013, S023.

14. **Jem and the Holograms and The Hunt.** Two films he thinks got everything right and failed: Jem with Jon Chu, The Hunt killed by a false political narrative. Lesson: you can do everything right and still lose. Sources: S004, S005, S014, S039.

15. **Freaky.** A film he loved, whose release he says he botched in 2020; it pushed him to put Halloween Kills on Peacock the same day. "I take all the blame for the release" (S014) Sources: S014, S028.

16. **La La Land.** He thought he had Damien Chazelle's next film after Whiplash, skipped a lunch, and lost it. Sources: S051, S096.

17. **Halloween and John Carpenter.** A 15-minute meeting: the film will be made with or without us, so let's make it good together. Jamie Lee Curtis threw her phone at the scale offer, then took scale with a bigger cut. Halloween cost about $10M and earned $260M. Sources: S018, S050.

18. **Exorcist: Believer.** A $400M trilogy deal where Universal paid everything up front and Blumhouse took no back end: the reverse of his model, which he did because he was "dying to do it" and later said he did not like. Sources: S018, S027, S097.

19. **M3GAN 2.0 (2025).** A $12M hit's sequel cost at least $25M, switched genres, moved to summer, and opened to $10.2M. He owned it in public. "We all thought Megan was like Superman. We could do anything to her." (S093) Sources: S005, S093.

20. **Obsession (2026).** A $750,000 film by a YouTube creator that rose in its second and third weekends and grossed hundreds of millions. Lesson: the audience was there; the industry made the wrong films for it. Sources: S053, S082, S107.
