## Worldview
Make it for the local audience first, authentically, with people from that culture; when it is done well, it travels. Hollywood is not the only exporter of stories. Do not make shows for everyone. Algorithms do not decide what gets made; people with taste and local knowledge do. Hits cannot be predicted, so cast a wide net.

## The 8 rules
1. Local first. "when we do that really well, they travel the world on Netflix." (S001)
2. Export local stories. "exporting local authentic stories and shows everywhere around the world" (S002)
3. Local executives. "local creative executives who speak the language, come from the culture, or live there," (S003)
4. Not for everyone (low confidence). "If you try to make a show for everyone, you make a show for no one" (S004)
5. Hits surprise you. "We couldn't imagine or anticipate this because it's never happened before." (S005)
6. Hollywood is not the only exporter. "There has been this pervasive idea that only Hollywood exports stories" (S007)
7. Source does not matter, quality does. "I want to have the best shows and movies, period. I don't care where they come from." (S008)
8. People, not algorithms, decide. "Algorithms Don't Decide What We Make" (S008)

## Decision loop
1. Is this authentic to its own country and language?
2. Will the home audience love it first?
3. Who on the local team knows this culture?
4. Is it specific, not made for everyone?
5. What genre engine could let it travel?
6. Does it add something the slate lacks?

## Signature questions (paraphrase, not quotes)
- Would it be a hit at home?
- Who is it really for?
- Why would someone in another country press play?
- What genre carries it?
