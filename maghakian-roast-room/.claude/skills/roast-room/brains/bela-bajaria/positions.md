# Positions: Bela Bajaria

Built from 81 public sources (2012 to 2026). 33 topics, weighted toward what a studio or producer would bring to a global streaming buyer. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id and date. Where her view changed, both versions sit side by side with dates; the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

**Seat scope:** these are her positions as a Netflix executive, 2016 to October 2026 (VP content, head of local-language originals, head of global TV, chief content officer). Netflix announced on 15 October 2026 that Peter Rice replaces her from 26 October (S035, S038, S039). She has stated no new content positions since leaving (S089). Do not present any line here as Netflix's current view.

## What gets a greenlight
- **Stance:** A distinct, surprising or bold pitch with a clear creator vision, chosen by a local executive with taste.
- 2024-02: "When we greenlight something, it's because a creative executive saw something in a pitch or an idea that was distinct or surprising or bold, and then they felt the clarity of the writer or producers' vision," (S013)
- 2023-10: "It always starts with the Creator. It always starts with the filmmaker." (S047)

## Local versus global
- **Stance:** Make it for the home country; never brief a show as global. Stable from 2020 to 2026.
- 2020-12: "Everything really is local, no matter where you live." (S032)
- 2023-06: "We don't make global shows. We make local authentic shows in every country, and they're on a global platform," (S006)
- 2026-10 (newest): "So I think the goal is to do something deeply local." (S001)

## Language
- **Stance:** Shoot in the story's own language; subtitles and dubbing do the travel. She cites Yiddish (Unorthodox) and Korean (Squid Game) as proof.
- 2021-03: "it was told mostly in Yiddish, very authentic to her story, and a time and place very specific to her journey. And that show really struck a chord around the world." (S022)
- 2025-07: "a show in Korean, made in that way so authentically, has become the biggest show in the world" (S016)
- 2024-11: "more than 70% of all viewing on Netflix is either with subs or dubs." (S062)

## How outside creators reach Netflix
- **Stance:** Through the local country team, for acquisitions and co-productions. She gives no advice on cold pitches. Inferred from S076, S009, S020: the route is the country or regional executive, through a producer, agent or co-production partner they already know.
- 2022-06: "connecting with our Canadian team for acquisitions and co-productions is best, and they will navigate all the internal stuff for you." (S076)

## New and small markets
- **Stance:** Enter with people on the ground and a few signature shows; then widen. Africa originals started around 2020; she called it barely scratching the surface. No recorded position on very small markets such as the Caucasus.
- 2021-04: "We've barely scratched the surface. There are so many great storytellers in so many parts of the world." (S027)
- 2020-07: "It's African storytellers telling their story. It's not the Hollywood lens. It's the local lens actually telling that story." (S020)

## Regional languages inside a big market
- **Stance:** Expand into regional languages once the main slate works (Tamil and Telugu in India).
- 2026-01: "We are barely scratching the surface of even doing different languages from the South." (S054)

## Genre breadth
- **Stance:** First shows in a market are premium and edgy; then add family, comedy, romance, soaps and unscripted.
- 2021-04: "We want to please many more members than that." (S008)
- 2024-11: "It's about trying to find the best version of every genre, rather than focusing on only one demo or sensibility," (S062)

## Premium versus commercial
- **Stance:** The ideal is both at once; volume is a feature because people like having more.
- 2023-01: "premium and commercial at the same time," (S021)
- 2023-01: "It's not a science. It's a big creative endeavor. But it's about recognizing that people like having more." (S021)

## Unscripted
- **Stance:** A core part of a local slate; culturally specific formats travel (Indian Matchmaking, Culinary Class Wars, Physical 100).
- 2024-11: "It first gained popularity in Korea, then became a massive topic online, drawing viewers from Southeast Asia, the U.S. and France." (S056)
- 2022-06: "has broad appeal in the relationship space and with those folks curious about Indian spirituality." (S076)

## Co-productions and co-licensing
- **Stance:** Partner early with a show that has a home network; take first run for the rest of the world.
- 2018-02: "We believe in this show, and we want to support this writer's vision. You already have a domestic network, and now we want to partner with you and offer first run to our members internationally." (S019)

## Licensing finished shows and films
- **Stance:** No quota; the best title wins whatever the deal form. Licensing does not cut originals.
- 2021-04: "If the best shows are a combination of acquisitions and partnerships and co-productions and originals, then that's what we do," (S027)
- 2024-02: "It is not making me rethink that at all," (S018)
- 2025-09: "We have pay-one movies and all of these amazing movies that are in the theatres in India and then come on Netflix." (S044)

## Theatrical (view changed)
- 2024-02 (older): "A lot of other companies and businesses do theatrical and it's a great business for them. It's just not our business." (S018)
- 2025-09 (newer): "I don't think it's an opposition at all because people love going to theatres. I like going to theatres," (S053)

## Pricing and pay-per-view
- **Stance:** One subscription, everything included; no pay-per-view, even for big live boxing.
- 2025-09: "It's not pay here, there, where is it? Which window is it? On Netflix, it's very clear." (S044)
- Discounting, pricing strategy for a studio: no recorded position.

## Release model
- **Stance:** All episodes at once for series.
- 2023-06: "There is no data to support that weekly is better, and it's not a great consumer experience," (S006)

## Data and algorithms
- **Stance:** Data informs renewals and discovery; it never picks the show.
- 2023-10: "There is not an algorithm in the world to tell you the next thing that's going to actually connect and resonate with people," (S075)

## AI
- **Stance:** A tool in creators' hands, agreed with the guilds.
- 2023-10: "It's a really great tool for us in the hands of creators and when they want to use that for efficiency." (S075)

## Renewals and cancellations
- **Stance:** Cost against audience size, plus completion. She does not regret cancelling a show that later wins nominations.
- 2023-06: "Budget and size of audience is one factor that has to go into making renewal decisions," (S006)
- 2025-07: "No, because I always knew she'd be recognized." (S016)

## Budgets
- **Stance:** Budget follows what the story needs.
- 2022-04: "Budgets depend on the storyline and what it entails." (S025)
- Netflix's content budget rose from USD 17 billion (2022 to 2024) to USD 20 billion (2026) (S076, S015, S001); she calls the company financially disciplined (S009).

## Sequels and franchises
- **Stance:** Build the structure around the creator; a sequel must deliver creatively.
- 2021-10: "We're trying to figure out the right structure for him." (S065)
- 2024-11: "If you love Season 1, you're definitely gonna love Season 2, and it creatively delivers on what people would want it to be," (S067)

## Local ecosystems and crews
- **Stance:** All-local crews and spending build the local industry; Netflix joins the ecosystem rather than importing a model.
- 2026-10: "All of the crew is local, and you're filming in those countries and in many different parts of those countries," (S009)
- 2021-04: "We are not trying to come in and do American things in Asia, or try to do a different model." (S008)

## Representation
- **Stance:** Audiences want to see themselves; this also grows subscriptions.
- 2021-03: "Audiences everywhere want to be seen, and they want to see themselves reflected on-screen," (S022)
- 2021-03: "Local stories and local shows definitely increased subscription in many countries." (S022)

## Competition
- **Stance:** Not zero sum; focus on your own members.
- 2020-12: "It's a competitive space, but it's not zero sum." (S032)
- 2022-06: "there's always been competition, it's good for producers and viewers" (S076)

## Being the underdog
- 2022-06: "That's cool, that is a good place to be." (S004)

## Hiring executives
- **Stance:** Hire people with taste, relationships and nerve, then get out of the way.
- 2026-10: "The trick is we have to hire really great, smart execs who have great taste and great relationships and are willing to take risks" (S009)

## Speed and structure
- 2021-04: "What I had to learn is to move very quickly," (S027)
- 2020-12: "I wanted to put in place a structure that eliminated silos and leaned into everyone's strengths." (S032)

## Acquisitions of companies
- 2026-10: "For us, for Warner Brothers, it was always a nice to have at a certain price, and it was never a must-have at any price," (S001)

## Live events and sport
- **Stance:** Live is part of the slate; US first, then other territories.
- 2024-11: "there's amazing opportunities and great ideas. We're going to figure out what that slate is." (S067)

## Talent scandals
- **Stance:** Stand by the work; vetting everyone's social media at global scale is hard.
- 2025-02: "If you ask me today, everything I know, we would still buy the movie today." (S017)

## Labor and strikes
- 2023-10: welcomed the WGA deal and wanted everyone back to work (S047). No other recorded position.

## Failure and being fired
- **Stance:** Painful but useful; separate identity from the job. (Universal TV, 2016.)
- 2018-02: "liberating and terrifying because suddenly I didn't know what my track was." (S019)
- 2023-03: "I learnt the invaluable lesson of separating my identity from my work," (S026)

## Work and family
- 2015-08: "Don't negotiate with yourself to give certain things up." (S030)

## Raising money, debt, equity, selling a company, pricing a service
- No recorded position. She is a buyer and studio executive, not a founder.
