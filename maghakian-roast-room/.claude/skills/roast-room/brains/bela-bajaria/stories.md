# Stories: Bela Bajaria

Built from 81 public sources (2012 to 2026). The stories and cases she reuses, one or two lines each, with the lesson she draws and source ids. Netflix cases first, then the studio years.

---

1. **Squid Game: a regional hit that became the world's biggest show.** The Korea team called it a tentpole for Korea and Asia; nobody foresaw the global scale. She later called it "made in Korea by Koreans for Koreans" and was disappointed it missed a 2025 Emmy. Lesson: authenticity first; you cannot plan the scale. "We knew it would be a big regional hit," (S024) Sources: S009, S016, S024, S065, S076.

2. **Lupin: a French classic, told locally, beat everything in 2021.** Most popular show Netflix launched that year, not just among local-language titles. Lesson: success comes from local creators with passion. "That type of success comes from discovering and supporting local creators who have a passion for the story they want to tell." (S060) Sources: S023, S060, S061.

3. **Money Heist: from a Spanish network to Netflix's top non-English show.** Acquired after its Antena 3 run; 65 million households in four weeks for part 4. Lesson: a licensed local show can become a global hit on one screen next to Ozark. Sources: S023, S047, S002.

4. **The couch versus the arthouse.** A viewer who would never drive 40 miles to a foreign-film cinema clicks Money Heist after Ozark. Lesson: access and discovery, not taste, were the barrier. "from your couch, you may click play on 'Money Heist,'" (S023) Sources: S023, S047.

5. **Unorthodox: Yiddish, specific, worldwide.** Lesson: language is no barrier when the story is specific and true. Sources: S022, S061.

6. **Indian Matchmaking: a cold call after a documentary.** She watched A Suitable Girl, called director Smriti Mundhra, and proposed a reality series built on her own push-pull with arranged marriage. Lesson: universal drama sits inside a culturally specific premise. Sources: S028, S036, S076.

7. **Opening the country offices (2019).** Given local-language originals, she got out her passport: Tokyo, Seoul, Bangkok, a real Mumbai team, then Africa. Lesson: local storytelling takes local hiring and investment. "There's no road map for this, local programming is unprecedented at this scale," (S002) Sources: S002, S020, S034, S071.

8. **India: from prestige to a broad slate.** Sacred Games and Delhi Crime opened the market; she pushed for family drama, rom-coms and unscripted, then Tamil and Telugu. Lesson: the first shows set a perception you must widen. Sources: S008, S020, S027, S054. (Sarandos later said he might have gone more populist first, S088; his view, not hers.)

9. **Queen Sono and Blood & Water.** South African shows by African storytellers, not a Hollywood lens on Africa. Lesson: who tells the story changes the story. Sources: S020.

10. **Culinary Class Wars.** A Korean cooking competition that started at home, then spread online to Southeast Asia, the US and France. Lesson: unscripted travels by the same rule as drama. Sources: S056.

11. **The Queen's Gambit and Beef: no algorithm would have asked.** A period show about a woman playing chess; a show about loneliness with road rage as the inciting incident. Lesson: data does not originate hits. Sources: S006.

12. **Emilia Perez (2025).** Under a star's resurfaced posts, she said she would still buy the film. Lesson: stand by bold work; vetting at global scale has limits. Sources: S017.

13. **Killing The Mothership (2024).** A finished Halle Berry film shelved after production and story problems, with the talent's agreement. Lesson: do not launch what does not work. Sources: S013, S018.

14. **"Ted, I've never done any of those things."** Sarandos hired her in 2016 for unscripted and licensing, which she had never run: "I hire smart people, and you'll figure it out." Lesson: hire for judgment, then trust. Sources: S020, S076.

15. **Fired from Universal TV (2016).** After five years building the studio, she was pushed out amid friction with NBC; Kevin Reilly told her firing was a rite of passage. Lesson: separate identity from the job; use the gap. Sources: S019, S026, S082.

16. **Mindy on Fox.** Universal's Mindy Kaling comedy went to Fox, not sister network NBC. Lesson: put the show where it fits; it tells creators you back them. Sources: S029, S031.

17. **The car washes and the creative directory.** Her immigrant family built car washes from scratch; she wrote to everyone in the Hollywood Creative Directory and CBS answered (1996). Lesson: act like an owner and learn every part of the business. Sources: S015, S020, S030, S031.

18. **The brown girl as the hero.** Reading scripts at CBS, she pictured a brown girl as the lead, then saw all-white casts; as a child she lost her British accent watching American TV. Lesson: point of view is an asset; audiences want to see themselves. Sources: S020, S076, S066, S015.
