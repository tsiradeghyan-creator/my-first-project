# Frameworks: Bela Bajaria

Built from 81 public sources (2012 to 2026): keynotes (APOS 2021, Banff 2022, UCLA 2023, Fortune MPW 2023, MIPCOM 2026), Netflix press days and showcases, the TCA tour, and long trade interviews (Variety, THR, Deadline, Fortune, THR India). The seat covers her Netflix years, 2016 to October 2026; CBS and Universal TV material (2012 to 2016) is background. She names few frameworks; most names below are short descriptive ones marked *(unnamed)*. "Sources" counts distinct sources where the idea appears. The "How to score" lines are scoring guidance for the council, built from her stated habits, not her words.

---

## 1. Local shows on a global platform *(her phrase)*
- **What it is:** Netflix does not make "global shows". Each country team makes a local, authentic show for its own country, and the platform puts it in front of the world. Travel is an outcome, never the brief.
- **Parts:** made in the country, by people from it, in its language; judged first by the home audience; then launched everywhere with subtitles and dubbing.
- **When to use:** Any project pitched as "international", "global" or "for everyone".
- **How to score:** 5 if the project is plainly built for its home audience and would still be made if it never left the country; 3 if it is local but bent toward foreign viewers; 1 if it is designed as a global product with no home audience.
- **Quote:** "We don't make global shows. We make local authentic shows in every country, and they're on a global platform," (S006)
- **Also:** "we don't make global shows, we make local specific, authentic shows and films, and then we launch it to a global audience." (S047)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S006, S009, S027, S047, S061, S062, S064, S032)

## 2. Massive local impact, home country first *(her phrase)*
- **What it is:** The success test for every local title is the reaction at home. If people there love it, others will follow; anything abroad is a bonus.
- **Parts:** the home audience loves it, it is their favorite show, they talk about it; then discovery abroad, which she calls "gravy".
- **When to use:** Setting goals and metrics for a local-language project.
- **How to score:** 5 if there is a clear path to being a top title at home; 3 if the home case is modest and the pitch leans on export; 1 if the home market is ignored.
- **Quote:** "For all of this local content, to be honest, the focus is always massive local impact, that's the most important thing" (S008)
- **Also:** "They love it. It is made for them. It's their favorite show or movie on Netflix. And then amazing if other people connect with it, find it, share it, that's all gravy." (S001)
- **Also:** "If people love it in that country, other people will love it too," (S027)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S008, S027, S032, S062, S064, S009, S023)

## 3. The more specific, the more universal *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Her core creative bet. Cultural and personal specificity is what makes a story feel true everywhere. Hits share one trait: they are authentically what they set out to be.
- **Parts:** a specific place, time, language and point of view; no sanding off for outsiders.
- **When to use:** When someone wants to soften the local texture, change the language, or add a foreign lead to "help it travel".
- **How to score:** 5 if the story could only come from this place and these people; 3 if it is generic with local decoration; 1 if the specifics were removed to make it travel.
- **Quote:** "The more specific something is, the more universal it feels and becomes," (S009)
- **Also:** "the more specific and more authentic something is, actually, the more universal it can feel." (S022)
- **Also:** "what we have proven, over and over, is that authenticity travels." (S073)
- **Sources:** 9 (S008, S009, S022, S023, S028, S047, S073, S076, S059)

## 4. Everyone is no one *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A show aimed at all audiences ends up loved by none. Know exactly who it is for.
- **Parts:** name the audience; refuse the "international" or "global" framing; accept that a narrow, deep audience is the start, not the ceiling.
- **When to use:** Any pitch whose audience is "everyone", "all ages", or "the world".
- **How to score:** High if the core audience is named and specific; low if the pitch claims universal appeal up front.
- **Quote:** "I always feel like if you try to make a show for everybody, you make a show for nobody," (S022)
- **Also:** "When you try to make something that appeals to everyone, you just end up making something that appeals to no one." (S064)
- **Sources:** 4 (S022, S028, S047, S064)

## 5. Empowered local decision makers *(her phrase)*
- **What it is:** Each country office has executives from that creative community with their own budget and greenlight authority. They decide in their time zone and language, so decisions are faster and culturally informed. Her job is to hire them and let them work.
- **Parts:** local creative executives who speak the language and grew up in the business; greenlight power on the ground; taste, relationships and willingness to take risks.
- **When to use:** Judging who will champion a project inside a streamer, and how a project from a small market reaches a decision maker.
- **How to score:** 5 if a named local or regional executive would own the project; 3 if it depends on a distant head office; 1 if no one with local taste would ever see it.
- **Quote:** "make the decisions in their own time zone in their own country and in their own language." (S008)
- **Also:** "They have greenlight authority in those countries because they know it," (S009)
- **Also:** "My superpower is I hire really great people and I let them do their job," (S001)
- **Sources:** 10 (S001, S002, S006, S008, S009, S020, S036, S047, S076, S022)

## 6. Creator first: back the vision *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Every title starts with a creator who has something to say. The executive's job is to recognize a distinct voice and back it, including the structure around it.
- **Parts:** a pitch that is distinct, surprising or bold; clarity of the writer's or producer's vision; a creator passionate about this story; then support and trust.
- **When to use:** Judging any pitch or package; deciding who should lead a project.
- **How to score:** 5 if a specific creator with a clear, personal vision drives it; 3 if the creator is a hired hand on a concept; 1 if there is no author.
- **Quote:** "It always starts with the Creator. It always starts with the filmmaker. And it starts with being very specific and authentic to that voice and whatever language that is." (S047)
- **Also:** "When we greenlight something, it's because a creative executive saw something in a pitch or an idea that was distinct or surprising or bold, and then they felt the clarity of the writer or producers' vision," (S013)
- **Also:** "there's a filmmaker or creator who has a vision and something to say and you back that vision." (S002)
- **Sources:** 9 (S002, S013, S023, S047, S060, S065, S066, S075, S029)

## 7. Gut over algorithm *(she calls the talk "myth busters")*
- **What it is:** Data informs access, discovery and renewals; it does not choose what gets made. Greenlights are creative judgment, and hits cannot be engineered.
- **Parts:** taste and gut at the greenlight; data at renewal (cost versus audience, completion); humility about prediction.
- **When to use:** When a plan leans on "the data says this will work" or tries to reverse-engineer a hit.
- **How to score:** Good sign if the case rests on a voice and a story; warning sign if it rests on a formula copied from a past hit.
- **Quote:** "there is not an algorithm in the world to tell you the next thing that's going to actually connect and resonate with people." (S047)
- **Also:** "There's not an algorithm that would probably say, you know what's a great idea? A period show about a woman playing chess," (S006)
- **Also:** "There's no math to it. It's all creative gut judgment. And a little fairy dust and a little luck along the way." (S009)
- **Sources:** 6 (S006, S009, S013, S023, S047, S075)

## 8. Best show wins, from any source *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** No fixed ratio of originals to licensed titles. Originals, acquisitions, co-productions and pay-one theatrical films all compete; the best title gets the slot.
- **Parts:** original commission; co-production; licensing a finished or already-aired show; pay-one window after theatrical release.
- **When to use:** Choosing a route to a streamer for a project that already has a home broadcaster or a finished cut.
- **How to score:** High if the project is strong enough to win on quality whatever the deal form; the deal form itself is neutral.
- **Quote:** "The strategy is always going to be, we want to have the best shows. If the best shows are original shows, that's great. If the best shows are a combination of acquisitions and partnerships and co-productions and originals, then that's what we do," (S027)
- **Also:** "I want to have the best shows and movies, period. I don't care where they come from," (S006)
- **Also:** "I want the best movies on Netflix, period. And that is going to be a mix of original movies and pay-one movies." (S044)
- **Sources:** 6 (S006, S018, S019, S027, S044, S076)

## 9. Best version of every genre, many tastes and moods *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A global service serves many tastes and moods, even inside one family. The slate needs the best version of each genre, not one house sensibility. Program by taste, not demographics.
- **Parts:** genre breadth (drama, comedy, unscripted, anime, docs, live); excellence inside each genre; no "coastal bubble" taste.
- **When to use:** Judging where a project fits and whether its genre is underserved.
- **How to score:** 5 if it could be the best show in its genre for its audience; 3 if it is a solid entry in a crowded genre; 1 if it is made for the programmer's own taste only.
- **Quote:** "It's about trying to find the best version of every genre, rather than focusing on only one demo or sensibility," (S062)
- **Also:** "The biggest mistakes I see creative executives make are to program for one sensibility or assume that their personal tastes are what everyone else loves." (S013)
- **Also:** "It's not targeted at demos, we look at taste." (S025)
- **Sources:** 7 (S009, S013, S016, S025, S047, S062, S020)

## 10. Storytelling plus discovery *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Local shows travel because great storytelling meets easy access: subtitles, dubbing, recommendations and one screen where a viewer can "scroll over" to another country.
- **Parts:** quality subtitles and dubbing; recommendations; low-friction discovery; one hit from a country opens that country's catalogue.
- **When to use:** Estimating how a local title might be found abroad, and what a creator should do to be ready (dub-friendly, subtitle-friendly).
- **How to score:** Guidance only: travel potential rises when the story is clear without cultural footnotes and the genre already has a following (crime, thriller, romance, anime-style worlds).
- **Quote:** "great storytelling coupled with, you know, really amazing quality subtitles and dubbing and with easy access and discovery" (S047)
- **Also:** "more than 70% of all viewing on Netflix is either with subs or dubs." (S062)
- **Also:** "If you're sitting in Mumbai, Madrid, or Mexico City, you can click on Netflix and find your local show or you can scroll over." (S054)
- **Sources:** 8 (S002, S023, S047, S049, S054, S060, S062, S064)

## 11. Everything is local *(her line on the word "international")*
- **What it is:** She banned the word "international" on her team. There is no US core plus a foreign remainder; every member watches local content wherever they live.
- **Parts:** no "US versus international" split; a truly global launch day; first run for the rest of the world matters.
- **When to use:** When a pitch treats its own country as a stepping stone to the US.
- **How to score:** Good sign if the project treats its home market as the main market.
- **Quote:** "We don't talk about the U.S. and label everything else as 'international,'" (S032)
- **Also:** "I wanted to erase this idea that there is US content and "international" content. All content is local for our members" (S049)
- **Sources:** 3 (S019, S032, S049)

## 12. Did people love it and stay? *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Her success and renewal test: did people press play, finish, love it and tell friends; weighed against what it costs.
- **Parts:** completion; word of mouth; budget versus audience size.
- **When to use:** Renewal, second seasons, sequels, scaling up.
- **How to score:** Guidance: strong if a first season was finished and talked about at a cost that fits its audience; weak if viewers started but did not finish.
- **Quote:** "I want our shows to resonate with people. That's what I'm always looking for. Did people love it? Did they stay and watch?" (S015)
- **Also:** "Budget and size of audience is one factor that has to go into making renewal decisions," (S006)
- **Also:** "Engagement is really important. Did people press play, but they didn't finish the series?" (S006)
- **Sources:** 3 (S006, S015, S009)

## 13. Be there: country offices, not regional hubs *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Local storytelling needs people on the ground in each country, not a regional office covering ten. Her 2019 build-out opened Tokyo, Seoul, Bangkok and a real Mumbai team.
- **Parts:** office in the country; local hire with great contacts; join the local ecosystem; do not import an American model.
- **When to use:** Judging whether a streamer is actually buying in a given market, and who would take the call.
- **How to score:** Guidance: a market with a local Netflix team and slate is a live buyer; a market without one is reached only through a regional team, a co-production or a finished title.
- **Quote:** "even if we have people in Singapore for Asia, we really need a Tokyo office, a Seoul office, a Bangkok office." (S002)
- **Also:** "being 'there' is the best for us, (it's how) we make sure we have great and authentic local stories, and you can do that when you're in the country," (S076)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S008, S020, S076, S034)

## 14. Widen a country's slate beyond prestige *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The first shows in a new market tend to be premium, edgy drama. She then pushes for breadth: family drama, comedy, romance, soaps, unscripted, so the service pleases many more members.
- **Parts:** prestige opener; then broad genres; then regional languages within the country.
- **When to use:** Judging what a market's buyer needs next, and whether a project is the hundredth dark crime drama or the missing family show.
- **How to score:** Higher for a well-made project in a genre the market's slate lacks.
- **Quote:** "I think sometimes when we're in a country, we make the first couple shows people externally and even sometimes internally perceive us as just (producing) premium or edgy dramas," (S008)
- **Also:** "We want to have a show you could co-view with your family," (S027)
- **Sources:** 4 (S008, S020, S027, S054)

## 15. Co-licensing: first run for the rest of the world *(her first Netflix model, 2016 to 2019)*
- **What it is:** When a show already has a home network, Netflix partners early and takes first run everywhere else. Coming in at script or pilot stage can raise the budget and get a straight-to-series order.
- **Parts:** domestic broadcaster keeps home rights; Netflix takes the rest of the world; early entry lifts production value.
- **When to use:** A project with a local broadcaster attached that needs money to reach a higher level.
- **How to score:** Good fit if a local broadcaster is committed and the show has an authored vision; weak if nobody at home has committed.
- **Quote:** "We believe in this show, and we want to support this writer's vision. You already have a domestic network, and now we want to partner with you and offer first run to our members internationally." (S019)
- **Also:** "it's afforded certain shows to go straight to series and increased production value because we come in early to partner." (S019)
- **Sources:** 2 (S019, S033)

## 16. Lived-in voice *(unnamed, from the studio years)*
- **What it is:** At CBS and Universal she looked for writers with unique voices who had lived the story. Genre was secondary.
- **When to use:** Testing whether the writer owns the world of the story.
- **How to score:** 5 if the writer clearly lived or deeply knows this world; 1 if it is researched from outside.
- **Quote:** "When a writer pitches us, it has to feel personal, that the writer has somehow lived those stories." (S029)
- **Also:** "there's something special about that voice, the authenticity, something that feels bold or exciting" (S015)
- **Sources:** 3 (S015, S029, S020)

## 17. The right home for the show *(unnamed, from the studio years)*
- **What it is:** As a studio head she sold to every outlet, not only the sister network, and placed each show where it fit best. Relationships with every buyer mattered more than volume.
- **When to use:** A seller deciding between buyers, or holding out for the right platform.
- **How to score:** Good if the target buyer's slate has a gap this show fills; poor if the pick is driven by convenience.
- **Quote:** "Getting Mindy on Fox sent exactly the right message: We will support your show and make sure it ends up at the right place." (S029)
- **Also:** "It's not volume necessarily, it's not like you just need a certain amount and number of shows." (S068)
- **Sources:** 3 (S029, S031, S068)

## 18. Act like an owner: the studio car wash *(her phrase "my studio car wash")*
- **What it is:** From the family car-wash business: know every part of the operation, build from scratch, act like an owner. She hired at Universal for entrepreneurial spirit plus creative skill.
- **When to use:** Judging a small studio or producer as a partner.
- **How to score:** Guidance: strong if the team owns budget, schedule and delivery as well as the creative.
- **Quote:** "That family business was very instrumental in the way that I choose to manage," (S015)
- **Also:** "For me that entrepreneurial spirit, that sort of understanding of acting like an owner, or having that idea of building something is important." (S068)
- **Sources:** 3 (S015, S031, S068)

## 19. Hit-or-miss business, consistent investment *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Hits cannot be predicted, so you keep investing steadily across many bets and do not pull back when others do. Squid Game was expected as a regional hit; the global scale surprised everyone.
- **When to use:** When someone wants a guarantee, or wants to stop after one miss.
- **How to score:** Guidance: favor a slate or plan that survives a miss over one that needs a single hit.
- **Quote:** "We're in a hit-or-miss business," (S009)
- **Also:** "We couldn't imagine or anticipate this because it's never happened before. It's so hard to imagine something to this kind of scale," (S024)
- **Also:** "The focus was always to continue to invest and not to pull back." (S054)
- **Sources:** 5 (S009, S023, S024, S054, S004)
